## 1. PPE Regulation (EU) 2016/425: are sunglasses and non-prescription tinted glasses PPE (Category I), and what is required?

### Takeaway
Non-corrective sunglasses are Category I PPE: CE marking, EU declaration of conformity, technical documentation and user information are mandatory, but the manufacturer self-certifies (no notified body). A Belgian seller who imports is an "importer" with verification, labelling and 10-year record duties; one who sells under his own brand becomes the "manufacturer" and carries all of it himself.

### Cited Findings

**Classification**
- [S] Annex I, Category I covers "exclusively" minimal risks, including "damage to the eyes due to exposure to sunlight (other than during observation of the sun)". Category II is everything not in Category I or III — [Reg. 2016/425 Annex I, legislation.gov.uk copy](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/425/annex/I/adopted); official text: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [S] SGS lists "sunglasses for personal use" as Category I; Compliance Gate lists "sunglasses (to protect against natural UV radiation)" as a Category I example — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2023/03/cc-q1-2023-hl-ppe); [Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/personal-protective-equipment-regulation)
- [S] The Belgian FPS Economy (FOD Economie) describes the same system: for Category I the manufacturer applies internal production control only — [FOD Economie, PPE page](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/safety-products-and-services/specific-regulations/protective-equipment/safety-personal-protective)
- [T] Art. 3(1): PPE is equipment "designed and manufactured to be worn or held by a person for protection against one or more risks to that person's health or safety", plus interchangeable components essential for its protective function — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Art. 2(2)(c): PPE "designed for private use to protect against atmospheric conditions that are not of an extreme nature" is excluded from the Regulation. Art. 2(2)(e): head, face or eye protection covered by UNECE Regulation No 22 (motorcycle/moped helmets and their visors) is also excluded — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)

**Conformity route and documents (Category I)**
- [T] Art. 19(a): Category I uses internal production control (Module A, Annex IV). No notified body — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 19, Annex IV](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj); confirmed in substance [S] by [Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/personal-protective-equipment-regulation)
- [T] Art. 8(2): the manufacturer draws up technical documentation (Annex III), carries out the conformity assessment, draws up the EU declaration of conformity (Art. 15, model in Annex IX) and affixes the CE marking (Arts. 16–17: visibly, legibly and indelibly on the PPE; on packaging and documents only where that is not possible) — [Reg. 2016/425](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Annex III technical documentation includes: description and intended use; assessment of the risks the PPE protects against; list of applicable essential health and safety requirements; design drawings; harmonised standards applied; test reports; description of production controls; a copy of the user instructions — [Reg. 2016/425 Annex III](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Art. 8(3): technical documentation and EU declaration kept for 10 years after the PPE is placed on the market. Art. 8(5): type, batch or serial number on the PPE. Art. 8(6): manufacturer's name, registered trade name or trade mark and a single postal address on the PPE (or packaging/accompanying document where not possible) — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Art. 8(7) and Annex II point 1.4: instructions and information "in a language which can be easily understood by consumers and other end-users, as determined by the Member State concerned". Content includes storage/use/cleaning instructions, performance, limits of use, meaning of markings, the risk protected against, the standards applied, and the manufacturer's name and address — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 8(7), Annex II 1.4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Art. 8(8): the EU declaration must accompany the PPE, or the instructions must give the internet address where it can be accessed. Art. 15(2): the declaration is translated into the language(s) required by the Member State of sale — [Reg. 2016/425 Arts. 8(8), 15](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [S] In practice a sunglasses file consists of: PPE declaration of conformity, label files, instructions, and a test report — [Compliance Gate, sunglasses](https://www.compliancegate.com/european-union-sunglasses-eyewear-regulations/)
- [S] Example of what suppliers actually hand over: a supplier test report testing one pair per style in four colourways against BS EN ISO 12312-1:2013+A1:2015, with some clauses excluded — [Midocean sample PPE test report](https://cdn1.midocean.com/document/test-ppe/mo2816-test-ppe.pdf)

**Importer (Art. 10)**
- [S] Importers place only compliant PPE on the market and must first ensure the manufacturer carried out the Art. 19 conformity assessment — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 10, legislation.gov.uk copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/425/article/10)
- [S] FOD Economie's list of importer duties: confirm the conformity assessment and technical documentation exist and the CE marking is present; keep a copy of the EU declaration and make the technical documentation available to authorities for ten years; cooperate on measures to eliminate risks — [FOD Economie](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/safety-products-and-services/specific-regulations/protective-equipment/safety-personal-protective)
- [T] Art. 10(3): importer's own name, trade name or trade mark and postal address on the PPE or, where not possible, on packaging or an accompanying document. Art. 10(4): ensure instructions in the required language. Art. 10(6): sample testing "when deemed appropriate", investigate and keep a register of complaints, non-conforming PPE and recalls. Art. 10(7): corrective action, withdrawal or recall and notification of authorities where there is a risk. Art. 10(8): 10-year retention — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [S] The importer is responsible for obtaining the declaration of conformity and validating that it is correct and supported by verifiable test reports — [Suffolk Trading Standards guidance on importing PPE](https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/asset-library/eng-guidance-for-importing-ppe-general.docx) (UK guidance on the identical retained text)

**Distributor (Art. 11)**
- [T] Distributors act with due care and verify before selling: CE marking present; required documents and instructions in a language easily understood in the Member State of sale; manufacturer and importer identification present. They must not sell PPE they have reason to believe is non-compliant — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)

**When importer or distributor becomes "manufacturer" (Art. 12)**
- [S] An importer or distributor is treated as a manufacturer, with the Art. 8 obligations, when it places PPE on the market "under his name or trademark" or modifies PPE already on the market in a way that may affect compliance — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 12, legislation.gov.uk copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/425/article/12)

**Traceability and responsible operator**
- [T] Art. 13: economic operators must be able to identify who supplied them and whom they supplied (other operators) for 10 years — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [T] Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Art. 4 applies to PPE: a product may be placed on the market only if an EU-established economic operator (EU manufacturer, importer, authorised representative, or failing those a fulfilment service provider) is responsible for holding the declaration, making technical documentation available and cooperating with authorities. That operator's name and contact details including postal address must be on the product, packaging, parcel or an accompanying document (Art. 4(4)) — [Reg. 2019/1020 Art. 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1020/oj)

### Inferences
- **Private label = manufacturer.** Putting your own brand on Alibaba-sourced glasses triggers Art. 12. You then need the full Annex III file in your own name and you sign the declaration of conformity yourself. A factory test report issued in the factory's name for "its" model is weak evidence unless it demonstrably covers the exact lens/frame you sell.
- **Language.** For Flanders and the Netherlands, Dutch instructions and warnings are the minimum. Selling to all of Belgium realistically means Dutch and French (German for the German-speaking area). The exact Belgian language rule was not verified in this session (see Gaps).
- **A product that claims to protect against headlight glare may fall outside "Category I".** Category I is an exhaustive list and names *sunlight*. Glare from car headlights is artificial light. If the product is presented as protecting against a safety risk other than sunlight, the default is Category II (notified-body type examination). If it claims no protection at all, it is not PPE and falls under the GPSR instead. The pragmatic route used by sellers seen in rulings (see Q7) is to treat the product as sunglasses under EN ISO 12312-1 with a night-legal transmittance. This classification point is unresolved and should be put to FOD Economie or a notified body before investing.
- **"Wind and rain only" for private use is excluded PPE** (Art. 2(2)(c)). Clear glasses sold purely as comfort against wind/rain would then be an ordinary consumer product under the GPSR. Insects, grit and impact are not "atmospheric conditions" (see Q3).

### Gaps
- The Commission's PPE Regulation Guidelines (categorisation appendix) could not be searched or opened. Researcher's recollection, unverified: the guidelines list non-corrective sunglasses for private and professional use as Category I and "all other eye protection and filters" as Category II. Check at [Commission PPE page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/mechanical-engineering/personal-protective-equipment-ppe_en).
- The Belgian implementing act for PPE and the precise Belgian language rule (researcher's recollection: Code of Economic Law art. VI.8 requires labelling and instructions in a language understandable to the average consumer "given the language area" of sale) were not verified.
- The Dutch implementing decree (recollection: Warenwetbesluit persoonlijke beschermingsmiddelen 2018, Dutch-language instructions) was not verified.
- One search result listed EN ISO 12312 under "Category III"; it conflicts with all other sources and looks like a labelling error on a low-quality site, but it was not resolved.

## 2. EN ISO 12312-1: filter categories, driving/road-use rules, night use, yellow and polarised lenses

### Takeaway
The standard's road-use rule is the core problem for the product idea: a filter with luminous transmittance below 75% is "not suitable for road use/driving in twilight or at night", and the user information must say so. A lens sold for night cycling must therefore transmit at least 75% (upper Category 1 or Category 0), which rules out normal polarised lenses and most visibly yellow/tinted lenses.

### Cited Findings

**Editions and harmonisation status**
- [S] Current edition is ISO 12312-1:2022 (second edition, 2022-06). EN ISO 12312-1:2022 supersedes EN ISO 12312-1:2013 and 2013/A1:2015 — [ANSI preview of ISO 12312-1:2022](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO+12312-1-2022.pdf); [iTeh EN ISO 12312-1:2022](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/cen/055a84f1-0a40-4b5c-ac83-f87b9f3e4d37/en-iso-12312-1-2022)
- [S] A European amendment A11 exists (cited as EN ISO 12312-1:2022/A11:2024; some national bodies label it A11:2025) — [EVS A11:2024](https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-iso-12312-1-2022-a11-2024); [SIS A11:2025](https://www.sis.se/en/produkter/environment-health-protection-safety/protective-equipment/head-protective-equipment/ss-en-iso-12312-12022a112025/)
- [S] An industry-body note says a Commission implementing decision cites EN ISO 12312-1:2022 and /A11:2024 as giving presumption of conformity under the PPE Regulation. A testing-company update (July 2026) says EN ISO 12312-1:2022+A11:2024 replaces EN ISO 12312-1:2013+A1:2015 with an Official Journal date of 16 June 2026 — [European Outdoor Group](https://www.europeanoutdoorgroup.com/members-knowledge-hub/publication-of-new-standards-in-the-official-journal-european-union); [QIMA regulatory update 07/2026](https://www.qima.com/regulatory-updates/07-26)
- [S] A further ISO amendment (Amd 1) went through draft stages in 2025–2026, and a consolidated "BS EN ISO 12312-1:2022+A1:2026" is on sale — [DIN draft A1](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/draft-standard/din-en-iso-12312-1-a1/392285933); [en-standard.eu listing](https://www.en-standard.eu/bs-en-iso-12312-1-2022-a1-2026-eye-and-face-protection-sunglasses-and-related-eyewear-sunglasses-for-general-use/)
- [T] Harmonised standards are voluntary; they give a presumption of conformity (Art. 14). The legal requirement is Annex II of the Regulation — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)

**Scope**
- [S] The standard covers afocal (plano) sunglasses and clip-ons "for general use, including road use and driving", intended for protection against solar radiation. It does **not** apply to eyewear for protection against radiation from artificial light sources. Occupational eye protection falls under a different standard — [ANSI preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO+12312-1-2022.pdf)

**Filter categories (luminous transmittance τv)**
- [S] Category 0: over 80% to 100%. Category 1: over 43% to 80%. Category 2: over 18% to 43%. Category 3: over 8% to 18%. Category 4: over 3% to 8% — [College of Optometrists, "Tints and driving"](https://www.college-optometrists.org/clinical-guidance/guidance/knowledge,-skills-and-performance/examining-patients-who-drive/tints-and-driving) (cites the 2013 edition)
- [S] Swiss health authority: Category 0 gives very limited glare reduction and is "not suitable as sunglasses"; Category 1 (43–80%) is lightly tinted — [BAG factsheet sunglasses](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/en/dokumente/str/nis/uv/faktenblatt-sonnenbrillen.pdf.download.pdf/Factsheet%20sunglasses.pdf)

**Road use and driving**
- [S] Filters suitable for road use and driving must be Category 0, 1, 2 or 3 and meet additional spectral and signal-light requirements. Category 4 is not suitable for driving — [College of Optometrists](https://www.college-optometrists.org/clinical-guidance/guidance/knowledge,-skills-and-performance/examining-patients-who-drive/tints-and-driving)
- [S] "Sunglass filters with a luminous transmittance of less than 75% are not suitable for road use in twilight or at night" — [College of Optometrists](https://www.college-optometrists.org/clinical-guidance/guidance/knowledge,-skills-and-performance/examining-patients-who-drive/tints-and-driving). The same 75% minimum applies in the ophthalmic-lens counterpart BS EN ISO 8980-3 — [Optician Online](https://www.opticianonline.net/features/new-british-standards)
- [S] The 2022 edition keeps this structure: its table of contents has clause 5.3.2 "Requirements for road use and driving", and test reports refer to "Clause 5.3.2.2 – Driving in twilight or at night" — [ANSI preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO+12312-1-2022.pdf); [Midocean test report](https://cdn1.midocean.com/document/test-ppe/mo2816-test-ppe.pdf)
- [S] Real-world labelling: a 50%-transmittance Category 1 driving lens is sold as "suitable for driving in accordance to DIN EN ISO 12312-1 (not suitable for driving at night)" — [Eschenbach ambelis drive](https://www.eschenbach-vision.com/en-GB/products/ambelis-drive)
- [S] A night-driving product can pass: in a 2025 UK ASA case the seller's certification report showed 79.38% luminous transmittance, which "passed for 'Driving in Twilight or Night'" against the 75% threshold — [ASA ruling Pressplay Ltd](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/pressplay-ltd-a24-1266131-pressplay-ltd.html)

**User information**
- [S] The information note should reference the standard, the type and category of the filter, and cleaning instructions — [CCPC Ireland, sunglasses](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/product-safety/sunglasses)
- [S] Product leaflets under the standard carry statements such as "not suitable for driving and road use" where applicable — [example leaflet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0060/4261/5923/files/NoticeSolaire5x7cm-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Correction to the brief.** The night threshold is **75%**, not 80%. A night-legal lens can be Category 0 (>80%) or the top of Category 1 (75–80%). The Pressplay product at 79.38% was Category 1 and still passed the night clause.
- **A polarised lens cannot be a night lens.** An ideal polariser passes at most about half of unpolarised light, so polarised sunglasses sit in Categories 2–3. Marketing a polarised lens for night cycling contradicts the 75% rule and the mandatory "not suitable in twilight or at night" information. That is both a PPE non-conformity (wrong/missing mandatory information; Annex II 1.4) and a misleading-claim problem (Q7).
- **Yellow lenses.** A pale yellow lens can exceed 75%, but it must also pass the signal-light recognition (Q-value) and spectral requirements for road use. Strong yellow or amber tints commonly fall below 75%.
- **"Road use" plausibly includes cycling.** The standard speaks of "road use and driving", not only of motor vehicles, so a cycling-at-night claim is within the clause. This reading is the researcher's, not confirmed by an authority.
- **Headlight-glare protection is outside the standard's scope** (artificial light is excluded). Passing EN ISO 12312-1 shows the lens is permitted at night; it does not substantiate any claim that it reduces headlight glare.

### Gaps
- The standard is paywalled (ISO 12312-1:2022 about USD 176; BS consolidated 2022+A1:2026 about USD 421; companion test-method standard ISO 12311:2023 about USD 143 — [PECB](https://store.pecb.com/products/standard-iso-12312-1), [en-standard.eu](https://www.en-standard.eu/bs-en-iso-12312-1-2022-a1-2026-eye-and-face-protection-sunglasses-and-related-eyewear-sunglasses-for-general-use/), [Intertek Inform](https://www.intertekinform.com/en-us/standards/iso-12311-2023-601717_saig_iso_iso_3346119/)). Exact clause wording for the 2022 edition and its amendments was not seen. Confidence in the 75% rule: high (three independent secondary sources plus a regulator ruling). Confidence in exact 2022 clause numbers: medium.
- **Signal-light Q-values not verified.** Researcher's recollection of earlier editions: relative visual attenuation quotient at least 0.80 for red, 0.60 for green, 0.40 for blue, with yellow at 0.60 or 0.80 depending on edition; spectral transmittance between 475 nm and 650 nm at least 0.2 × τv. A search for these values returned nothing usable. Must be read from the purchased standard.
- **Polarising-filter requirements not verified.** Recollection: plane of transmission within ±5° of vertical, left/right misalignment at most 6°, and a minimum polarising efficiency ratio (8:1 for Categories 2–4, 4:1 for Category 1). Unconfirmed.
- Exact mandatory warning wording, and whether the driving-unsuitability symbol must be on the product for Category 4, were not verified for the 2022 edition.
- Whether the OJ citation date of 16 June 2026 for A11 is correct, and whether A1:2026 is harmonised, could not be confirmed against the Official Journal. What A11 and A1 change is unknown.

## 3. Clear cycling glasses with impact/wind/insect claims: EN 166 / EN ISO 16321-1 and Category II?

### Takeaway
A claim of protection against impact, flying particles or insects is a mechanical eye-protection claim, which is outside the exhaustive Category I list and therefore Category II: EU type-examination by a notified body. EN 166 lost its presumption of conformity on 11 November 2025; EN ISO 16321-1 is now the reference.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Category I is limited to listed minimal risks (superficial mechanical injury, sunlight, non-extreme atmospheric conditions, etc.). Category II covers all risks not in I or III and requires EU type-examination by a notified body — [Reg. 2016/425 Annex I](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/425/annex/I/adopted); [Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/personal-protective-equipment-regulation)
- [T] Art. 19(b): Category II = EU type-examination (Module B, Annex V) followed by conformity to type based on internal production control (Module C, Annex VI) — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [S] "From November 11, 2025, the EN 166:2001 standard for protective eyewear will no longer provide presumption of conformity for CE and UKCA marking." EN ISO 16321 is the only valid basis for new certifications — [BSI webinar page](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/media/webinars/are-your-protective-eyewear-products-ready-for-the-11-november-2025-transition); [BSI blog](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/a-new-era-in-eye-protection-understanding-en-iso-16321/); [VDP](https://www.vdp.com/EN/Nieuws/order/asc/1/3687)
- [S] Products already certified to EN 166 can continue to be sold until their certificate expires, at most five years from issue — [BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/a-new-era-in-eye-protection-understanding-en-iso-16321/)
- [S] Conflicting date: a Bollé-authored article says the transition end was pushed from 11 November to 11 December 2025 — [Fisher Scientific Lab Reporter](https://www.fishersci.it/it/en/lab-reporter/2025/issue-4/en-iso-16321.html); contradicted by [BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/media/webinars/are-your-protective-eyewear-products-ready-for-the-11-november-2025-transition) and [VDP](https://www.vdp.com/EN/Nieuws/order/asc/1/3687)
- [S] Status in late 2026: Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1971 of 3 September 2026 adds EN ISO 16321-1:2022/A1:2025 to the harmonised PPE standards and defers the withdrawal of the references to EN ISO 16321-1:2022 and EN ISO 16321-3:2022 — [Lovguiden summary of the OJ decision](https://www.lovguiden.dk/en/det-offentlige/eur-lex-eu-tidende/2026-09-04-eu-opdaterer-harmoniserede-standarder-for-beskyttelsesudstyr-paa-arbejde); base list: [Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/941](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/941/oj/eng)
- [S] EN ISO 16321-3:2022/A1:2026 was published 13 August 2026 — [Genorma](https://genorma.com/en/standards/en-iso-16321-3-2022-a1-2026)

### Inferences
- Selling clear glasses with wording such as "impact resistant", "protects against stones/insects" makes them Category II eye protectors. A small seller cannot self-certify that. Either buy from a manufacturer holding a valid EU type-examination certificate (and act as importer/distributor under that manufacturer's name), or drop the protective claims.
- Private-labelling Category II eyewear is harder still: the brand owner is the manufacturer and needs the type-examination certificate in its own name (or a formal arrangement with the certificate holder and notified body).
- Clear glasses with no protective claim at all are not PPE; they are a general consumer product under the GPSR (Q5).

### Gaps
- No source seen that explicitly classifies *cycling* glasses. The Category II conclusion follows from Annex I and from the recalled (unverified) Commission categorisation guidance.
- EN ISO 12312-1 contains optional robustness/impact "strength level" claims for sunglasses (recollection, unverified). Whether using those optional claims keeps a product in Category I was not established.
- The deferred withdrawal date for EN ISO 16321-1:2022 in Decision 2026/1971 was not seen.
- Notified-body fees for EU type-examination of eyewear were not found.

## 4. Helmet visors: is an aftermarket bicycle/speed-pedelec visor PPE; helmet standards; BE and NL helmet rules; effect on certification, warranty, insurance

### Takeaway
A visor for an ECE 22 moped helmet is outside the PPE Regulation but needs UNECE approval, and under R22 anything below 80% transmittance must be marked "DAYTIME USE ONLY". A visor for a bicycle or NTA 8776 helmet that claims eye/face protection is PPE (most likely Category II). Helmet makers warn against non-approved attachments, so a third-party visor puts the helmet's tested configuration and warranty in doubt.

### Cited Findings

**Helmet obligations**
- [S] Belgium: speed-pedelec riders must wear either a moped helmet or a bicycle helmet (EN 1078, CE label) that additionally protects the temples and back of the head. The speed pedelec is "bromfiets klasse P" in the Wegcode — [Vias briefing Speedpedelecs (2025 update)](https://www.vias.be/publications/Briefing%20-%20Speedpedelecs%20-%20update%202025/Briefing_Speedpedelecs.pdf); [veiligverkeer.be](https://www.veiligverkeer.be/weggebruikers/speedpedelec/verplichte-en-veilige-uitrusting); [vlaanderen.be](https://www.vlaanderen.be/speedpedelec); [Mobiliteitsbrief 173](https://mow.vlaanderen.be/storage/30/mobiliteitsbrief-173.pdf)
- [S] NTA 8776 is a Dutch standard and not a legal requirement in Belgium, according to Test-Aankoop citing the Flemish government and police — [Test-Aankoop complaint page](https://www.test-aankoop.be/klagen/publieke-klachten/fout-in-een-artikel/fada0992d0f1124784). Retailers claiming NTA 8776 "will soon be mandatory in Belgium" are not an official source.
- [S] Netherlands: an approved helmet is mandatory on mopeds, light mopeds and speed pedelecs. A speed-pedelec helmet is recognised by the marking NTA 8776:2016; a moped helmet with E-mark (ECE 22.05 or 22.06) is also allowed. The NTA 8776 helmet is not allowed on an ordinary moped — [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/verkeersveiligheid/vraag-en-antwoord/helmplicht-brommer-snorfiets-speedpedelec); [Staatscourant 2022, 26086](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2022-26086.pdf)
- [S] Dutch fine for no helmet: €120 per Rijksoverheid/ANWB; an insurer gives €130 for 2026 — [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/verkeersveiligheid/vraag-en-antwoord/helmplicht-brommer-snorfiets-speedpedelec); [Univé](https://www.unive.nl/bromfietsverzekering/speed-pedelec/helm-verplicht)

**Visors under UNECE R22**
- [T] ECE 22 helmets and their visors are excluded from the PPE Regulation (Art. 2(2)(e)); they are type-approved under UNECE R22 instead — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)
- [S] Visors transmitting at least 80% are approved for day and night. Below 80% the visor must carry "DAYTIME USE ONLY" or the equivalent symbol. The lower limit for tinted visors was 50% under 22.05 and is 35% under 22.06 — [rad.eu](https://blog.rad.eu/en/can-you-ride-with-a-dark-or-tinted-visor/); [Billy's Crash Helmets guide to ECE 22.06](https://billyscrashhelmets.co.uk/a-guide-to-ece-22-06-approval-of-motorcycle-helmets-and-visors/); UK guidance: [gov.uk](https://gov.uk/government/publications/motorcycle-helmets-visors-and-goggles-safety-advice/motorcycle-helmets-visors-and-goggles)
- [S] The UN amendment text requires the visor marking to include "the symbol denoting daytime use only, if appropriate", clearly legible, indelible and wear-resistant — [UN Digital Library, R22 Rev.3 Amend.3](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/426687/files/E_ECE_324_Rev.1_Add.21_Rev.3_Amend.3_E_ECE_TRANS_505_Rev.1_Add.21_Rev.3_Amend.3-EN.pdf)

**Speed-pedelec helmets and visors in the market**
- [S] NTA 8776 sits between a bicycle and a moped helmet: more impact absorption, larger coverage (temples, back of head). Many NTA 8776 helmets are sold with an integrated or removable visor, described as protection against rain, wind, insects and dirt. Sources mention visors as product features, not as tested requirements — [Bern e-bike helmet guide](https://bernhelmets.com/pages/e-bike-helmet-guide); [Decathlon NL](https://www.decathlon.nl/sporten/stadsfietsen/speed-pedelec-helm); [road.cc interview on NTA 8776](https://road.cc/ebiketips/content/advice/features/interview-the-new-met-grancorso-helmet-and-a-new-helmet-standard-1649)

**Modification, warranty**
- [S] Helmet manuals prohibit modification and unapproved attachments: Specialized ("Do not modify or remove any of the original component parts of the helmet as it may reduce or diminish the helmet's protective capabilities"), Bell (similar), Polisport (unapproved attachment "will void all Polisport's warranties"), MET (warranty excludes use not in accordance with the manual) — [Specialized manual](https://fortnine.ca/assets/pdf/specialized/bicycle_helmet_owners_manual_english.pdf); [Bell manual](https://www.bellhelmets.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-bellhelmets-Library/default/dwede5ccf1/manuals/Bell-Helmets-Bike-Helmet-Manual-B5000433-F-INT.pdf); [Polisport](https://www.polisport.com/images_prods_static/87402_pt.pdf); [MET manual](https://www.met-helmets.com/app/uploads/2020/06/OWNERS-MANUAL_MET_Parachute-MCR_1ISTM120003.pdf)
- [S] Art. 12 / recital logic: an operator who modifies PPE in a way that may affect compliance is treated as manufacturer — [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/425/article/12)

### Inferences
- **Three different legal products.**
  1. Visor for an ECE 22 helmet: needs R22 approval (E-mark) as a visor; a tinted one is daytime-only below 80%. An unapproved Chinese visor for an approved moped helmet is not lawful equipment for a rider who relies on the ECE route.
  2. Visor for a bicycle or NTA 8776 helmet with protective claims (wind-borne debris, insects, impact): PPE in its own right or an interchangeable protective component (Art. 3(1)), most likely Category II.
  3. Visor sold with no protective claim, as a rain/wind comfort accessory for private use: arguably excluded PPE (Art. 2(2)(c)) and a GPSR product. Even then the seller must do a GPSR risk analysis that covers field of view, optical distortion, fogging, shattering, and behaviour in a crash.
- **Night-use tint.** By analogy with R22 (80%) and EN ISO 12312-1 (75%), a tinted or yellow visor sold for use in the dark has no standard to lean on unless it is essentially clear.
- **Certification.** A helmet's certificate covers the configuration that was tested. A third-party visor fitted by the consumer is not covered, and the visor seller cannot claim the helmet "remains certified". The seller should not state or imply compatibility with a helmet standard without test evidence.
- **Liability shift.** If a clip-on visor snags, shatters or changes how the helmet performs in a crash, the visor seller (as importer/manufacturer) is the obvious defendant, because the helmet maker will point to its "do not modify" warning.

### Gaps
- The Belgian Wegcode article (recollection: art. 36 of the Royal Decree of 1 December 1975) was not read; the wording comes from Vias and VSV summaries.
- **Whether EN 1078 or NTA 8776 contain visor requirements or tests was not established.** Both are paywalled and no secondary source addressed it.
- No Lazer, ABUS or Cratoni manual was found; the warranty quotes come from other brands.
- **No source found on the rider's insurance position** when a third-party visor is fitted. Not established either way.
- Official UNECE R22.06 clause numbers for the visor transmittance rule were not seen (recollection for 22.05: para. 6.15.3.4; copy at [smf.org](https://smf.org/docs/articles/hic/ECE22rv4e_helmet_standard.pdf)).
- Cost and procedure for R22 visor approval or for Category II certification of a bicycle visor were not found.

## 5. General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988 and Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Art. 4: online-seller duties, and what dropshipping from China means

### Takeaway
Since 13 December 2024 every online offer must show the manufacturer, the EU responsible person (when the manufacturer is outside the EU), product identification with a picture, and the safety warnings in the consumer's language. A product with no EU-established responsible operator may not be sold at all. In dropshipping, the Belgian seller is the only EU operator in the chain and ends up carrying importer-type responsibility for goods he never sees.

### Cited Findings

**Application and scope**
- [S] The GPSR was adopted 10 May 2023, entered into force 12 June 2023 and applies from 13 December 2024 — [ecommercegermany GPSR guide](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/gpsr-general-product-safety-regulation/); official text: [Reg. 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj)
- [T] Art. 2(1): for products under Union harmonisation legislation (such as PPE), Chapter III Section 1 (operator obligations, Arts. 9–18, including the Art. 16 responsible person), Chapters V, VII and IX–XI do not apply. The rest does, including Art. 19 (distance sales), Art. 20 (accident reporting), Chapter IV (online marketplaces), Chapter VI (Safety Gate) and Chapter VIII (recalls and remedies) — [Reg. 2023/988 Art. 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj)
- [S] Confirmation of that reading for another harmonised sector: "Article 19 of the GPSR, related to information to be provided in the case of online / distance sales, does apply to cosmetic products" — [Cosmetics Europe guidance](https://cosmeticseurope.eu/download/cGd4SnlBQ3RlMjNpRDEvNU1jOW5iQT09/readonly); [CosLaw](https://coslaw.eu/distance-sales-article-19-of-the-general-product-safety-regulation-applies-to-cosmetics/)
- [S] For CE-marked categories the Art. 16 GPSR duty is replaced by the equivalent duty in Art. 4 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020; the listing duties still apply — [Vimm (Belgian compliance blog)](https://www.vimm.be/en/blog/gpsr-article-19-product-information-webshop); [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/fulfilment-providers-gpsr-rp-role/)

**Art. 19: what the online listing must show**
- [S]/[T] The offer must "clearly and visibly indicate", before purchase: (a) the manufacturer's name, registered trade name or trade mark, and postal and electronic address; (b) where the manufacturer is not established in the EU, the name, postal and electronic address of the responsible person (Art. 16(1) GPSR or Art. 4(1) Reg. 2019/1020); (c) information identifying the product, including a picture, its type and any other product identifier; (d) any warning or safety information required on the product, packaging or accompanying document, in a language easily understood by consumers as determined by the Member State — [Reg. 2023/988 Art. 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj); [Cosmetics Europe guidance](https://cosmeticseurope.eu/download/cGd4SnlBQ3RlMjNpRDEvNU1jOW5iQT09/readonly); [Vimm](https://www.vimm.be/en/blog/gpsr-article-19-product-information-webshop); Commission material: [GPSR presentation](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/safety/consumers/consumers_safety_gate/obligationsForBusinesses/documents/GPSR-Presentation-website.pdf), [GPSR Q&A](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/safety/consumers/consumers_safety_gate/obligationsForBusinesses/documents/Q&A.pdf)

**Responsible person / economic operator**
- [S] A non-EU manufacturer needs an EU-established responsible person; without one the product may not be placed on the EU market, and the person's details must appear in the listing. A fulfilment service provider takes the role only when no other EU operator exists — [ecommercegermany](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/gpsr-general-product-safety-regulation/); [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/fulfilment-providers-gpsr-rp-role/)
- [T] Reg. 2019/1020 Art. 4(2)–(4): order of responsible operators (EU manufacturer; importer; authorised representative with written mandate; fulfilment service provider); tasks (verify declaration and technical documentation exist, keep the declaration, supply documents to authorities, report risks, cooperate, take corrective action); contact details on product, packaging, parcel or accompanying document. Art. 6: products offered online are deemed made available on the Union market if the offer targets EU end users — [Reg. 2019/1020](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1020/oj)

**Non-harmonised products (e.g. a visor or clear glasses with no protective claim): full GPSR**
- [T] Art. 9(2): the manufacturer carries out an internal risk analysis and draws up technical documentation before placing on the market; Art. 9(3): kept for 10 years. Art. 9(5)–(7): type/batch/serial number; name, postal and electronic address; instructions and safety information in the consumer's language. Art. 11: importer verifies these, adds own name and postal and electronic address, keeps documentation 10 years. Art. 12: distributor checks. Art. 13: whoever places a product on the market under own name or trade mark is the manufacturer. Art. 16: no placing on the market without an EU responsible person — [Reg. 2023/988 Arts. 9–16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj)

**Incidents, Safety Gate, recalls**
- [T] Art. 20: the manufacturer notifies accidents caused by the product via the Safety Business Gateway without undue delay; importers and distributors who learn of an accident inform the manufacturer. Arts. 35–37: recall notices and consumer remedies (choice of at least two among repair, replacement, refund) — [Reg. 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj)

**Dropshipping**
- [S] When a seller sells the supplier's brand, the supplier counts as manufacturer, "yet you still need a Responsible Person in the chain and you still need the listing information" — [EUverify](https://euverify.com/?p=10207)
- [S] "Anyone who imports goods from China or another non-EU country is, as importer, product liable within the EU for those goods" (Dutch insurer guidance); KVK likewise warns importers from China to check product rules first — [Univé](https://www.unive.nl/zakelijk/bedrijfsaansprakelijkheidsverzekering/aansprakelijkheid-import-export); [KVK](https://www.kvk.nl/internationaal/importeren-uit-china-controleer-vooraf-de-regels/)
- [S] Test-Aankoop publishes a consumer warning about dropshipper webshops — [Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/webshops/nieuws/dropshipping)
- [T] Definition: an importer is any natural or legal person established in the Union who places a product from a third country on the Union market — [Reg. 2023/988 Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj); same in [Reg. 2016/425 Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/425/oj)

### Inferences

**Role matrix (researcher's synthesis of the provisions above)**

| | A. Import own stock, supplier's brand | B. Private label, own brand | C. Dropshipping, China direct to consumer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role for PPE (sunglasses) | Importer (Art. 10) | Manufacturer (Art. 12 → Art. 8) | In substance importer: the only EU operator placing the third-country product on the EU market; at minimum distributor |
| Technical file and declaration | Verify they exist; hold declaration copy 10 years; be able to produce the file | Own file (Annex III), own signed declaration, CE affixed under own responsibility | Same duty as A, but no physical control and usually no real documents |
| Name and address on product | Manufacturer's plus importer's | Own (as manufacturer) | Must still be on product, parcel or accompanying document; depends entirely on the Chinese shipper |
| Dutch (and French) instructions and warnings | Must ensure | Must write | Must ensure; in practice absent |
| GPSR Art. 19 listing | Yes | Yes | Yes; "responsible person" is the seller himself unless the factory has an EU authorised representative |
| Sample testing, complaint register, recall | Yes | Yes, plus production control | Yes in law; hard to execute |
| Product liability | "Producer" as importer | "Producer" as own-brander | Importer or, failing that, supplier treated as producer (Q6) |
| Customs/VAT | Seller imports, pays duty and VAT | Same | Consumer is importer of record unless IOSS/duty-paid shipping is arranged |

- **Dropshipping is the weakest model legally.** The seller carries importer-level duties without being able to inspect goods, add labels or leaflets, or run a recall with batch traceability. A typical AliExpress-sourced item (no EU address, no Dutch warnings, questionable CE mark) cannot lawfully be listed, because the Art. 19 data fields cannot be truthfully completed.
- **A Shopify store is not an "online marketplace".** The marketplace obligations (Art. 22) fall on platforms like Amazon or Bol; on a self-hosted Shopify shop, all duties sit with the seller.
- **Model A or B with a small batch is the only realistic compliant path**: receive goods, check markings and documents, add an address label and Dutch/French leaflet, keep the file.

### Gaps
- No official Commission, FOD Economie, NVWA or KVK statement was retrieved that says in terms "a dropshipper is the importer". The conclusion rests on the statutory definitions and secondary sources. The Blue Guide 2022 ([OJ C 247, 29.6.2022](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52022XC0629(04))) and the Commission Notice on market surveillance of products sold online were not opened.
- Belgian penalties for GPSR/PPE breaches (recollection: Code of Economic Law Book XV, graded sanction levels with fines multiplied by surcharges, and turnover-based maxima for some consumer-law breaches) were not verified. No sanction amounts are asserted here.
- Whether an "electronic address" can be a web form or must be an e-mail address is discussed in the Commission Q&A but the answer was not seen.

## 6. Product liability: new Directive (EU) 2024/2853, current Belgian regime, importer exposure, insurance

### Takeaway
Under both the current and the new regime, the EU importer and the own-brand seller are strictly liable as "producer/manufacturer" for defective products from China. The new Directive (products placed on the market after 9 December 2026) removes the €500 threshold, eases the claimant's burden of proof and closes the gaps so that an EU-based seller of non-EU goods is always reachable. Marketing glasses as improving night vision raises the safety expectation against which "defect" is judged.

### Cited Findings

**New Directive (EU) 2024/2853**
- [S] Entered into force 8 December 2024; Member States must transpose by 9 December 2026; it applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 December 2026 — [Commission, liability for defective products](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/goods/free-movement-sectors/liability-defective-products_en); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2024/12/New-EU-Product-Liability-Directive-Comes-Into-Force)
- [S] Liable parties now include, besides manufacturers and importers, authorised representatives, fulfilment service providers, distributors and (conditionally) online platforms. Liability is allocated in a cascade and is joint and several where several operators are liable. Platforms acting purely as intermediaries are not liable — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/new-rules-new-players-whos-next-in-the-eus-productliability-shakeup-); [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/eu-revised-product-liability-directive-2024-navigating-the-new-liability-framework-for-noneu-manufac); [Commission](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/goods/free-movement-sectors/liability-defective-products_en)
- [T] Art. 4(10): "manufacturer" includes anyone who has a product designed or manufactured or who presents themselves as manufacturer by putting their name, trade mark or other distinguishing feature on it. Art. 8(1)(c): where the manufacturer is outside the EU, the importer, the authorised representative and, failing both, the fulfilment service provider are liable. Art. 8(3): if no such EU operator can be identified, each distributor is liable unless it identifies an EU operator or its own supplier within one month of the injured person's request — [Dir. 2024/2853 Arts. 4, 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)
- [T] Art. 7: defectiveness is assessed against the safety a person is entitled to expect or that is required by law, taking into account among other things the presentation of the product, labelling and instructions, reasonably foreseeable use, and product safety requirements. Art. 10(2): defectiveness is presumed where the claimant shows the product does not comply with mandatory product safety requirements intended to protect against the risk that materialised, or where the defendant fails to disclose evidence (Art. 9) — [Dir. 2024/2853 Arts. 7, 9, 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)
- [T] Art. 6: compensable damage covers death, personal injury (including medically recognised psychological harm), damage to private property and loss of non-professional data; no lower threshold and no cap. Art. 15: liability cannot be excluded or limited by contract. Art. 16: 3-year limitation from knowledge. Art. 17: 10-year long-stop from placing on the market (25 years for latent personal injury) — [Dir. 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)

**Belgium: current regime and transposition**
- [S] The Act of 25 February 1991 on product liability has been incorporated into Book 6 (extra-contractual liability) of the Belgian Civil Code, Chapter 7, arts. 6.41–6.55. Liability is strict: the criterion is the defect, not fault. General fault liability remains available alongside it — [ie-net seminar on Book 6 (May 2024)](https://ie-net.be/sites/default/files/BUITENCONTRACTUELE_AANSPRAKELIJKHEID_F.LEGRAND_MEI_2024_DEF.pdf); [Wolters Kluwer schema Boek 6](https://assets.contenthub.wolterskluwer.com/api/public/content/2133756-algemeen-schema-boek-6-99d6979e49); [Linklaters three-language translation of Book 6](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/linklaterslbf57-linklatersx7ccc-linklatersxf9f5-394d/media/files/document-store/pdfns/2024/june/book-6_three-languages_translation_final.pdf)
- [T] Current EU basis (Directive 85/374/EEC): Art. 3(1) own-brander is producer; Art. 3(2) the importer into the EU is deemed producer; Art. 3(3) each supplier is treated as producer if the producer or EU importer cannot be identified within a reasonable time; Art. 9 has a €500 lower threshold for property damage — [Dir. 85/374/EEC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1985/374/oj)
- [S] Belgian transposition of the new Directive: the federal government approved a preliminary draft bill on 18 July 2026, amending Book 6 art. 6.41 ff. Linklaters saw no indication Belgium would miss 9 December 2026. The government acknowledged that the Directive's disclosure rule goes beyond existing document production under art. 877 Judicial Code — [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/blogs/productliabilitylinks/2026/belgium-moves-to-transpose-new-product-liability-directive); [Lexgo](https://www.lexgo.be/en/news-and-articles/15004-belgium-moves-to-transpose-new-product-liability-directive)
- [S] Conflicting trackers: Reed Smith lists Belgium among states with draft legislation; Faegre Drinker's October 2026 update reportedly lists Belgium among states with no meaningful public steps — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/eu-product-liability-directive-where-things-stand-with-member-state-transposition/); contradicted by [Faegre Drinker, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/transposing-the-eu-new-product-liability-directive-a-member-state-progress-report-october-2026-update)

**Insurance**
- [S] Product liability is normally a standard component of a general business liability policy (Dutch AVB; Belgian equivalent "BA uitbating / BA na levering"). Premium depends on type of business, turnover and deductible. A separate product liability policy is sometimes needed — [Univé](https://www.unive.nl/zakelijk/bedrijfsaansprakelijkheidsverzekering/aansprakelijkheid-import-export); [Insify](https://support.insify.nl/hc/nl/articles/17816495784082-Wat-is-product-aansprakelijkheid); [KVK, insurances for international business](https://www.kvk.nl/internationaal/check-deze-9-verzekeringen-als-je-internationaal-zakendoet/)
- [S] Importing from outside the EU increases exposure because the importer is treated as producer — [Univé](https://www.unive.nl/zakelijk/bedrijfsaansprakelijkheidsverzekering/aansprakelijkheid-import-export)

### Inferences
- **Crash scenario.** A customer crashes at night wearing glasses sold as "see better at night". The claimant argues defect on three grounds: (1) presentation created an expectation of improved night vision that the product does not deliver or that it reduces (any tint removes light); (2) non-compliance with mandatory safety information (a sub-75% lens sold for night without the required warning), which under the new Directive triggers a *presumption* of defect; (3) inadequate warnings. Causation remains the claimant's hurdle, and contributory fault of the rider can reduce damages (Art. 13 new Directive), but the seller of record is the defendant and cannot contract out.
- **All three business models lead to the Belgian seller.** Importer (A) and own-brander (B) are producers outright. The dropshipper (C) is importer, or a supplier/distributor who cannot name an EU producer or importer, which has the same result.
- **Timing.** Stock placed on the market before 9 December 2026 stays under the old rules; anything sold after falls under the new regime once transposed. A launch in 2027 is fully under the new Directive.
- **Legal form matters.** As a sole proprietor (eenmanszaak) the seller is liable with private assets; personal-injury claims can dwarf the turnover of a small webshop. This makes insurance, or a limited-liability company, a threshold decision rather than an afterthought.
- **Insurability of the claim itself.** Insurers typically ask about non-EU sourcing and product type; safety-related eyewear from unvetted Chinese suppliers, or dropshipping, may be surcharged or declined. This is the researcher's expectation, not a sourced fact.

### Gaps
- **No premium figure for a small Belgian webshop was found.** The single search on this returned only qualitative sources. Quotes from a Belgian broker are needed.
- The article numbers within Book 6 for importer and supplier liability, and whether the €500 threshold is carried in Book 6, were not verified.
- Whether the Belgian transposition act had been adopted and published by 17 November 2026 could not be confirmed. One search summary said Belgium "did not apply the optional development-risk defence but reserved the option"; the meaning of that sentence is ambiguous and was not checked against the Linklaters text.
- Dutch transposition status (relevant when a Dutch consumer sues) was not researched.
- No case law on accidents involving night-driving glasses was found or searched for.

## 7. Advertising and consumer law: misleading claims, substantiation, rulings on night-driving glasses, medical-device risk

### Takeaway
Claims such as "blocks headlight glare", "see better at night" and "safer cycling" are factual performance and safety claims that the trader must be able to prove. The UK regulator has banned exactly these claims twice in 2025 even where the lens was legally night-compliant, and the US FTC acted against "NightSafe" glasses in the 1990s. No Belgian (JEP) or Dutch (RCC) decision on such products was found.

### Cited Findings

**Legal framework**
- [T] Directive 2005/29/EC Art. 6: a practice is misleading if it contains false information or deceives the average consumer about the main characteristics of the product, including fitness for purpose, benefits, risks, and results to be expected from its use. Art. 7: misleading omissions. Art. 12: authorities and courts may require the trader to furnish evidence of the accuracy of factual claims and treat them as inaccurate if the evidence is not produced. Annex I: blacklisted practices include falsely claiming a product is able to cure illnesses or dysfunction (point 17), and claiming approval or endorsement not held (point 4) — [Dir. 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj)
- [S] Netherlands self-regulation: misleading advertising is unfair under art. 7 of the Nederlandse Reclame Code, with art. 8 as the test for misleadingness; consumers can file complaints with the Stichting Reclame Code — [RCC appeal decision 2020/00267 (Boek9)](https://www.boek9.nl/system/files/B92020/B9CvBRCC20201013_Geanonimiseerde%20uitspraak%20dossier%202020%2000267%20CvB.pdf)

**UK ASA rulings (not binding in BE/NL, but directly on point)**
- [S] **Pressplay Ltd (A24-1266131), listed in ASA rulings of 29 January 2025.** Ad for anti-glare driving glasses claimed they reduce eye strain and enhance visibility "day or night", with "HELP PREVENT DANGEROUS HEADLIGHT […] GLARE", "SAY GOODBYE TO GLARE", "CRYSTAL-CLEAR VISION", "DRIVE SAFER AT NIGHT". The seller produced a certification report showing 79.38% luminous transmittance, above the 75% night threshold. The ASA still upheld the complaint: the ad must not appear again, and the seller must not state or imply that the glasses prevent glare or make driving safer without adequate evidence — [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/pressplay-ltd-a24-1266131-pressplay-ltd.html); [LexisNexis list of ASA rulings 29/01/2025](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/asa-rulings-29-january-2025)
- [S] **Visual Stress Consultancy Ltd (A24-1253433), same period.** Website claim (seen 24 July 2024) that the right tints could help with "blinding headlights at night". The lenses could not be classified, so it could not be confirmed they were legal for night driving; a disclaimer did not cure the impression. Upheld; the ASA also noted ads must not condone unsafe driving. The company removed the claim — [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/visual-stress-consultancy-ltd-a24-1253433-visual-stress-consultancy-ltd.html); commentary: [Optician Online](https://www.opticianonline.net/opinion/simon-jones-colour-me-bad)
- [S] **Boots (2015), not upheld.** The ASA accepted that a reflection-free ("anti-glare") coating on spectacle lenses reduces halo effects from headlights, so the claim was unlikely to mislead — [ASA ruling Boots A15-291507](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/boots-professional-services-ltd-a15-291507.html)
- [S] A further 2025 ASA ruling (ABD Supplies Ltd, A25-1277053) surfaced in the same searches and cites CAP Code rules 3.1, 3.3 and 3.9; its subject matter was not confirmed — [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/abd-supplies-ltd-a25-1277053-abd-supplies-ltd.html)

**Other jurisdictions**
- [S] New Zealand: the advertising complaints board found a "Tac Glasses" TV ad misleading and portraying an unsafe practice by associating the glasses with driving into oncoming traffic "when they aren't fit for this purpose" — [Consumer NZ](https://www.consumer.org.nz/articles/tac-glasses-tv-ad-portrayed-a-disregard-for-safety)
- [S] US FTC, NightSafe Glasses (consent order C-3736, file dated May 1997): the FTC alleged false and unsubstantiated claims that the glasses make night driving safer by improving night vision; the settlement barred those claims and the use of the "NightSafe" name or similar. No recent FTC action on yellow night-driving glasses was found — [FTC consent order](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/cases/1997/05/c3736.do.pdf)
- [S] No decision of the Dutch Reclame Code Commissie on night glasses was found in a targeted search — search of RCC-related sources, e.g. [Consumentenbond overview of RCC decisions](https://www.consumentenbond.nl/acties-claims/nieuws/2024/deze-fabrikanten-misleiden-volgens-de-reclame-code-commissie)

**Medical-device boundary**
- [T] Regulation (EU) 2017/745 Art. 2(1): a product is a medical device if the manufacturer intends it for a medical purpose such as diagnosis, prevention, treatment or alleviation of disease, or compensation for an injury or disability. Intended purpose is derived from labelling, instructions and promotional material (Art. 2(12)) — [Reg. 2017/745](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)

### Inferences
- **What is defensible.** Verifiable product facts: measured luminous transmittance, filter category, UV protection, "meets the requirements of EN ISO 12312-1 for use in twilight and at night" (only if the test report says so), anti-reflective coating reduces reflections on the lens surfaces.
- **What is not defensible without controlled evidence.** "Blocks/eliminates headlight glare", "see better at night", "safer cycling", "HD/night vision". The Pressplay ruling shows that legal night-compliance does not substantiate efficacy or safety claims. (Scientific evidence on whether yellow lenses help is covered by another researcher; if that evidence is negative, these claims are not merely unproven but false.)
- **Product name is a claim.** The FTC order banned the name "NightSafe". A brand or product name such as "NightRide" or "anti-verblinding" carries the same implied claim.
- **Medical wording moves the product into the MDR.** Phrases like "for night blindness", "helps with astigmatism/cataract glare", "reduces migraine" state a medical purpose. That would require medical-device compliance and also risks the blacklist (false cure claims). Keep all copy non-medical.
- **Belgian and Dutch law mirror the Directive.** In Belgium the rules sit in Book VI of the Code of Economic Law (unfair commercial practices towards consumers), enforced by the Economic Inspection; in the Netherlands in arts. 6:193a ff. of the Civil Code, enforced by ACM. Because the shop targets Dutch consumers, Dutch consumer law and ACM oversight apply to those sales alongside Belgian supervision. (Article numbers and fine levels: see Gaps.)
- **Safety-related advertising.** Both ASA rulings add a second ground beyond misleadingness: encouraging unsafe road behaviour. Imagery of riding into headlights with tinted lenses invites the same criticism under general advertising codes.

### Gaps
- The JEP (Belgium) decision database was not searched before the search budget ran out. No JEP decision is known to the researcher.
- The RCC database itself (reclamecode.nl) was not searchable; "none found" means none surfaced in one web search.
- The ASA rulings were read only through search summaries. Exact ruling dates, CAP Code rule numbers for Pressplay and Visual Stress, and the full reasoning were not seen (asa.org.uk could not be opened).
- Belgian article numbers (recollection: Code of Economic Law arts. VI.93–VI.100 for unfair practices, sanctions in Book XV) and fine levels, and ACM fine maxima, were not verified.
- No Commission or national borderline guidance on tinted glasses as medical devices was retrieved.

## 8. Market surveillance reality: authorities, campaigns, Safety Gate, sanctions

### Takeaway
Belgian product-safety enforcement sits with FOD Economie (Quality and Safety for the product, Economic Inspection for consumer law and advertising) and Customs at the border; in the Netherlands NVWA covers consumer products and ACM consumer law. No sunglasses-specific campaign or Safety Gate alert was found in this session, but recent Belgian and Dutch actions show the pattern that gets caught: missing or wrong-language warnings, missing or wrong CE marking, and non-EU webshop goods.

### Cited Findings
- [S] FOD Economie runs product-safety campaigns checking administrative compliance (labelling, CE marking, instructions for use) and technical safety, for example on safety vests — [FOD Economie, safety vests campaign](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/protective-equipment/safety-vests)
- [S] FOD Economie has run campaigns on bicycle helmets and on toys sold by non-European webshops — [bike helmet safety and visibility](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/safety-products-and-services/european-market-supervision/bike-helmet-safety-visibility); [toys sold by non-European webshops](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/safety-products-and-services/european-market-supervision/safety-toys-sold-non-european)
- [S] March 2026: FOD Economie checks found a large number of e-scooters sold in Belgium non-compliant; typical defects were warnings and safety information missing or not in the right language, and CE marking missing or wrongly applied; products were taken off the market — [VRT NWS, 20 March 2026](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/20/esteps-uit-de-handel-fodeconomie-beenders/)
- [S] Economic Inspection sweeps of webshops find high infringement rates on consumer-law points (7 in 10 checked webshops not in order on promotions) — [Forum for the Future](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/opgelet-7-op-10-gecontroleerde-webshops-niet-in-orde-met-promoties-/30698)
- [S] Netherlands: NVWA states it cannot act effectively against sellers outside the EU and has "no view of goods bought by Dutch consumers outside the EU"; in a test it found 80 unsafe products on a non-EU webshop in 10 minutes; it asks platforms to delist and shares results with other Member States and the Commission — [Radar](https://radar.avrotros.nl/artikel/80-onveilige-producten-gevonden-in-10-minuten-toezichthouder-waarschuwt-voor-webshops-buiten-eu-53798); [Hart van Nederland](https://www.hartvannederland.nl/advies-en-tips/waarschuwen/artikelen/helft-speelgoed-buitenlandse-sites-onveilig-nvwa)
- [S] Dutch regulators (including ILT) and Customs issued a joint signal that e-commerce platforms outside the EU create a market for unsafe products and called for a chain-wide approach — [ILT signal report](https://english.ilent.nl/documents/substances-and-products/supervision-of-online-shops/signal-reports/e-commerce-platforms-outside-the-eu-create-unsafe-market-products); [KVK, avoid selling unsafe products](https://www.kvk.nl/en/international/avoid-selling-unsafe-products-on-online-platforms/)
- [S] A targeted search for Safety Gate alerts on sunglasses citing EN ISO 12312-1 (insufficient UV protection, missing driving warning) returned no alert — search result set, e.g. [CCPC Ireland sunglasses page](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/product-safety/sunglasses)
- [T] Reg. 2019/1020 Arts. 25–28: customs authorities control products entering the EU and suspend release where documentation or required markings are missing or the CE marking is false, referring the case to the market surveillance authority — [Reg. 2019/1020](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1020/oj)

### Inferences
- **Enforcement asymmetry.** Authorities admit they cannot reach Chinese sellers. A Belgian sole trader with a KBO number, a Belgian address in the listing and a Belgian bank account is the easy target in the chain, and the GPSR Art. 19 listing data makes the responsible party visible at a glance.
- **Likely triggers for a micro-seller**: a consumer or competitor complaint, a thematic web sweep, a customs hold on an imported batch (model A/B), or an accident report. Probability of a random check is low; consequence when it happens is a sales stop, withdrawal/recall at the seller's cost, and possible fines.
- **The defects authorities look for are cheap to get right** (CE mark, address labels, Dutch/French warnings, declaration of conformity) and are exactly the ones dropshipped goods fail.
- **Advertising claims are a separate, parallel exposure** via the Economic Inspection, ACM, JEP/RCC complaints and consumer organisations (Test-Aankoop, Consumentenbond).

### Gaps
- **The Safety Gate database itself could not be queried.** Absence of results from one web search does not show absence of alerts. Researcher's recollection (unverified): alerts on sunglasses, especially children's, for inadequate UV filtering or wrong category marking do exist, and a PROSAFE joint market-surveillance action on sunglasses around 2010–2012 found high non-compliance on marking and information.
- No FOD Economie or NVWA campaign specifically on sunglasses, night-driving glasses or cheap online eyewear was found.
- Typical sanction amounts in Belgium and the Netherlands were not verified (see Q5 and Q7 Gaps).
- The division of PPE supervision in the Netherlands (recollection: NVWA for consumer PPE, Labour Inspectorate for workplace PPE) was not verified.

## 9. E-commerce basics for a Belgian seller (registration, VAT, customs, consumer rights, packaging, social status), and time/cost overview

### Takeaway
The administrative baseline is manageable: enterprise number, VAT small-business exemption up to €25,000 turnover, modest social contributions as student-entrepreneur or secondary-occupation self-employed. Since 1 July 2026 every low-value parcel from China carries customs duty (flat €3 per item line), which removes the cost advantage of dropshipping; regular imports of glasses pay about 2.9% duty plus 21% import VAT.

### Cited Findings

**VAT**
- [S] Belgian small-enterprise exemption threshold: €25,000 annual turnover (excl. VAT). One source reports a planned increase of €1,000 per year from 2027 to reach €30,000 in 2031 — [Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/financieel/fiscaliteit/btw/btw-vrijstelling)
- [S] EU-wide SME scheme (since 2025): a Belgian small enterprise can apply another Member State's exemption if its total EU turnover does not exceed €100,000 and it stays under that state's national threshold. This requires a prior notification via the Belgian portal in addition to the normal e-604A/B choice; the tax administration began processing prior notifications on 19 January 2026 — [Forum for the Future](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/sme-regeling-start-van-de-verwerking-van-voorafgaande-kennisgevingen-vanaf-19-januari-2026/29712); [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/vat-exemptions/indexamp_nl.htm); Dutch side: [Belastingdienst EU-KOR](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/kleineondernemersregeling-in-de-europese-unie-eu-kor)
- [S] Exempt small enterprises must file an annual customer listing, which since 2025 covers all turnover — [Accountable](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/help-center/alles-wat-je-moet-weten-over-de-status-van-btw-vrijstelling-voor-kleine-ondernemingen/)
- [T] Cross-border B2C distance sales of goods are taxed in the customer's Member State once the seller's total intra-EU distance sales exceed €10,000 per year (Art. 59c VAT Directive); the One-Stop Shop avoids registering in the Netherlands — [Dir. 2006/112/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj)

**Customs**
- [S] The €150 customs-duty exemption for low-value consignments ended on 1 July 2026. A flat €3 customs duty applies to goods in parcels worth €150 or less sent from outside the EU directly to consumers, until 1 July 2028, based on Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 (agreed 11 February 2026). It covers about 93% of e-commerce flows into the EU — [European Commission, customs reform](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/11/eu-end-150-customs-duty-exemption-2026.html); [Marosa](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/eu-removal-of-customs-duty-relief-for-low-value-consignments)
- [S] Counting method differs by source: the Commission says €3 "per item"; a customs broker applies it per declaration line grouped by 6-digit tariff code (three different codes in one parcel = €9) — [European Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en); [Gerlach Customs](https://gerlach-customs.com/?p=136126)
- [S] A separate €2 Union handling fee is scheduled from 1 November 2026 according to the Commission page as summarised in search — [European Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en)
- [S] EU third-country duty for HS 9004.10 (sunglasses): 2.9%, per a tariff aggregator citing TARIC — [tariff aggregator](https://custom-tariffs-seo.pages.dev/en/codes/9004-10-sunglasses); industry chart: [The Vision Council EU tariff chart](https://thevisioncouncil.org/sites/default/files/assets/media/TVC_TariffChart_EU_080125.pdf)

- **Small-business VAT exemption and the Netherlands.** Below €10,000 of cross-border B2C sales, Dutch customers are served under Belgian VAT rules (exempt under the Belgian scheme). Above €10,000, Dutch VAT applies unless the seller also claims the Dutch exemption through the EU SME scheme, or registers for OSS, which means leaving the exemption for those sales. An accountant should confirm before the threshold is approached.
- **Under the exemption, import VAT (21%) on stock is not recoverable**, so it is a real cost in the margin calculation.
- **Dropship economics after July 2026.** A €10 pair of glasses shipped directly from China now attracts €3 duty (30% of value), possibly the €2 handling fee, VAT and a carrier fee. Either the seller prepays (margin loss) or the customer is surprised at the door (returns, complaints, and a likely breach of the duty to state the total price including all charges).
- **Withdrawal right with China-sourced goods.** The seller must give a return route; if he wants the customer to pay return postage he must say so in advance, and a return address in China without clear cost information exposes him to bearing the cost.
- **Two-year guarantee is the seller's obligation**, not the Chinese supplier's. In the Netherlands the conformity period can exceed two years depending on the expected lifespan of the product (recollection of Dutch law, not verified).
