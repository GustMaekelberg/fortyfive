## 1. Which advertisers use animation or cartoon creative, by category, and is it the norm, an occasional winner or rare?

### Takeaway
The strongest category evidence covers mobile apps. Across large app-creative datasets, animation is the **norm and the winner for mobile games**, and **real-life or UGC footage wins for non-game apps, especially on social platforms**. Outside games, animation appears as a **brand-building or explanatory choice** by named advertisers: Lemonade (insurance), Duolingo (mascot-led), Imprint (the product itself is animated visual lessons) and Chartr (chart-driven media). It rarely appears as the dominant direct-response format. For most other categories in the brief (kids' education, non-profits, posters, board games, mechanism products), I found no quantitative evidence, only isolated examples.

### Cited Findings
**Mobile games (animation is the norm)**
- **[vendor] AppsFlyer, 2024:** about 220,000 creative variations from roughly 2,000 apps, minimum $50 spend per creative per month. For games, animated ads had about 26% higher IPM (installs per thousand impressions) than ads with real-life footage. A press release words the 26% as "engagement" rather than IPM. — [AppsFlyer 2024 report](https://appsflyer.com/?p=431613); [AppsFlyer state-of-creative key findings](https://appsflyer.com/resources/reports/gated/state-of-creative); [AppsFlyer press release](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/creative-optimization-data-report/)
- **[vendor] AppsFlyer, 2025:** 1.1M creatives across 1,300 apps. Game video ads *without* UGC performed about 20% better on average than ads with UGC (+25% on ad networks, +15% on DSPs). The report suggests games put more emphasis on animated characters within the ad. — [AppsFlyer 2025 report](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/creative-optimization-report-2025/); [AppsFlyer 2025 PR](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=464235)
- **[vendor] Liftoff 2025 Mobile Ad Creative Index:** 4.7 trillion impressions and 1.1bn installs, January 2023 to May 2025. Playables had 8× the impression-to-install rate of non-playables for top-spending games and 16× for other game advertisers. Top teams are "enhancing their highest-performing assets through 3D elements and motion". — [Liftoff highlights](https://www.liftoff.ai/blog/highlights-2025-mobile-ad-creative-index/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interactive-ads-surge-20-among-top-apps-as-advertisers-bet-big-on-playables)

- **[vendor] AppsFlyer, 2025:** UGC works best on social platforms for non-gaming apps (+22%) and gaming apps (+12%). — [AppsFlyer 2025](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/creative-optimization-report-2025/)
- **[vendor] Liftoff 2025:** app advertisers using UGC video raised impression-to-install conversion by an average of 152% over the prior year. This is a year-on-year change, not a head-to-head with animation. — [Newswire/Liftoff](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/liftoff-s-2025-mobile-ad-creative-index-reveals-ugc-is-a-key-differentiator-for-top-apps-837345804.html)
- **[vendor] AppsFlyer 2026 edition:** 1.2M creatives across 10 verticals. The extracts did not include an animated vs real-life comparison, so I could not confirm whether the 2024 finding still holds. — [AppsFlyer 2026](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/creative-optimization/)

**Physical products with mechanisms**
- **[vendor]** Ecommerce guides say "how it works" video helps because shoppers "can't picture how an item works or fits". No measured animation-specific result was found. — [Cloudinary](https://cloudinary.com/blog/questions/how-can-product-videos-improve-your-ecommerce-sales-with-tech-tips-for-better-ux/); [AcquireConvert](https://acquireconvert.com/video-animation/product-video/); [eCommerce Fastlane 3D playbook](https://ecommercefastlane.com/increasing-product-page-conversions-through-3d-animation/)

**Premium brands with hand-made animation (context)**
- Apple, Anthropologie, Prada, Dior, e.l.f. and Loewe have recently run claymation, stop-motion or hand-drawn ads. Commentators frame this partly as a way to "prove commitment to human creativity" and avoid accusations of AI use. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91638642/meet-the-marketers-fighting-ai-suspicion-advertisings-new-secret-weapon)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Category status, as far as the evidence goes:
  - **Mobile games:** animation is the **norm**.
  - **Micro-learning and book-summary apps:** animation or illustration is **common**, because the product is visual content.
  - **Insurance and fintech brand work:** animation is an **occasional winner**, used to make abstract or "boring" products feel warm.
  - **SaaS explainers:** animation is the **norm for explainers**, but not proven for direct-response short-form.
  - **Data media:** charts are the **core product**.
  - **Crowdfunding and board games:** animation is **supporting** (GIFs, how-to-play).
  - **Beauty, fashion, food and most physical goods:** animation is **rare** as a selling format.
- [synthesis] Animation works best where the thing being sold is not filmable in an interesting way (an insurance policy, a learning method, a statistic) or is itself visual or digital. It also works where a brand can own a recurring character (Duolingo, game mascots).

### Gaps
- No Meta Ad Library or TikTok Creative Center count of the animated share by category was found in trade press.
- No performance data for animated ads by kids' education apps, non-profits or health campaigns, infographic poster or print sellers, budgeting or meditation apps, or neobanks (Cleo and Monzo searches returned nothing usable).
- Headway's split of animated vs filmed creative is unknown.

## 2. Animated short-form formats in 2025–2026: what has documented reach or conversion, and how are they built?

### Takeaway
The best-documented "format" result for a code-only creator is not a cartoon but **text-forward creative**. Motion's 2026 benchmark ($1.29B Meta spend) finds *text-only* assets have the highest hit rate (11.6%). *Illustration* sits mid-pack (6.8%), and *Animation* as an asset type under-captures spend relative to its volume. Cartoon formats (comic strip, storytime, hand-drawn) are catalogued by practitioners as used by "apps, supplements, or services that need a narrative", but no hit rates were found for them. Chart races and data stories have proven large-scale *reach* (Data is Beautiful, Chartr), and practitioners also criticise them as low-information.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmarks (vendor)**
- **[vendor] Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026:** $1.29B Meta spend, 578,750 creatives, 6,015 accounts, launched September 2025 to January 2026. Hit rate by asset type:
  - text-only 11.60%
  - product image with text 8.75%
  - lifestyle-product image 7.59%
  - UGC 7.56%
  - high production 6.87%
  - GIF 6.82%
  - illustration 6.80%
  - UGC mashup 6.28%

  Animation and carousel rank at the bottom, and both "under-capture spend relative to their volume share". Motion's explanation: text assets "can be re-produced and re-tested in hours". Hit rate depends on account tier: 4.0% for micro accounts, 8.8% for enterprise. — [Motion: Text-forward wins](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types); [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology); [Motion benchmarks hub](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/); [Motion talk, March 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-5000-meta-ads-the-2026-creative-benchmarks-report/)
- **[vendor] Motion 2026, visual formats:** Offer-First Banner has an 8.6% hit rate, Unboxing about 9.83%, and Celebrity about 2.1× spend-use ratio. Format rankings change by vertical, and "category context dominates format performance". — [Motion: top visual formats](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats); [Motion: varies by vertical](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)

**Cartoon and story formats (definitions, no metrics)**
- **Comic Strip:** "illustrated panel(s) telling story in a comic book format", used by "brands with quirky personalities or complex stories (think apps, supplements, or services that need a narrative)". It can be static or video. — [Motion: Comic Strip](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/comic-strip)
- **Storytime:** a creator-led personal narrative leading to the product. The format "varies", so an animated version is possible. — [Motion: Storytime](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/storytime)
- **Hand-Drawn:** a catalogued format; no metrics extracted. — [Motion: Hand-Drawn](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/hand-drawn)
- **"AI Slop Animation":** Motion now lists this as a format, an AI-generated animated visual used as a "pattern interrupt" in cold traffic. It is relevant as the competition the reader's code-made animation will be compared with, and as a reason viewers may be primed to suspect AI. — [Motion: AI Slop Animation](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/ai-slop-animation); [Motion talk on anti-AI comments, January 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/defeat-the-i-hate-ai-slop-comments-with-this-simple-trick-in-2026/)
- **Text Interrupt:** "went viral and was copied by many brands" for BFCM 2025. Other hook devices named in 2025 Motion talks: handwriting, stop motion, "Ad Inception". — [Motion: 33 BFCM formats](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/33-black-friday-ad-formats-you-need-to-try-now-for-2025/); [Motion: 30 ad formats, July 2025](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/the-30-ad-formats-every-top-brand-is-stealing-in-2025/)

**Chart races and data stories**
- **Data is Beautiful (YouTube):** reportedly about 1M subscribers within roughly two months and several million views per video (OMR, 2019; old). — [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/datenvisualisierung-youtube-millionen-views)
- **Critics:** chart races were banned from a major data-viz subreddit, and Andy Cotgreave called them the "fidget spinner of data viz". — [Built In](https://builtin.com/data-science/bar-chart-races)
- **[vendor] Data to Video** (sells a chart-race maker) advises 1080×1920 export, rivalry topics (countries, athletes) and 20–45 s runtimes. Its claim that such videos "consistently break 100k views" is unverified. — [Data to Video blog](https://data-reel-maker.lovable.app/blog/how-to-make-viral-bar-chart-race-videos)
- **Stick-figure explainers:** vidIQ cites "Casual Finance" as reaching more than 300,000 YouTube subscribers in about a year. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-channel-ideas-without-showing-your-face/)

**Construction rules from platforms**
- **[platform] TikTok, Auction Ads Creative Tips:** over 63% of the highest-CTR videos highlight the key message or product in the first 3 seconds. Secondary sites quoting 67% or 71% are unverified. — [TikTok Auction Ads Creative Tips PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/viral-tiktok-hooks-examples/)
- **[platform] TikTok:** videos using on-screen text in the first 7 seconds saw a 43% conversion lift over those that showed text later. TikTok's help centre gives a text-density guideline, extracted as "5–10 words per second". That figure is oddly high; verify it before relying on it. — [TikTok blog: creative that drives conversions](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/ar/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions); [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=bg-BG)
- **[platform] Google/YouTube Shorts ABCDs:**
  - vertical, sound-on, "social first"
  - say and show the brand in the first 5 seconds
  - use title cards that work with sound off
  - a clear CTA
  - "sight and sound working together"

  Google also recommends a human face, which this reader cannot use. — [Google Ads Shorts guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697); [Google ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551); [Think with Google short-form tips](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/marketing-strategies/video/short-form-video-advertising-tips); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/best-practices-youtube-shorts-ads-according-youtube)
- **[platform/vendor] Meta Reels:**
  - build native 9:16, sound-on, fast hook, keep inside safe zones
  - [vendor] Adobe: add at least one extra element (text overlay, voiceover, early hook)
  - [vendor] MediaPost analysis: visual dynamism, including animation, lifts effectiveness by 74%, and 63% of analysed campaigns lacked it
  - opening with prominent logos or heavy text overlays was associated with lower CTR
  - suggested lengths: 10–18 s or 15–30 s (sources differ)

  — [Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-native-reels-ads-can-lift-purchase-intent-5-3x-465615); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401156/meta-ads-feed-on-intimacy-and-immediacy-to-improve.html); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/facebook-reels-ads-guide/)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] For a code-only seller, the evidence supports **kinetic text plus a product or UI visual** over full cartoon storytelling. It has the best documented hit rate, is cheapest to iterate, and is what HyperFrames does natively. Cartoon characters are better kept as a *recurring brand device* (Duolingo-style consistency) than as one-off storytime ads.
- [synthesis] "Animation" as Motion tags it (likely polished animated video) under-captures spend. The win comes from *message clarity and testing speed*, not from motion itself, so the reader should exploit cheap variant generation (10+ hooks per concept).
- [synthesis] Chart races buy reach, not necessarily sales. They suit a data newsletter, poster or "one chart a day" brand, and less so a direct offer.

### Gaps
- No hit-rate or CTR data for comic-strip, whiteboard/doodle, quiz, map, calculator ("what $X becomes") or satisfying-loop formats specifically.
- Motion's per-vertical breakdown and animation hit-rate figure were not in the extracts. The full PDF is at [content.motionapp.com](https://content.motionapp.com/hubfs/Benchmarks-Report-2026.pdf) (not fetched).
- No platform data on loopability or replay effects for animated shorts.

## 3. Evidence on animated vs live action for digital products and apps, and by purchase consideration

### Takeaway
Head-to-head data is scarce and almost all vendor or platform-sourced. It consistently says **live action or creators beat CG or animation on CTR and installs for non-game products** (TikTok: +25% CTR for live action; AppsFlyer 2024: +15% IPM for real footage in non-game apps). **Games are the exception**, where animation wins. Animation does beat **static images** on clicks in most small tests, though not always on cost per lead. No source split results by low vs high consideration.

### Cited Findings
- **[platform] TikTok Creative Accelerator playbook (14 August 2023):** "Live Action content has seen a 25% lift in click-through rate, compared to computer-generated-imagery creatives". CG "(images, animation etc.) can also be used", and CG-only teams should "add more visual and sound elements". — [TikTok Creative Accelerator](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en)
- **[platform] TikTok 2022 playbook:** ads including creators saw a 26.5% CTR lift vs "traditional CG ads". — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook.pdf)
- **[vendor] AppsFlyer 2024:** non-game apps got +15% IPM from real-life footage over animation; games got +26% from animation. — [AppsFlyer](https://appsflyer.com/resources/reports/gated/state-of-creative)
- **[vendor] Animated vs static:** VistaCreate reported 1.5× more clicks for an animated ad than for a static image. In an older AdEspresso test, still images had a 38.2% better cost per lead than animated images. — [VistaCreate](https://create.vista.com/blog/animation-vs-static-image-facebook-ads-a-case-study/); [AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/blog/animation-vs-still-images-one-wins)
- **[vendor]** Knowlify: Meta has not published a study pitting animated against filmed or creator ads for the same message. Motion tends to help relative to still images. Strong campaigns often combine UGC "for context" with animation or demos "for mechanism and proof". — [Knowlify](https://knowlify.com/articles/do-animated-ads-work-on-meta); [Knowlify UGC vs animated](https://knowlify.com/articles/animated-ads-vs-ugc-ads)
- **[anecdote] r/FacebookAds:** a commenter who claims to manage 100+ brands says UGC beat "motion graphic explainer stuff" in most tests, but "never worked well for courses". — [Reddit via redlib](https://redlib.cypherpunktools.com/r/FacebookAds/comments/1k0jf04/a_winning_factor_i_found_in_my_ads)
- **[vendor] AppsFlyer 2025 caveat:** read IPM alongside retention. For games on ad networks, retention was *higher* without UGC. — [AppsFlyer 2025](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/creative-optimization-report-2025/)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Expect a roughly 15–25% CTR or install handicap against filmed creators for non-game digital products (two independent vendor and platform figures land in this band). To compensate, a code-made ad must win on clarity of the offer (text-forward, product UI visible from second 1), which Motion's data says is a winning trait in its own right.
- [synthesis] The handicap is likely smaller where the creator's face adds little: software UI, data, templates, and courses (per the anecdote). It is larger where trust in a person drives the sale (coaching, health, beauty).
- [synthesis] Low-consideration, low-price digital offers ($5–$30) are where an impression-level handicap matters least, because the landing page and price do the rest. This is an inference; no data split by consideration level was found.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 data from Sensor Tower, Vidmob, System1, Triple Whale, Foreplay or Billo separating animation from UGC was found in extracts.
- No evidence on purchase rate (as opposed to CTR or IPM) for animated vs live action.

## 4. Sound: is voice-over needed, and how to add sound legally on a tiny budget

### Takeaway
Platforms all push **sound-on** design. Google says sound lifts conversions by over 20%, and TikTok/Kantar report that 88% of users call sound essential. Survey data still shows a large muted minority, so **captions or on-screen text are mandatory and voice-over is a plus, not a prerequisite**. Business accounts are restricted to commercial libraries: TikTok's Commercial Music Library and Meta's Sound Collection, each licensed only on its own platform. Commercially usable TTS exists free (Kokoro, Apache-2.0) or cheap (ElevenLabs Starter, about $5–6/month). ElevenLabs' free tier is not licensed for commercial use.

### Cited Findings
**Muted viewing**
- **[independent-ish survey, 2019] Verizon Media/Publicis Media**, 5,616 US adults aged 18–54:
  - 69% watch video with sound off in public
  - 25% do so even in private
  - 80% are more likely to finish a video when captions are available
  - 37% say captions encourage them to turn the sound on

  — [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)
- **The "85% watched without sound" figure** traces to a 2016 trade article in which two publishers self-reported it. No platform has confirmed it. Reels sound-on estimates conflict: about 60% (Zebracat, unsourced) vs "80%+" (secondhand Meta guidance). A "21% of Shorts viewers watch muted" figure is unconfirmed. TikTok's own footnotes credit two different studies (Kantar 2021 and Nielsen 2020). — [TikTok Kantar report](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-insights-into-the-importance-of-sound-for-marketing-promo/601569)

**Music rules for business accounts**
- **TikTok:** Business accounts can use only the Commercial Music Library. Promotional posts from personal accounts using general-library music also risk infringement (TikTok's own blog). TikTok may flag accounts as commercial based on activity. Licences cover TikTok only, not cross-posts. - **ElevenLabs:** the free plan has no commercial licence and requires attribution for non-commercial use. The cheapest commercial tier is Starter (about $5–6/month, sources differ). - **AI labelling of voices:** the earlier notes found that generic TTS on TikTok reportedly needs no label (secondary source). TikTok's 2023 Creative Accelerator advice to CG-only teams to "add more visual and sound elements" implies sound design matters more when there are no people on screen. — [TikTok Creative Accelerator](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Build every video to work fully muted: all claims as on-screen text, high-contrast captions, inside safe zones. Then add a sound layer: Sound Collection or CML music chosen inside each app at upload, plus SFX on transitions, plus an optional Kokoro or ElevenLabs voice-over.
- [synthesis] Practical way around the licensing: render the MP4 *without* music (or with only SFX and voice from your own licensed sources), and add the platform-licensed track in-app on each platform. Each library licenses only its own platform, so a pre-baked track won't transfer.
- [synthesis] Free SFX and music libraries outside the platforms (for example Pixabay or YouTube Audio Library) were not researched in this round. Check their licences before baking them into renders.

## 5. Faceless animated or graphic accounts that built an audience and sell something

### Takeaway
Beyond the cases in the earlier notes (Visualize Value, Personal Finance Club, Easlo, Visual Capitalist), the new verifiable example is **Chartr**: a chart-driven media brand that built 500K+ subscribers and about 517K Instagram followers, and was acquired by Robinhood's Sherwood Media. Its visuals (clean charts) are exactly what code can produce. Stick-figure and infographic YouTube channels (Casual Finance, The Infographics Show) show *reach*, but their revenue figures are estimates from SEO or vendor blogs.

### Cited Findings
- **Chartr:** 500K+ newsletter subscribers in about 5 years, about 517K Instagram followers, acquired by Sherwood Media (Robinhood). Growth came from Reddit virality plus paid acquisition (SparkLoop, ads). Its monetisation is sponsorship and newsletter, not a product per se. — [Newsletter Deep Dives](https://newsletterdeepdives.beehiiv.com/p/deep-dive-chartr)
- **Casual Finance (stick-figure finance explainers):** more than 300K YouTube subscribers in about one year. Revenue not disclosed. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-channel-ideas-without-showing-your-face/)
- **[anecdote/vendor] Stick-figure explainer channels** are claimed to earn "$1,200–$1,500 per week" (Contra freelancer page, no source). The Infographics Show is estimated at $100K–$300K/month in ad revenue (SEO guide estimate). Both are low reliability. — [Contra](https://contra.com/community/bZeXikva-simple-stick-figure-explainer-videos-are); [Metricool/vidIQ-type guides](https://metricool.com/faceless-youtube-channel/)
- **Data is Beautiful:** about 1M YouTube subscribers in roughly 2 months (2019). This is a reach example only. — [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/datenvisualisierung-youtube-millionen-views)
- **[vendor] Data-viz niche monetisation paths** listed by a chart-tool vendor: platform rewards, finance and education sponsors, a paid newsletter, and selling datasets or chart templates. It is promotional. — [Data to Video blog](https://data-reel-maker.lovable.app/blog/creator-economy-data-niche)
- **Mass-produced faceless content risk:** a vendor guide says July 2025 YouTube enforcement buries or demonetises "generic stock footage, robotic TTS, no editorial judgment" (consistent with the earlier notes on policy). — [Virvid (vendor)](https://virvid.ai/blog/ai-faceless-youtube-channel-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] The repeatable pattern is a **distinct chart or graphic style + a recurring content series + an owned channel (newsletter)**, which is then monetised with sponsorships or a product. Chartr fits this, and Visualize Value and Personal Finance Club (earlier notes) fit it too. None sold off a single viral ad. All built an audience first.
- [synthesis] Code-generated charts and kinetic text can reproduce the Chartr and Visual Capitalist look. Fully animated cartoon storytelling (The Infographics Show) is costlier to make well in code and has no sales evidence.

### Gaps

- Chartr's TikTok or Reels performance and its revenue were not found.

## 6. What does not work in animation, according to the evidence

### Takeaway
Avoid categories where filmed people or sensory proof carry the sale, and avoid polished-but-slow animation. In the data, "Animation" as an asset type under-captures spend, live action beats CG on CTR for non-game products, and AI-looking animation now draws backlash. Courses are a reported exception where UGC under-performs.

### Cited Findings
- **Animation under-captures spend** relative to its volume on Meta (Motion 2026). — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- **Live action +25% CTR** vs CG on TikTok, and creators +26.5% CTR vs traditional CG. — [TikTok Creative Accelerator](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en); [TikTok SMB playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook.pdf)
- **Real footage +15% IPM** over animation for non-game apps (AppsFlyer 2024). — [AppsFlyer](https://appsflyer.com/resources/reports/gated/state-of-creative)
- **Heavy branding or text up front:** opening with prominent logos or heavy text overlays was associated with lower CTR in Reels. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-native-reels-ads-can-lift-purchase-intent-5-3x-465615)
- **AI-looking visuals backfire:** Valentino's labelled AI ad was called "cheap and unsettling", and McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas ad. Brands turn to hand-made looks to avoid AI accusations. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91638642/meet-the-marketers-fighting-ai-suspicion-advertisings-new-secret-weapon); [McGraw Hill blog, May 2026](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/blog/2026/05/ai-slop-backfires-in-ads)
- **Synthetic gloss on tactile products:** a production firm warns that "synthetic gloss, a floating 3D look" clashes with the tactile world of board games. — [Start Motion Media](https://www.startmotionmedia.com/board-game-kickstarter-videos)
- **Chart races are viewed as low-information** by data-viz practitioners. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/data-science/bar-chart-races)
- **Animated vs static is not a guaranteed win:** in AdEspresso's test, still images had a 38.2% better cost per lead. — [AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/blog/animation-vs-still-images-one-wins)
- **Categories dominated by filmed proof:** TikTok Shop GMV is dominated by beauty and apparel (earlier notes).

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Product types to avoid:
  - beauty, skincare and cosmetics (texture, faces)
  - apparel and fit
  - food and taste (despite Nielsen's old food exception, short-form food sells on real footage)
  - fitness results and health claims (trust in a person, plus regulatory risk)
  - coaching and personal services
  - gadgets whose selling point is "look, it really works"
- [synthesis] Execution traps to avoid:
  - slow intros
  - logo-first openings
  - long explainer arcs (60–90 s SaaS-explainer pacing)
  - generic stock-style illustration that reads as AI
  - identical template re-renders (also an originality-policy risk)

### Gaps
- No documented case of a specific product failing *because* it used animation.

## 7. Practical creative rules for a 15–25 s code-generated ad that must sell on its own, and a product-fit synthesis

### Takeaway
[synthesis, grounded in the cited platform and vendor guidance] Lead with the product's core message as large on-screen text in the first 1–3 seconds. Show the product (UI, chart, finished print, mechanism) by about second 3. Make every claim legible muted. Keep scenes short (roughly 1.5–3 s each, about 6–10 scenes in 20 s; this pacing is my inference, not sourced). End with one explicit CTA. Generate many hook variants rather than polishing one film. Strongest fit: digital products whose value is a screen or a number. Weakest fit: sensory physical goods.

### Cited Findings (rules and their sources)
- **Key message or product in the first 3 s:** 63% of top-CTR TikTok ads do this. — [TikTok Auction Ads Creative Tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)
- **On-screen text early:** text within the first 7 s gave +43% conversion. — [TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/ar/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions)
- **Brand within the first 5 s, title cards that work muted, clear CTA, sound and visuals coordinated.** — [Google ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551); [Google Shorts guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697)
- **Text-forward assets have the highest hit rate; iterate in hours.** — [Motion 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- **CG-only creative should add extra visual and sound elements.** — [TikTok Creative Accelerator](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en)
- **9:16, safe zones, at least one extra element (text overlay or voice-over); avoid logo-first or heavy-text openings.** — [Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-native-reels-ads-can-lift-purchase-intent-5-3x-465615)
- **Length:** 10–18 s "sweet spot" or 15–30 s (vendor guides disagree); product-page clips 10–25 s. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/facebook-reels-ads-guide/); [AcquireConvert](https://acquireconvert.com/video-animation/product-video/)
- **Captions:** 80% more likely to finish with captions (2019 survey). — [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/)
- **Sound adds over 20% conversions** (Google, Shorts). — [Google Shorts guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697)
- **Comic strip and story formats suit "apps, supplements, or services that need a narrative".** — [Motion Comic Strip](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/comic-strip)
- **Combine "context" with animation for "mechanism and proof".** — [Knowlify](https://knowlify.com/articles/animated-ads-vs-ugc-ads)

### Inferences

**[synthesis] 20-second template skeleton**

| Time | Scene |
|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Hook: a big kinetic-text claim or number, moving within the first frame (no logo) |
| 2–5 s | The product appears: UI mock-up, chart or composited product photo |
| 5–15 s | Proof or mechanism: 3 steps, before/after, a counter animating, a checklist ticking |
| 15–18 s | Price or offer card |
| 18–20 s | CTA ("Link in bio", "Get the template"), with a brand mark only here |

Production notes for the skeleton:
- Captions on everything.
- Music added in-app from the CML or Sound Collection; optional Kokoro or ElevenLabs voice-over.
- Make 5–10 hook variants per concept and vary substance, not just numbers (originality policies).
- A loop-friendly last frame that matches the first is cheap to try, but its benefit is unsourced.

**[synthesis] Product-type fit table. This is my synthesis. Ratings combine the evidence above (sections 1–6) with the earlier notes; none comes from a single study.**

| # | Product type | Fit with code-generated animation | Animated format most likely to sell it | Evidence behind the rating | Example hook line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Easlo and Personal Finance Club (earlier notes); text-forward wins (Motion 2026) | "I stopped guessing where my $3,200 paycheck goes." |

### Gaps
- No sourced optimal scene length or cut frequency for animated shorts. The 1.5–3 s pacing is my inference.
