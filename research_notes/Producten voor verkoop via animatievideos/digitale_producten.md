# Digital products as a first e-commerce experiment (solo seller, Belgium, <€500, sold in English, promoted with faceless animated shorts)

Research date: 19 November 2026. These notes rely almost entirely on web-search result extracts. Direct page fetches were blocked: gumroad.com failed DNS under the session allowlist, so no official fee page could be opened directly. Most fee figures come from third-party fee guides that quote the official pages, and many of those guides are written by competitors. Treat every fee as "reported as of 2026, verify on the official page before launch".

Labels used throughout:
- **[HARD]** official or filed data (SEC filing, official help page).
- **[TOOL]** marketplace-research-tool estimate.
- **[SELF]** self-reported or anecdotal.
- **[VENDOR]** vendor or competitor marketing.
- **[SYN]** my own synthesis or calculation.

Currency: EUR conversions use an **assumed rate of $1 = €0.86**. This rate was not looked up in this session; the report writer should verify the November 2026 rate.

## 1. Category overview: price points, delivery, effort, demand 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Spreadsheet templates are the best-documented small-seller category with real money in it. Etsy budget sheets range from $0.99 to about $25, and one documented seller moved from $2–3 printables to $20 sheets and reached six figures. They are also very crowded at the low end.

Generic printables, Canva templates and prompt packs show the clearest signs of AI-driven saturation and falling prices. Hosted tools, niche systems and anything with automation are the categories sources point to as defensible. Hard demand data by category barely exists; most "best-selling category" numbers are unsourced blog estimates.

### Inferences
- [SYN] Categories with **growing or holding** demand: spreadsheet *systems* with automation, hosted tools and calculators, Framer/website templates (the platform pays out a lot but is crowding), niche Notion systems.
- [SYN] Categories with **collapsing prices**: generic printables, Canva templates (median $6), prompt packs (the buyer can generate them with the same AI), generic resume templates.
- [SYN] **Too early or too thin**: AI-agent skills.
- [SYN] **Platform-gated**: stock motion templates. Motion Array submissions are closed and Envato takes 50%, which is poor for a learning experiment but relevant to the reader's skill set.
- [SYN] Best fit for 15–40 s animated videos: spreadsheets and calculators, because "watch the debt bar shrink" or "type your salary, see the chart" is a natural UI-mock-up hook. Motion-template packs also fit, because the product *is* the animation.
- [SYN] Poor fit: presets/LUTs (they need filmed footage to demo), e-books (static), communities.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–26 Etsy or Gumroad sales data by category. "Digital downloads = $1.2B or $1.9B a year on Etsy" figures conflict and are unsourced. — [Vendoo](https://blog.vendoo.co/top-selling-items-on-etsy); [Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/digital-products-etsy-2026)
- No trade-press article (Business Insider, The Verge and similar) on AI's effect on templates was found in the search extracts.
- Canva Creators program royalty terms were not found.

## 2. Marketplaces vs own store: fees, payouts, Belgium/EUR, net margin on $9/$19/$39

### Takeaway
On fees alone, Polar (4% + 40¢), Lemon Squeezy (5% + 50¢), Payhip free (5% + processor), Ko-fi free (5% + processor) and Whop keep about 88–95% of a $19 sale. Gumroad direct and Etsy keep about 83–87%, and Etsy falls to about 69% when an Offsite Ads sale occurs. Envato takes 50%.

Etsy is the only option here that brings meaningful buyer traffic of its own. Gumroad Discover (30% fee) and the Notion and Framer marketplaces bring some. For someone driving traffic from their own videos, an own-store merchant of record (Payhip, Lemon Squeezy, Polar or Gumroad) is cheapest and simplest.

### Net margin table [SYN: my calculations from the reported fees above; seller fees only, before VAT/sales tax and income tax]
Assumptions:
- Buyer is in the US.
- One item per order.
- Etsy uses the EU processing rate of 4% + $0.30 as an approximation, plus the $0.20 listing fee.
- "+FX" means Etsy's 2.5% currency conversion when listing in USD and paid out in EUR.
- Gumroad is shown both with processing included (A) and with 2.9% + 30¢ on top (B).
- Lemon Squeezy includes the 1.5% international-card surcharge.
- Payhip, Ko-fi and Stan use processing of 2.9% + $0.30.

## 3. Earnings reality: distribution, medians, refunds

### Takeaway
Earnings are extremely skewed. Etsy's 5.6M active sellers average about $1,870 GMS each, and the median is far lower. Third-party estimates suggest that most shops make under $100 a year. On Gumroad, about 5% or fewer of creators make more than $1k a month, and median creator income was about $70 a year in 2020 (about $72 a month in one 2026 estimate).

A realistic target for a first experiment is the first 1–50 sales over 3–6 months, not income. Refunds for templates and e-books are low compared with courses.

## 4. Demand signals: finance/budget products and adjacent niches

### Takeaway
Budget and finance spreadsheets have proven demand on Etsy: listings with thousands of reviews, a top seller at six figures, and search interest that peaks in January according to tool estimates. They are also among the most crowded niches, with price anchors at $0.99–$2. Paid apps cost about $100 a year, which frames the value of a one-time $9–$19 sheet or tool.

Adjacent niches with good animated-explainer fit include freelancer/small-business tools, job-search kits ($4–$12 resume templates), meal planners ($4–$10) and travel planners (no data found). Of these, freelancer and creator tools look less commoditised than resumes and meal planners.

## 5. Differentiation and paid web tools

### Takeaway
Defensibility comes from things a free or AI-generated copy lacks: working logic and automation, a hosted experience, niche specificity, bundles and updates, and visible human proof, which here means the seller's own videos. A small paid web tool at $5–$29 one-time is realistic as a learning project. The disclosed revenue examples are mostly hundreds to low thousands of dollars a month, though, and those come from broader tools with existing distribution. Free calculator hubs often earn $0.

### Cited Findings
- [BLOG] Buyers want "curated systems, hyper-specific solutions, and verified human quality". AI produced "a flood of nearly identical listings". — [Deadline Funnel](https://www.deadlinefunnel.com/blog/etsy-digital-products)
- [BLOG] "Insights and quantified results from your own use provide value that AI cannot generate" (on Notion templates). — [wikidocs](https://wikidocs.net/blog/@knarchive/14281/)
- [SELF] PrettyArrow's income jump came from moving from static printables ($3) to functional spreadsheets ($20). — [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/selling-spreadsheets-on-etsy/)
- [SELF] One-time-payment tools: Gramlab about $17k in one month; Solo Founder Pal about $10k/mo run rate (blog case study); Stagetimer about $20k/mo (mixed model). — [Indie Hackers Gramlab](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-we-made-17-000-in-one-month-with-a-one-time-payment-saas-no-ads-needed-95f68956e0); [ThinkDrop](https://thethinkdrop.blogspot.com/2026/03/how-solo-founder-pal-ai-hit-10kmonth.html); [Indie Hustle](https://indiehustle.beehiiv.com/p/a-countdown-timer-for-videos-making-10-000-mrr)
- [SELF] Low-end reality: a free calculator hub had $0 revenue, and a set of 10 free finance calculators projected only $50–$200/mo from ads. Extension sellers earn about 50¢ per sale at low prices. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/built-12-free-developer-cost-calculators-in-one-hub-109-users-day-1-with-zero-promotion-eb92ca5a05); [Indie Hackers ExtensionPay](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/extensionpay)
- [VENDOR] Selling skill or code files exposes the source; hosted access keeps it private. — [Agent37](https://www.agent37.com/blog/mcp-skills-monetization)
- Polar supports license keys and file downloads at the same fee ([Polar fees](https://docs.polar.sh/documentation/polar-as-merchant-of-record/fees)), which makes it a fit for gating a hosted tool.

### Inferences
- [SYN] Ranking of defensibility levers for this reader:
  1. Hosted interactive tool (it cannot be copied as a file; inputs animate, so it doubles as video content).
  2. Sheet with formulas, Apps Script or automation plus a video walkthrough.
  3. Niche specificity (e.g. US freelancers' quarterly estimated tax, or creators' sponsorship rate calculator).
  4. Bundle plus lifetime updates.
  5. Localisation: the reader's Belgian knowledge does not match a US audience, so localising for the US means working from US rules the reader must research.
- [SYN] A paid web tool is a realistic *alternative format*: a free version or free calculation drives traffic, and a one-time $9–$19 unlock adds saved scenarios, PDF export or advanced modes. Expect fewer impulse buys than on Etsy, because the tool has no marketplace traffic.

### Gaps
- No disclosed-revenue example was found for a consumer personal-finance calculator with a one-time unlock specifically.

## 6. Funnel and pricing mechanics; launch cost

### Takeaway
Typical benchmarks:
- Landing page opt-in: about 6.6% median across all industries (Unbounce), and 18–19% for lead-generation pages or email traffic.
- Email list to paid product: about 1–3% per launch, or about 0.3% per send (MailerLite).
- Order bumps: taken by 35–40% of buyers per SamCart; Teachable reports only about an 8% lift in order value.
- Pay-what-you-want: draws many $0 takers and a modest average price.

For a small account, a direct link-in-bio sale of a $9–$19 product plus a free lead magnet is a sensible dual setup. The whole launch can be done for under €100, and practically for about €15–€40 (domain plus a few Etsy listings), using free-plan platforms.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Unbounce median landing page conversion is 6.6% (Q4 2024, about 41k pages); email traffic averages 19.3%. Leadpages: lead-gen pages average 18%. — [ClickMinded](https://www.clickminded.com/landing-page-conversion-benchmark/); [Leadpages 2026](https://www.leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks-2026)
- [VENDOR/blog] Email-to-paid for digital products: 1–3% (warm list with launch sequence) up to 2–5%. MailerLite reports an average of 3 sales per 1,000 recipients per email send. — [Kajabi](https://www.kajabi.com/blog/email-marketing-to-sell-digital-products); [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/email-marketing-for-creators)
- [TOOL] Stan Store: modelled average order value $85 and conversion 3.5% (2.5–4% range). This is not Stan data, and the $85 is likely skewed by coaching products. — [ConvertMate](https://www.convertmate.io/revenue-tracking/stan-store)
- [VENDOR] Order bumps convert at 35–40% and a one-click upsell raises order value about 68% (SamCart, rival vendor). Teachable reports about an 8% lift with an order bump. — [SamCart](https://samcart.com/blog/how-to-increase-average-order-value); [Teachable](https://teachable.com/blog/order-bumps-drive-sales)
- [SELF] Pay-what-you-want: one seller saw lots of $0 takers but an average sale of $2.81. A WordPress theme got 100 downloads, 6 paid, £41 total. — [Alone on a Hill](https://aloneonahill.com/blog/pay-what-you-want-pricing-experiments); [WPShout](https://wpshout.com/?p=4578)
- [TOOL] Pay-what-you-want products are about 25% of Gumroad listings; median paid price $7. — [profitable.app](https://profitable.app/gumroad/stats)
- Cost items:
  - Etsy listing $0.20 each ([Checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees)).
  - Free plans: Payhip, Ko-fi, Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy and Polar have no monthly fee per the sources above.
  - Stan $29/mo after a 14-day trial ([CartMango](https://cartmango.com/stan-store-pricing/)).
  - Shopify Starter $5/mo but closed to new stores ([Kanal](https://getkanal.com/blog/shopify-starter-plan)).
- [GAP] Domain cost was not searched; a .com typically costs about €10–€15 a year (background knowledge).

### Inferences
- [SYN] Worked funnel for a small account, with illustrative numbers that are not sourced:
  - 10,000 short-video views lead to about 0.5–1% profile or link clicks (50–100 visitors; my assumption).
  - At about 2–4% purchase conversion, that is 1–4 sales of a $9–$19 product.
  - Alternatively, at about 20% lead-magnet opt-in, 10–20 emails; at 1–3% buying later, fewer than one sale.
  - So at this scale, Etsy's own search traffic may matter more than the videos for first sales, while the videos build the list and proof.
- [SYN] Budget plan under €100:
  - Domain about €12.
  - 20 Etsy listings about $4 (≈ €3.50) for first-shop listings, renewing every 4 months.
  - Gumroad, Payhip or Polar free plans €0.
  - Email: Kit, MailerLite or the platform's built-in email list free tier (tier limits not verified in this session).
  - Hosting a web tool on a free static host €0.
  - Optional: Etsy Ads €20–€30 test.
  - Total about €40–€50, leaving €400+ for paid video promotion tests or buying 2–3 competitor products to study.

### Gaps
- No platform-published link-in-bio click-through rate was found.
- No average order value for low-ticket template sellers specifically was found.
