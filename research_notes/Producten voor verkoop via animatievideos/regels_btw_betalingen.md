# Tax, legal, payment and admin set-up for a Belgian (Flemish) micro online seller (budget < €500), selling digital products, POD/dropship goods or affiliate links to US and worldwide consumers via short-form video — status 19 November 2026

Method note: researched on 19 Nov 2026 with ~42 web searches. Direct page fetches were blocked by the session network allowlist (a fetch of unizo.be failed with ENOTFOUND), so almost all findings come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not full reads. Legal articles marked "(legal text, not fetched)" are cited from the official text as I know it, with EUR-Lex/Justel links, and were not re-read in this session. Confidence levels are given where they matter. This is general regulatory research, not legal or tax advice. Belgian status, VAT and US-tax points need confirmation from an accountant, ondernemingsloket or social insurance fund before acting.

## 1. Belgian status and thresholds (self-employed registration, diverse inkomsten, platform regime, bijberoep, student-zelfstandige, social contributions, fixed costs)

### Takeaway
Recurring sales of your own products (digital or POD) or regular affiliate income are a professional activity. That means an enterprise number via an ondernemingsloket (€111.50 in 2026), joining a social insurance fund and, for VAT, at least a VAT registration under the small-enterprise exemption. The trigger is regularity, not an amount. "Diverse inkomsten" (33%) is only defensible for a truly one-off gain. The deeleconomie regime covers services only, so it does not cover selling goods. In bijberoep (alongside a job) no social contributions are owed below €1,922.16 net taxable income in 2026. A student-zelfstandige (18–25, at least 27 study credits) owes none below €8,687.04. The realistic fixed yearly cost of a minimal set-up is low: one-off €111.50, about €0 in social contributions if income stays under the threshold, and fund management fees only on contributions actually owed. A separate bank account and an accountant are optional.

### Cited Findings
**Registration trigger / diverse inkomsten**
- Unizo: whether you must register as self-employed depends less on how much you earn and more on how regularly you get income from it. Sporadic income can possibly be declared under "diverse inkomsten", but that carries risks, and Unizo advises checking with the local tax office first — [Unizo, "Hoeveel mag ik verdienen vooraleer ik me moet inschrijven als zelfstandige?"](https://www.unizo.be/starters/advies/moet-ik-starten-als-zelfstandige-voor-een-occasionele-activiteit) (search extract; fetch blocked).
- The law sets no limit on the number of transactions or on a tax-free amount. Once you make a profit it becomes "een handeltje" and the income is taxed as occasional income (FOD Financiën spokesperson, Feb 2024) — [VRT NWS 15/02/2024](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2024/02/15/belastingen-tweedehands-verkoop/).
- Diverse (occasional) income goes in the "Diverse inkomsten" section of the tax return and is taxed at 33%, unless your marginal rate is lower — [VLAIO, Bijverdienen, flexi-job of zelfstandige in bijberoep?](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/ondernemen-bijberoep/bijverdienen-flexi-job-zelfstandige-bijberoep). Legal basis: art. 90, 1° WIB 92 (occasional profits or gains), art. 171 WIB 92 (33% rate) (legal text, not fetched) — [Fisconetplus WIB 92](https://eservices.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/fisconet).
- A government plan for a "de minimis" exemption for occasional income from sales on online platforms (Vinted type) is mentioned. A legal source says the De Wever coalition agreement foresees an exemption up to €2,000, but it was "not yet anchored in enforceable legislation" — [Unizo deeleconomie](https://www.unizo.be/advies/actief-binnen-de-deeleconomie-hoeveel-belastingen-betalen-wat-met-sociale-bijdragen); [legalnews.be (Everest) DAC7 en de Belgische onlineverkoper](https://legalnews.be/handel-consument/dac7-en-de-belgische-onlineverkoper-fiscale-schaduwen-over-de-digitale-rommelmarkt-everest/). Not confirmed as enacted by Nov 2026. It would in any case target occasional sales of one's own goods, not a recurring product business.

**Deeleconomie regime**
- The Belgian deeleconomie regime applies only to services provided through a platform recognised by FOD Financiën. Renting movable/immovable goods falls outside it. A limit of €7,700 is cited for 2025 income; no 2026 amount was found — [Unizo, Een platform voor deeleconomie](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/bedrijfsvoering/ondernemingsvorm/deeleconomie-0); [Forum for the Future, deeleconomie update](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/deeleconomie-update-documentatiemap/29034). Consequence: selling goods or digital products to the public through Etsy/Gumroad is not deeleconomie income.

**Registration cost**
- KBO registration via an ondernemingsloket costs €111.50 in 2026 (exempt from VAT). It is a regulated tariff, the same at all approved loketten. Changes also cost €111.50. VAT activation through a loket is an optional paid service, e.g. Partena €64.05 excl. VAT (€77.50 incl.). Accountable estimates about €70 — [Partena tariefkaart ondernemingsloket 1 maart 2026 (PDF)](https://www.partena-professional.be/sites/default/files/uploads/Tarieven%20EOL/Partena%20Professional%20Tarieven%20Ondernemingsloket%201%20maart%202026.pdf); [billy.tech, inschrijving KBO prijs](https://billy.tech/nl/gids/onderneming-starten/kosten/inschrijving-kbo-prijs/); [VLAIO, Een ondernemingsnummer aanvragen](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/opstart-van-je-onderneming/opstarten-7-stappen/een-ondernemingsnummer-aanvragen). One outlier (Bobex) cites €105–110, probably outdated — [Bobex](https://www.bobex.be/nl-be/sociaal-secretariaat/vennootschap-oprichten/welk-ondernemingsloket-kiezen/).
- You can also activate a VAT number yourself at FOD Financiën for free (form 604A via Intervat/MyMinfin). That is my inference from the fact that the loket fee is an optional service. Confirm it.

**Self-employed in secondary occupation (bijberoep)**
- 2026: no social contributions if net taxable professional income is below €1,922.16. Above that about 20.5% is due. The provisional minimum for bijberoep is €98.51 per quarter — [Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/bedrijfsvoering/starten/wie-moet-starten-als-zelfstandige); [Accountable, sociale bijdragen zelfstandigen in bijberoep](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/sociale-bijdragen-zelfstandigen-in-bijberoep/); official table: [FOD Sociale Zekerheid, bijdragennota 2026 tabel (PDF)](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/sites/default/files/content/docs/nl/sociaal-beleid-vorm-geven/zelfstandigen/bijdragennota_2026_tabel_nl.pdf) (not opened; figures consistent across Unizo/Accountable/Billy).
- Unizo reports that this threshold will rise by €1,000 a year from 2027 up to €30,000 in 2031. This is unverified in official sources and is probably a misreading or conflated with another measure. Treat it as unconfirmed — [Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/starters/advies/moet-ik-starten-als-zelfstandige-voor-een-occasionele-activiteit).
- Bijberoep requires a main activity at least equivalent to half-time employment (general rule; not re-verified this session) — [VLAIO bijberoep](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/ondernemen-bijberoep/bijverdienen-flexi-job-zelfstandige-bijberoep).

**Student-zelfstandige**
- Conditions: aged 18–25, enrolled for at least 27 study credits or 17 hours of lessons per week, with an application at the social insurance fund (possible up to 6 months in advance). The status ends in the quarter you no longer meet the conditions, after the quarter of graduation, or on 30 September of the year you turn 25. If you keep operating without meeting the conditions, you are treated as main-occupation self-employed, with much higher contributions — [VLAIO, statuut student-zelfstandige](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/start/student-zelfstandige/statuut-van-student-zelfstandige); [KBC](https://www.kbc.be/ondernemen/nl/artikel/zelfstandige-worden/eigen-zaak-starten/statuut-student-zelfstandige.html); [student.be](https://www.student.be/nl/student-life/student-zelfstandige-alles-wat-je-moet-weten/); [Vlaanderen.be](https://www.vlaanderen.be/student-zelfstandige).
- 2026 thresholds: below €8,687.04 net taxable income, no contributions. Between €8,687.04 and €17,374.08, 20.5% on the part above €8,687.04. From €17,374.08, the same as main occupation. Exemption is not automatic, so a student who is unsure may pay the €98.51/quarter minimum provisionally — [Wikifin, statuut student-zelfstandige](https://www.wikifin.be/nl/belasting-werk-en-inkomen/ondernemen/het-statuut-van-student-zelfstandige); [Accountable student-zelfstandige](https://www.accountable.eu/blog/student-zelfstandige/). Partena FAQ figures (€8,430.73 / €16,861.46) are from an earlier year — [Partena FAQ](https://www.partena-professional.be/nl/faq-ondernemer).
- In Flanders the Groeipakket (child benefit) is lost if student-zelfstandige net taxable income exceeds €17,374.08 (2026) — same search extract as above ([Wikifin](https://www.wikifin.be/nl/belasting-werk-en-inkomen/ondernemen/het-statuut-van-student-zelfstandige)). Being dependent on parents for tax purposes (fiscaal ten laste) has separate net-means limits — [Securex lex4you](https://www.securex.be/nl/lex4you/werkgever/themas/aanwerven-arbeidsovereenkomst/bijzondere-overeenkomsten/blijft-de-student-fiscaal-ten-laste-van-zijn-ouders).

**Main occupation (for comparison)**
- The minimum contribution base for main occupation in 2026 is €17,374.08 — [Unizo](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/bedrijfsvoering/starten/wie-moet-starten-als-zelfstandige). My calculation: 20.5% × €17,374.08 ≈ €3,562/yr ≈ €890/quarter before fund fees. Verify against the [2026 bijdragennota table](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/sites/default/files/content/docs/nl/sociaal-beleid-vorm-geven/zelfstandigen/bijdragennota_2026_tabel_nl.pdf).

**Social insurance fund (mandatory affiliation) and management fees**
- Management fees are charged as a percentage on top of contributions: Xerius 3.05%, Liantis 3.95% (Accountable comparison, Jan 2026). Xerius confirms its quarterly amounts include 3.05% management fees — [Accountable, welk sociaal verzekeringsfonds](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/welk-sociaal-verzekeringsfonds/?amp=1); [Xerius sociale bijdragen](https://xerius.be/nl-be/zelfstandigen/sociale-zekerheid/sociale-bijdragen). With €0 in contributions the percentage fee is €0. Whether a fund charges a fixed affiliation fee was not confirmed.

### Inferences
- For an employed person or a student, a test with first income of a few hundred to a few thousand euros fits within the zero-contribution thresholds (bijberoep €1,922.16 net, i.e. after costs; student €8,687.04). The binding constraint is the paperwork: KBO number, fund affiliation, VAT notification and a tax return with a results section. Contributions are not the binding constraint.
- For someone who is neither employed (at least half-time) nor a student, any registered activity is main occupation, with about €890/quarter minimum contributions. That would make any paid set-up exceed the €500 budget within one quarter. This is the single biggest cost risk, so status eligibility should be checked first.
- Using "diverse inkomsten" for a shop that is promoted continuously through videos and sells repeatedly is weak. Regular marketing plus repeated sales point to a professional activity. Professional confirmation (tax office or accountant) is required if the reader wants to try it for a one-off test.
- Fixed yearly costs (my synthesis): €111.50 one-off KBO; optional €0–78 VAT activation; social contributions €0 below the threshold (provisional quarterly €98.51 may be charged unless a reduction is requested); accountant optional (€0 if DIY; Accountable/Dexxter-type apps cost roughly €100–300/yr, not verified this session); business bank account optional for a natural-person enterprise (many free or low-cost options; not verified this session).

### Gaps
- The official 2026 bijdragennota table PDF could not be opened. Figures rely on consistent secondary sources (Unizo, Accountable, Wikifin).
- 2026 deeleconomie ceiling and current status of the planned €2,000 de minimis for occasional platform sales: not found / not enacted as far as found.
- Whether funds charge a fixed fee when contributions are €0: not found.
- The Unizo claim that the bijberoep threshold rises to €30,000 by 2031 is unverified.

## 2. VAT: Belgian small-enterprise exemption, place of supply, €10,000 threshold, OSS, EU SME scheme, non-EU customers, marketplaces/merchants of record, DAC7

### Takeaway
A Belgian micro-seller can use the small-enterprise exemption (turnover ≤ €25,000, art. 56bis WBTW). It covers B2C electronically supplied services to EU consumers only while total EU cross-border B2C e-services and distance sales stay ≤ €10,000 (art. 59c VAT Directive), because the supply then stays in Belgium. B2C digital services to US/non-EU consumers are supplied where the customer lives (art. 58), so they are outside the scope of EU VAT. When you sell through a merchant of record (Gumroad, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Stripe Managed Payments) or an app store, the platform is the deemed supplier to the consumer (art. 9a Reg. 282/2011 / contractually). That removes all consumer-facing VAT and sales-tax handling. You then make a B2B supply to the platform, and the platform reports you under DAC7 if you exceed 30 sales or €2,000. Even an exempt small enterprise usually still has a Belgian VAT number and must self-assess Belgian VAT on foreign B2B services it buys (Meta ads, SaaS) via a special return. Confirm this point with an accountant.

### Cited Findings
**Belgian small-enterprise exemption (vrijstellingsregeling kleine ondernemingen)**
- Threshold: annual turnover ≤ €25,000 excl. VAT. Notification is made with form e-604A/e-604B — [cleartax BE](https://www.cleartax.com/be/nl/vat-exemption-scheme-belgium); [Accountable, wijzigingen 2025 btw-vrijstelling kleine ondernemingen](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/blog/wijzigingen-2025-btw-vrijstelling-kleine-ondernemingen/). Legal basis art. 56bis Wetboek btw (legal text, not fetched) — [Fisconetplus](https://eservices.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/fisconet).
- From 2025 there is a dual threshold: national €25,000 plus an EU-wide €100,000 for using the exemption in other Member States, with prior notification listing the Member States and turnover per country — [VLAIO, Nieuwe regeling btw-vrijstelling kleine ondernemingen](https://www.vlaio.be/en/node/12973); [BDO Belgium, 6 key details of the new VAT franchise regime 2025](https://www.bdo.be/en-gb/insights/news-alerts/2024/6-key-details-vat-franchise-regime-for-small-businesses-in-belgium-starting-2025). €100,000 EU cap = art. 284(2)(b) VAT Directive as amended by Directive (EU) 2020/285 (legal text, not fetched) — [EUR-Lex 32020L0285](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2020/285/oj).
- FOD Financiën: since 1 Jan 2025, Belgian-established taxable persons can be exempt in one or several other Member States under the SME scheme, after prior notification via a new Intervat module. Foreign users receive an "EX"-suffixed number — [finance.belgium.be, SME scheme](https://finance.belgium.be/en/enterprises/vat/international/sme-scheme); [Commission SME VAT rules explanatory notes (PDF)](https://sme-vat-rules.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/sme-explanatory-notes_en.pdf).
- In October 2025 the Commission called on Belgium, France and Malta to complete implementation of the cross-border SME scheme (IT systems for information exchange) — [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/European-Commission-Calls-on-B-60087). Whether Belgium has fully fixed this by Nov 2026 was not confirmed.
- The EU's Your Europe page states that a national SME exemption also applies to telecom, broadcasting and electronic (TBE) services you supply — [Your Europe, VAT exemptions](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/vat-exemptions/indexamp_nl.htm).
- A Belgian practitioner source says that for calculating the €25,000 threshold, services supplied outside Belgium must be assessed under the general B2B rule (wording ambiguous) — [Accountable help center](https://www.accountable.eu/nl-be/help-center/alles-wat-je-moet-weten-over-de-status-van-btw-vrijstelling-voor-kleine-ondernemingen/).

**Place of supply / €10,000 / OSS**
- Art. 58 VAT Directive: B2C electronically supplied services are taxed where the customer is established, has a permanent address or usually resides — [VAT Directive art. 58 (legislation.gov.uk mirror)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/112/article/58/data.html); official: [EUR-Lex 32006L0112](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj).
- Art. 59c (inserted by Directive 2017/2455, in force since 1 July 2021): an EU-established supplier whose total cross-border B2C TBE services plus intra-EU distance sales of goods stay below €10,000 in the current and previous calendar year keeps the supply in its home state. Once the threshold is exceeded, destination rules apply from that point — [Revenue.ie, electronically supplied services](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-services/electronic-services/vat-and-electronically-supplied-services/index.aspx); [Directive 2017/2455 art. 1 (legislation.gov.uk)](https://legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2017/2455/article/1/data.pdf); Belgian explainer: [practicali.be, webverkopen nieuwe regels 1 juli 2021](https://www.practicali.be/blog/webverkopen-nieuwe-regels-inzake-btw-op-1-juli-2021).
- Above €10,000 the seller charges the customer-country VAT and can declare it quarterly through the OSS Union scheme in Belgium — [SBB, OSS-regeling voor B2C-diensten](https://www.sbb.be/nl/node/2133); [Accountable, OSS regeling](https://www.accountable.eu/nl/blog/oss-regeling-zelfstandige/). Legal basis arts. 369a–369k VAT Directive (legal text, not fetched).
- Services with significant human input (e.g. consulting by video call, custom software) are not "electronically supplied services". Automated downloads of templates/PDFs are the standard example of ESS — [Taxually](https://www.taxually.com/blog/when-and-where-to-charge-eu-vat-on-digital-services); definition in art. 7 and Annex I Implementing Regulation 282/2011 (legal text, not fetched) — [EUR-Lex 32011R0282](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2011/282/oj).

**Non-EU (US) consumers**
- Under art. 58 the place of supply of B2C e-services to a US-resident consumer is the US, i.e. outside the territorial scope of EU (and Belgian) VAT. This follows from the art. 58 rule cited above. I found no Belgian official page stating it explicitly in search extracts, so confidence is medium-high (well-established rule, not re-read).
- Physical goods exported from the EU to non-EU customers are exempt exports (art. 146 VAT Directive) (legal text, not fetched). POD goods printed and shipped inside the US never enter the EU VAT system as a supply by the Belgian seller (inference).

**Marketplaces and merchants of record (MoR)**
- Art. 9a Implementing Regulation 282/2011: where electronic services are supplied through a telecom network, interface or portal such as an app marketplace, the intermediary is presumed to act in its own name, i.e. to be the supplier to the consumer. The presumption can only be rebutted where the underlying provider is explicitly identified as supplier in the contractual and billing arrangements. The CJEU upheld its validity in C-695/20 Fenix International (2023) — [DLA Piper on Fenix](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2023/03/validity-of-article-9a-of-council-implementing-regulation-282-2011-confirmed); [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/european-union-the-court-of-justice-issues-judgment-on-validity-of-deemed-reseller-rules-for-online-platforms-facilitating-electronic-supplied-services); [legislation.gov.uk art. 9a](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/282/article/9a/data.pdf). Also referenced: CJEU C-101/24 Xyrality (Oct 2025) — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/10/eu-cjeu-confirms-electronic-marketplaces-undisclosed-agents-germany.html).
- Gumroad has been merchant of record since January 2025. It is the legal seller and calculates, collects and remits VAT/GST/sales tax. Sources conflict on US sales tax coverage (one says it has also collected US/Canadian sales tax since July 2026). Fee: 10% + $0.50 direct, 30% on Discover sales — [wearefounders.uk, Gumroad fees 2026](https://www.wearefounders.uk/gumroad-fees-2026-what-sellers-actually-pay-per-sale/); [topbubbleindex, Gumroad taxes](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/gumroad-taxes/); [cartmango, Gumroad review](https://cartmango.com/gumroad-review/).
- Paddle, Lemon Squeezy: about 5% + $0.50, Lemon Squeezy plus 1.5% international. Lemon Squeezy is now part of Stripe and twice-monthly payouts are reported — [dupple.com, cheapest MoR platforms 2026](https://dupple.com/learn/cheapest-merchant-of-record-platforms); [payhip, Paddle alternatives](https://payhip.com/blog/paddle-alternatives/). Stripe Managed Payments (Stripe's own MoR) added Checkout Sessions support on 22 Apr 2026 per the Stripe changelog. Third parties conflict on whether it is GA or invite-only, and add-on pricing is cited at ~3.5% on top of normal fees — [Stripe changelog 2026-04-22](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/dahlia/2026-04-22/managed-payments); [Stripe roadmap](https://stripe.com/roadmap.md); [dodopayments (competitor)](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-managed-payments-fees-explained).
- Etsy: $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee (on item price incl. shipping), plus country-specific payment processing. Etsy is a registered marketplace facilitator in most US states and collects/remits US sales tax — [selfemployed.com, Etsy 2026](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/etsy-seller-ai-disclosure-fees-2026/); [Printify help, sales tax via sales channel](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/4483608076945-How-do-I-set-up-sales-tax-through-my-sales-channel). For digital downloads sold to EU consumers via Etsy, Etsy acts as deemed supplier under art. 9a / art. 14a (inference consistent with the above; not confirmed from an Etsy page this session).

**What remains for the Belgian seller when using an MoR (my analysis, medium confidence; accountant confirmation needed)**
- Your supply is then a B2B service (licence/resale) to the platform. The place of supply is the platform's establishment (art. 44 VAT Directive general B2B rule; legal text, not fetched). If the MoR is in the EU (e.g. Paddle's EU entity, if any), the service is reverse-charged there and the Belgian seller would normally list it in the intra-EU listing of services (intracommunautaire opgave). If the MoR is in the US/UK (Gumroad Inc., Lemon Squeezy/Stripe), it is outside the scope of Belgian VAT. Neither case is Belgian-taxable turnover, so it should not use up the €25,000 Belgian threshold. That last point is ambiguous per the Accountable extract above; confirm it.
- Purchases of foreign B2B services (Meta/TikTok/Google ads from Ireland, Canva, Shopify, MoR fees): an exempt small enterprise generally has to self-assess Belgian VAT (21%) under reverse charge and file a special VAT return for that quarter. Search extracts only support this indirectly (a 2019 forum answer and a Domus Medica guide on exempt entities buying abroad) — [juridischforum.be](https://juridischforum.be/viewtopic.php?p=611293); [Domus Medica wegwijzer kleine ondernemingsregeling (PDF)](https://domusmedica.be/sites/default/files/Wegwijzer_DomusMedica_kleineondernemingsregel_digitaal.pdf). Confidence medium. This is a real recurring admin item and a 21% cost on ad spend.

**DAC7 platform reporting**
- Platform operators report sellers to FOD Financiën (MyMinfin e-service DPI-DAC7) unless the seller has fewer than 30 sales and no more than €2,000 in consideration in the reporting year. The deadline is 31 January of the following year. The data are used for risk analysis and exchanged internationally — [news.belgium.be (28 Dec 2023)](https://news.belgium.be/nl/node/35809/pdf); [FOD Financiën nota DPI/MyMinfin (PDF)](https://financien.belgium.be/sites/default/files/downloads/169-dpi-nota-myminfin.pdf); [FOD Financiën list of jurisdictions (PDF)](https://financien.belgium.be/sites/default/files/downloads/169-lijst-rechtsgebieden-onderworpen-rapportering-2023-2024.pdf). Legal basis Council Directive (EU) 2021/514 (DAC7) (legal text, not fetched) — [EUR-Lex 32021L0514](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2021/514/oj).
- Being reported does not itself create a tax liability — [legalnews.be](https://legalnews.be/handel-consument/verkopen-via-een-online-platform-opgelet-de-fiscus-kijkt-mee-everest-advocaten/). US platforms (Gumroad, Etsy) report under DAC7 for EU-resident sellers in so far as they are in scope as "platform operators" in or reporting to an EU state (inference; Etsy is known to report, Gumroad not verified).

### Inferences
- Simplest VAT path for digital products: sell through an MoR. Consumer-side EU VAT, UK VAT and US sales tax then all move to the MoR. The Belgian seller still needs a VAT number with the exemption (for reverse-charge purchases and possibly the services listing). There is no OSS and no per-country tracking.
- With own Stripe/Shopify checkout under the Belgian exemption: Belgian/EU consumers are invoiced without VAT (exemption notice on invoice) while EU-wide B2C e-sales stay ≤ €10,000. US consumers are invoiced without VAT (outside scope). The seller must track EU cross-border B2C totals and switch to OSS + destination VAT once €10,000 is crossed. They must also watch other countries' digital-VAT/GST rules (UK, Norway, Switzerland, Australia etc. tax foreign digital suppliers from the first sale or above low thresholds; not researched in detail this session). That last risk is why an MoR is attractive.

### Gaps
- No official FOD Financiën page was retrieved that explicitly states how cross-border B2B services (to an MoR) and out-of-scope B2C services count toward the €25,000 threshold.
- Exact current Belgian procedure and form for the exempt small enterprise's special return on reverse-charged purchases: not confirmed from an official source.
- Whether Paddle and Lemon Squeezy currently onboard individual Belgian sole traders, and current Lemon Squeezy signup status: not confirmed.
- Non-EU digital-VAT/GST regimes (UK, CH, NO, AU, etc.) for own-checkout sellers: not researched.

## 3. US side: federal income tax, state sales tax, W-8BEN, withholding, treaty, import duties after the end of de minimis

### Takeaway
A Belgian individual with no US office, employees or inventory generally has no US federal income tax on business profits. Platforms request a W-8BEN to document foreign status. Treaty benefits matter mainly for US-source royalties (0% under the Belgium–US treaty) and to avoid 24–30% default withholding. US state sales tax economic nexus (mostly $100,000 of sales in a state; 200-transaction tests are being dropped) is far above micro-scale. Marketplace facilitators (Etsy, MoRs) collect anyway. For physical goods, the US de minimis exemption has been suspended for all countries since 29 Aug 2025, with statutory repeal from 1 July 2027. POD printed and shipped inside the US avoids import duties for the seller. Goods shipped from the EU/China now attract duties on every parcel.

## 4. Consumer and product law (EU + US), and financial-content caution

### Takeaway
Selling to EU consumers, including from Belgium to Belgians, brings full EU consumer law into play. You must give pre-contract information and confirm the contract. There is a 14-day withdrawal right, which you can switch off for downloads only through an express-consent plus acknowledgement checkbox and a confirmation. A 2-year legal guarantee for conformity applies to digital content. For physical goods, GPSR applies: a seller who sells POD items under their own brand is probably the "manufacturer", needs an EU-based responsible person and must show product-safety info in listings. The new Product Liability Directive applies to products placed on the market from 9 Dec 2026 and expressly covers software and digital manufacturing files. In the US the practical rules are the FTC Act (no deceptive claims), the Endorsement Guides, the fake-reviews rule (16 CFR 465, in force since Oct 2024), CAN-SPAM, COPPA and ROSCA for subscriptions; click-to-cancel was vacated in July 2025 and is being re-proposed. Finance content: personalised buy/sell recommendations on financial instruments are regulated investment advice (FSMA licence). ESMA says disclaimers do not protect finfluencers.

### Inferences

## 5. Advertising and platform rules: commercial-content disclosure, Belgian influencer rules, DSA, AI-content labelling

### Takeaway
Your own product promotions and affiliate links are commercial communications. They must be recognisable as advertising, and Belgian FOD Economie expects creators who regularly earn money to show their enterprise number, address and e-mail on their profile or website. Since 2024 the address may be given via a link to a website. EU AI Act art. 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026. The May 2026 Digital Omnibus deal delayed only the art. 50(2) machine-readable marking for systems already on the market (to 2 Dec 2026). The deployer's duty to disclose deepfakes (art. 50(4)) hits realistic depictions of real people, objects or places, not obviously stylised motion graphics. Platforms require labels for realistic synthetic content. A cloned or realistic synthetic voice that could be mistaken for a real person is the most likely trigger. Plainly animated explainer graphics with a generic TTS voice are generally outside mandatory labels, though voluntary labelling is cheap insurance.

### Cited Findings
- FOD Economie: influencers who regularly make advertising are enterprises and must show enterprise number, address and e-mail on website/social media. In 2024 the interpretation was relaxed: the address may be given via a reference to the website, and an approved business centre may serve as the address — [VRT NWS 2 Jan 2024](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2024/01/02/influencers-hoeven-op-hun-profiel-niet-meer-te-melden-waar-ze-wo/); [Forum for the Future, verplichte identificatiegegevens](https://blog.forumforthefuture.be/nl/article/bedrijfswebsite-en-accounts-op-sociale-media-verplichte-identificatiegegevens-/19596); [VRT NWS 2022](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2022/08/05/influencers-vs-fod-economie/).

## 6. Intellectual property: assets, music, fonts, trademarks, AI-assisted designs, marketplace AI policies

### Takeaway
Business/commercial accounts on TikTok and Instagram are limited to the platforms' commercial music libraries. Trending copyrighted tracks in promotional posts risk muting or takedown. Use CML/Sound Collection or licensed libraries. Stock assets and fonts must have licences that allow commercial use and, for POD/templates, "merchandise/redistribution" use. Desktop font licences often do not allow embedding in sellable templates. In the US (USCO Part 2, Jan 2025), prompts alone do not create authorship. Only human-selected, arranged or modified elements are protectable. The EU also requires human authorship. So purely AI-generated designs are hard to protect and easy to copy. Etsy allows AI-assisted items with disclosure ("designed by"). Since 22 Oct 2026 it bans AI-generated functional guides/patterns/recipes and fully automated listings.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok business accounts see only the Commercial Music Library in-app for promotional content, including organic posts. Instagram business accounts are restricted to Meta Sound Collection or similar. Personal accounts posting sponsored/promotional content can face the same restriction. Consequences include muted audio, removed posts and flagged accounts — [Soundstripe, TikTok music licensing rules](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-licensing-rules); [Epidemic Sound, brands using music on social media](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/brands-using-music-on-social-media/); [Planoly, commercially licensed vs trending sounds](https://planoly.com/blog/commercially-licensed-vs-trending-sounds-what-can-brands-use); [Illinois Lawyer Now (Jan 2025)](https://www.illinoislawyernow.com/2025/01/riffs-of-risk-are-you-using-a-personal-account-for-your-business-on-social-media/). The official TikTok/Meta help text was not retrieved.
- USCO "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability" (29 Jan 2025): human authorship is still required. Prompts alone do not give sufficient control. Protection is possible for perceptible human-authored inputs and for creative selection, coordination, arrangement or modification of outputs. Existing law is sufficient. Most jurisdictions, including the EU, require human authorship — [USCO NewsNet 1060](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html); [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/copyright-office-releases-new-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-works); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/). EU: originality = "author's own intellectual creation" (CJEU C-5/08 Infopaq; C-145/10 Painer) (case law, not fetched).
- Etsy: the July 2024 seller policy introduced the "made by / designed by / sourced by / handpicked by" labels. AI-assisted items fall under "designed by", and selling AI prompts is not allowed — [TechCrunch 9 Jul 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/etsy-new-seller-policy-2024-generative-ai). Update of 22 Oct 2026: AI creations remain allowed with meaningful creative input and disclosure. A new rule requires an unedited real-item photo, with exceptions for some POD/digital/custom items. AI-generated functional guides (craft patterns, recipes, instructions) are banned, as are fully automated/bot listings — [Digital Commerce 360 (26 Oct 2026)](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/10/26/etsy-ai-policy/amp/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/etsy-forces-sellers-to-show-one-unaltered-photo-in-every-listing/); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=320422); [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-expands-ai-training-rights/). Etsy's own policy text was not retrieved. Whether "AI-generated instructions" captures AI-written PDF guides or planners is a material question for template sellers.

### Inferences
- Trademark pitfalls (general knowledge, not researched this session): brand names or logos in listing titles or designs ("Notion template", "Cricut", "Disney-style", sports teams, song lyrics) are the commonest cause of Etsy/Redbubble takedowns and account strikes. Check designs and slogans against the EUIPO/USPTO registers (TMview) before listing.
- Value protection for AI-assisted templates rests more on brand, speed and bundle design than on copyright. Human-authored structure (formulas, layout choices, written instructions) is the protectable part.

### Gaps
- Font licensing specifics (Google Fonts OFL allows commercial embedding; many commercial desktop licences restrict template redistribution) were not researched this session.
- The YouTube Audio Library / Content ID position for business channels was not researched.
- Official EU Commission/EUIPO statements on AI-output copyrightability (2025–2026) were not retrieved.

## 7. Payments, payouts, chargebacks and privacy

### Takeaway
A Belgian sole trader can use Stripe (EU cards about 1.5% + €0.25, international cards about 3.25% + €0.25, plus about 2% FX if conversion is needed), Mollie (similar card rates) or PayPal. MoRs charge 5–10% + $0.50 and also absorb tax compliance and some chargeback handling. A multi-currency account (e.g. Wise) avoids double FX. Chargebacks on digital goods cost about $15 per dispute on Stripe (euro amount unconfirmed), and a counter fee is now possible. Keep download/access logs. For an email list: GDPR (legal basis, privacy notice, double opt-in advisable) and Belgian cookie consent for non-essential cookies. CCPA only applies above $26.625m revenue or 100,000 California consumers, so it is irrelevant at this scale.

## 8. Synthesis: comparison of four set-ups for a Belgian resident (my synthesis; based on the citations in sections 1–7)

### Takeaway
Simplest legal first experiment: a digital product (templates/PDF) sold through a merchant-of-record platform (Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy/Stripe Managed Payments, Paddle, Payhip-as-MoR) or Etsy. It needs the same Belgian base registration as the others (KBO number, social fund, VAT number under the exemption) but removes consumer-side VAT/sales-tax compliance, US nexus and most product law. Affiliate-only is the lowest in money and product law but brings the heaviest disclosure obligations and needs W-8BEN/treaty paperwork for US programmes. POD/US dropship carries the most obligations (GPSR, PLD from 9 Dec 2026, tariffs/duties, returns, sales tax on Printful charges) and the least margin.

### Inferences
- Recommended first experiment: (1) digital product via MoR or Etsy. Register in bijberoep or as student-zelfstandige, with the VAT exemption, and keep net income below the zero-contribution threshold during the test. Budget impact: €111.50 plus optional VAT-activation help, well within €500. Professional confirmation is needed on: (a) eligibility for bijberoep/student status; (b) whether an exempt small enterprise must file special returns on Meta/TikTok ad purchases and the intra-EU services listing for MoR supplies; (c) income-tax classification (winst vs baten) and deductible costs.
- If the reader is neither employed nor a student, main-occupation minimum contributions (~€890/quarter) make every set-up exceed the budget quickly. In that case only a genuinely one-off sale declared as diverse inkomsten is cheap, and it carries a requalification risk. Confirm with the tax office (Unizo advice).
