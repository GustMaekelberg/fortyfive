## 1. Sourcing prices and MOQs (China: Alibaba / Made-in-China / AliExpress; private label; documentation)

### Takeaway
Yellow/polarized "night driving" glasses are a pure commodity at roughly $0.40–$2.30 (€0.35–€2.00) per piece with MOQs as low as 12–300, and TR90 photochromic cycling glasses with a custom logo cost roughly $3.40–$6.00 (€2.90–€5.15) FOB at 100–300 pieces. For a universal clip-on helmet brim/visor no reliable wholesale price was found: listings are dominated by whole helmets and brand-specific visors, which itself indicates that visor fitment is largely proprietary. A factory "CE certificate" is weak evidence; only an accredited-lab report for the exact model counts, and the seller who brands the product carries the legal responsibility.

**CE documentation and its reliability**
- "A CE certificate can easily be forged. Even if it was issued based on test reports of a legitimate agency, it does not necessarily guarantee the quality of products" — [ExamineChina: CE certificate verification](https://www.examinechina.com/ce-certificate-china/)
- A test report from an accredited laboratory for the specific model has real value; a stand-alone factory "CE certificate" is rated low value and is often not backed by testing — [GPSRready: Chinese supplier certificates](https://gpsrready.pl/en/blog/certyfikaty-chinskie-a-gpsr)
- Documented fraud patterns: a testing laboratory that did not exist (found by customs), report templates copied from a certification body's public sample, QR codes printed on forged SGS reports; red flags are a missing report number, no way to verify with the lab, or a different model in the report than the one bought — [Leeline: avoid fake certifications](https://www.leelinegroup.com/avoid-fake-certifications/); [Seminglight: fake CE seized at customs](https://seminglight.com/uv-lash-light-certification-customs/); [ChinaISPP: verifying supplier certifications](https://www.chinaispp.com/how-to-verify-if-a-chinese-supplier-has-genuine-product-certifications/)

- Legal position: the brand that sells the pair is treated as the manufacturer and signs the declaration of conformity; a CE mark printed by the factory does not transfer that responsibility — [Commercive: selling sunglasses from China (2026)](https://www.commercive.co/blogs/sunglasses-from-china)
- EN ISO 12312-1 covers afocal sunglasses and clip-ons for general use; an amendment (EN ISO 12312-1:2022/A1) is in draft, so labs may test against different versions — [iTeh: EN ISO 12312-1:2022](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/055a84f1-0a40-4b5c-ac83-f87b9f3e4d37/en-iso-12312-1-2022); [iTeh: prA1](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/9fe885f9-e8f2-4185-b0a0-b06265c7a327/en-iso-12312-1-2022-pra1)

### Inferences
- Product (1) has no sourcing barrier whatsoever: MOQ 3–50 pieces at under €1–2 means every competitor, marketplace seller and discount chain has the same product. Purchase price is irrelevant to the P&L; marketing and trust are the whole cost base.
- Product (2) at 300 pieces with logo and box implies a first order of roughly €1,500–3,000 FOB including branding (300–400 × €4–7), which is within a small budget. Anti-reflective + hydrophobic + anti-fog coatings were not priced in any extract; expect a surcharge over the $3.40–6.00 base (assumption: +$1–3 per piece).
- Product (3): the absence of a clear commodity "universal bicycle-helmet brim" on Alibaba, plus model-specific spare visors from Abus, R2B and Pro Sport Lights, suggests a universal visor is either a textile/foam brim (Da Brim style, not meant for high speed) or needs own design work. That is both the opportunity (less commoditised) and the cost (development, fit, liability).
- Da Brim's own "not for high-speed/performance cycling" caveat is a warning for a 45 km/h speed-pedelec use case: wind load and helmet-certification implications need separate technical/regulatory review.

- No AliExpress consumer prices for Rockbros, Scvcn or West Biking glasses, nor for universal clip-on helmet brims, were retrieved; Kapvoe prices are US retail only.

- Whether suppliers can supply a genuine, model-specific EN ISO 12312-1 report on request was not tested; sources only say this must be requested and verified with the issuing lab.

## 2. European alternatives (EU dropship suppliers, wholesalers) versus China direct

### Takeaway
EU-based dropship platforms remove customs friction and cut delivery to roughly 3–7 days, but add subscription costs (BigBuy roughly €69–119/month plus a registration fee) and offer the same generic products at higher unit prices. No EU white-label eyewear supplier or Benelux cycling-accessory wholesaler prices could be retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- BigBuy (Spain): monthly plan roughly **€69–99** plus a one-time registration fee of about **€89–90**; one pricing summary lists the Ecommerce Pack at **€89/month** and Marketplaces Pack at **€119/month**; annual billing about 20% cheaper; a B2B pack at €0/month — [Trendtrack: BigBuy review](https://www.trendtrack.io/blog-post/bigbuy-review); [Copyfy: BigBuy review](https://www.copyfy.io/en/blog/bigbuy-review); [AliDropship: is BigBuy legit (2026)](https://alidropship.com/is-bigbuy-legit/)
- CJdropshipping has no subscription fee (pay product + shipping per order); EU-warehouse orders reach Belgium in **3–7 days** according to supplier-side guides, while another source reports **7–15 business days** for European orders and sellers report **10–25 days** on China-sourced parcels — [NicheDropshipping: suppliers in Belgium](https://nichedropshipping.com/dropshipping-suppliers-in-belgium/); [Zoloo: EU dropshippers 2026](https://zoloo.co/en/blog/dropship-leveranciers-europa); [DailyFulfill: CJ shipping times, promise vs reality (2026)](https://www.dailyfulfill.com/cj-dropshipping-shipping-times-promise-vs-reality-2026/)
- Parcels shipped from outside the EU carry the risk that the customer is charged import VAT on delivery; EU-warehouse stock avoids this — [CJdropshipping: dropshipping Netherlands](https://cjdropshipping.com/blogs/dropshipping-knowledge/Dropshipping-Netherlands)

### Inferences
- With the €3-per-item customs duty on China-direct consumer parcels since 1 July 2026 (see section 3), EU-warehouse or own stock is now clearly preferable for a €20–25 product: €3 is 15% of the net selling price.
- EU dropship catalogues contain the same unbranded goods as Alibaba; they solve logistics, not differentiation or documentation.

### Gaps
- No BigBuy/Spocket/Syncee catalogue prices for cycling glasses or night glasses were found.
- No CJdropshipping per-order shipping fee to Belgium/Netherlands was found.
- Dutch/Belgian cycling-accessory wholesalers that supply bike shops (and their price lists or dealer conditions) were not researched; search budget ran out.
- EU-based white-label eyewear manufacturers were not researched.

## 3. Landed cost: freight, duty, import VAT, 2026 low-value parcel rules, lab testing

### Takeaway
For a bulk order of a few hundred glasses, freight and duty add only about €1–2 per unit; the decisive 2026 change is the flat **€3 customs duty per item** on low-value consumer parcels from outside the EU (in force since 1 July 2026), which damages China-direct dropshipping of cheap items. Independent lab testing is quoted at roughly $200–$1,000+ per test/report, a meaningful fixed cost on a 300–500 unit run.

### Cited Findings
- **EU €3 duty**: since **1 July 2026** the EU no longer exempts e-commerce parcels under €150 from duty and charges **€3 per item** on goods shipped directly to consumers from non-EU countries — [Anadolu Agency, 1 July 2026](https://aa.com.tr/en/europe/eu-ends-duty-free-treatment-for-low-value-e-commerce-imports/3983325); [European Commission news, 29 June 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ensuring-fairness-and-safety-eur3-customs-duty-low-value-parcels-2026-06-29_en); [Cross-Border Magazine, 9 July 2026](https://cross-border-magazine.com/eus-e3-fee-on-low-value-is-now-in-force/)
- Legal basis and duration: Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382, agreed 11 February 2026; temporary measure applying **1 July 2026 – 1 July 2028** — [MHA customs update](https://www.mha.co.uk/insights/customs-update-3-duty-on-low-value-e-commerce-imports); [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/eu/eu-e3-levy-low-value-e-commerce-import-package-july-2026/)
- Counting and scope are described inconsistently: "per item category in each shipment" / "per item type by customs code"; some guidance says it initially applies to goods sold by non-EU traders registered in IOSS, with a possible extension to all low-value imports; a separate EU-wide handling fee is still under negotiation — [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/eu/eu-e3-levy-low-value-e-commerce-import-package-july-2026/); [4Seller explainer](https://www.4seller.com/blog/en/article/331-New-EU-3-Customs-Duty-for-Low-Value-E-Commerce-Parcels-in-2026-What-European-Sellers-Need-to-Know); [ExamineChina](https://www.examinechina.com/3-customs-duty-on-small-parcels-in-the-eu/)
- **Freight China → Belgium (forwarder marketing pages, 2026)**: general air freight **$3.85–11.00/kg**; express courier **$4.80–7.50/kg**, "cost-effective only under roughly 100 kg" — [Gerudo Logistics, April 2026](https://www.gerudologistics.com/shipping-from-china-to-belgium)
- Transifly (August 2026 anchors): air ≈ **$4.90/kg**, express ≈ **$7.93/kg**, **air DDP $7.50/kg with 7–9 days transit**, sea LCL ≈ **$41/CBM**, sea DDP **$1.90/kg** — [Transifly: China to Belgium](https://www.transifly.com/shipping-from-china-to-belgium/)
- Sino-Shipping (October 2026): air **$3.95/kg only for 1,000 kg+**; express **$5–10/kg**; LCL about **$35/CBM**; Belgium applies customs duty plus **21% VAT** — [Sino-Shipping: China–Belgium](https://www.sino-shipping.com/freight-china-belgium/)
- Basenton: **air DDP $5.5–12/kg**; **sea DDP $220–380/CBM** (conflicts by an order of magnitude with other sea figures; quotes needed) — [Basenton: shipping costs China to Belgium](https://www.basenton.com/shipping-costs-from-china-to-belgium/)
- **Lab testing**: independent testing for UV protection, polarization, transmittance and mechanical durability costs **$200–$1,000+ per test/report (€170–860+)**; a full series "ranges from a few hundred to several thousand USD" depending on scope (sourcing-company figures, not lab price lists) — [Karusonco: CE & FDA testing guide](https://www.karusonco.com/guides/eyewear-factory-ce-fda-testing-guide/); [Karusonco: vetting an OEM sunglasses manufacturer](https://www.karusonco.com/guides/vet-oem-sunglasses-manufacturer/)
- Typical accredited-lab scope for sunglasses includes lens mechanical strength, lens surface quality and nickel release of the frame (EN 16128) — [QIMA: sunglasses testing](https://www.qima.com/consumer-products/lab-testing/sunglasses)
- If the factory arranges the lab, the buyer loses independence; guides advise commissioning the test and sending samples yourself, and requesting spectral transmittance curves — [Karusonco: vetting guide](https://www.karusonco.com/guides/vet-oem-sunglasses-manufacturer/)

### Inferences
- Bulk order of 400 glasses in cases weighs about 50–60 kg (assumption: 120–150 g per boxed unit). At air DDP $5.5–12/kg this is roughly $300–700, i.e. **€0.65–1.50 per unit**. Sea freight is pointless at this volume.
- For a VAT-registered seller, 21% import VAT is a cash-flow item (recoverable), not a cost; it must still be pre-financed on the first shipment (about €500–900 on a €2,500–4,300 consignment).
- China-direct dropshipping of a €2 product now carries **€3 duty + shipping + VAT**, so the landed cost per order is driven by fixed per-parcel charges, not by the product.
- One independent transmittance/EN ISO 12312-1 report at roughly €200–900 adds €0.50–2.25 per unit on a 400-unit run, but is the only way to make honest, defensible claims (and is itself a differentiator).

### Gaps
- **EU duty rates could not be verified** in this session (search returned only US tariff pages). Researcher's unverified background knowledge: sunglasses and other spectacles under HS 9004.10 / 9004.90 carry about **2.9%** third-country duty, and safety headgear and headgear parts (HS 6506.10 / 6507) about **2.7%**. Verify in TARIC before use. The worked examples use these as labelled assumptions; the amounts are immaterial (under €0.30 per unit).
- Customs clearance / broker / courier disbursement fees for a commercial import into Belgium were not found (assumption used in examples: €50–150 per shipment).
- Exact scope of the €3 duty (per item vs per tariff line; IOSS-only or all parcels) was not confirmed against the regulation text.
- No price list from an accredited lab (SGS, Intertek, TÜV, a Benelux optical lab) for a transmittance-only measurement was found; a spectral transmittance measurement alone may be much cheaper than a full EN ISO 12312-1 series (not verified).
- Packaging/EPR obligations (Fost Plus in Belgium, Verpact in the Netherlands) and their thresholds were not researched.

## 4. Shopify cost structure in Belgium (2026) and alternative channels

### Takeaway
Shopify itself is cheap relative to everything else: Basic is reported at about €32/month (monthly) or €24/month (annual), and Shopify Payments is available in Belgium with Bancontact at a flat €0.39 and iDEAL at €0.29 per transaction. Marketplaces (bol, Amazon, Decathlon) cost roughly 10–18% commission plus fixed fees, which for a €25–50 product is often cheaper than a paid-social acquisition cost, but they expose the product to direct price comparison.

## 6. Customer acquisition: paid benchmarks, policy limits, cheaper channels

### Takeaway
Benchmarks imply a realistic paid-social cost per first order of about €20–40 for a cold, unknown single-product store (Dutch average CPC €0.58 at a 1.4–2.7% conversion rate; large-panel Meta median CPA about $39), which is equal to or above the entire net selling price of a €25 product. Ad platforms and regulators also restrict exactly the claims that make night-glasses ads convert ("see better at night", safety, before/after), so the honest version of the product is harder to advertise profitably.

A planning range of **€12 (optimistic) / €20–25 (realistic) / €35–40 (pessimistic)** per first order is consistent with the low-ticket benchmark bands above.
- Rule check: CPA at no more than 30–40% of AOV means **€7.50–10 on a €24.95 order** and **€15–20 on a €49.95 order**. The first is below almost every benchmark; the second is achievable only with above-median execution.
- Break-even ROAS for the worked examples (section 8) is about 2.0–2.8×, against a large-panel median of 1.88×: the median advertiser loses money on first orders at these price points and depends on repeat purchase, which a pair of glasses or a visor barely has.
- The honest positioning (clear/photochromic category 0–1 lens, "does not improve night vision, reduces wind/tearing and stray reflections") avoids the FTC-type claim risk but removes the emotional hook that sells night glasses; expect lower click-through than competitors who over-claim.
- Marketplaces and Google Shopping capture existing demand ("fietsbril helder", "nachtbril fiets") at CPCs of about €0.35 and higher conversion, which fits a functional product better than interruption ads on Meta/TikTok.
- A consumer fair stand (hardware €290–1,100 plus floor rent, travel and time) needs dozens of direct sales to pay back; realistic value is feedback and dealer contacts rather than profit.

## 7. Dropshipping reality check

### Takeaway
Published failure rates for dropshipping stores (80–90% in year one; 1–5% reaching real net profit) come from industry blogs rather than audited data, but they all point the same way, and the arithmetic in section 8 explains why. For night-driving glasses specifically, the retail price transparency is brutal: Action sells yellow photochromic "night glasses" for €1.99 and bol.com lists them from €9.95.

### Cited Findings

## 9. Differentiation and defensibility

### Takeaway
Reselling stock designs gives no defensibility: suppliers offer the same frames to anyone at MOQ 10–300. Real differentiation is affordable only in "soft" forms (independent test data, honest positioning, speed-pedelec niche focus, dealer and lease relationships, bundles); "hard" product development is feasible for a simple injection-moulded visor at roughly $1,000–15,000 tooling, but an own eyewear frame needs 600–1,200 pairs per model.

### Cited Findings
- Stock models with logo from **100–300 pieces**; own frame design (ODM) typically **600–1,200 pairs per model** — [Lansee photochromic category](https://www.lanseeoptic.com/product_category/photochromic-sunglasses/); [JH Eyewear](https://www.jheyewear.com/product/tr90-polarized-cycling-sunglasses-oem/)
- Injection-mould tooling in China: simple prototype single-cavity mould **$1,000–3,000 (€860–2,580)**; vendor ranges up to **$6,000**; steel production / multi-cavity tooling **$5,000–15,000+ (€4,300–12,900+)** — [Haizol: China injection molding industry report 2026](https://www.haizol.com/news/china-injection-molding-industry-report-2026); [ZetarMold: injection mold price list 2026](https://zetarmold.com/?p=52613); [RapidDirect: injection molding quote breakdown](https://www.rapiddirect.com/blog/injection-molding-quote-cost-breakdown/)
- Low-volume moulding runs of **50–200 units** are accepted by some shops — [Haizol: low-volume injection molding](https://www.haizol.com/blog/low-volume-injection-molding)
- Independent accredited test reports are described as the document with real evidential value, unlike factory certificates — [GPSRready](https://gpsrready.pl/en/blog/certyfikaty-chinskie-a-gpsr)
- Regulators have acted against unsubstantiated night-driving claims (FTC, NightSafe) — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/39519)
- Existing universal brim (Da Brim) sells at $43–56 and explicitly excludes high-speed use; speed-pedelec helmet brands sell model-specific visors — [Da Brim](https://dabrim.com/); [ANWB: speed pedelec vizier](https://www.anwb.nl/webwinkel/p/194497/speed-pedelec-fietshelm-vizier)
- Lease contracts let riders add accessories at the start, and remaining maintenance budget (€300–1,200 at Cyclis) can be spent on accessories at the dealer — [o2o](https://www.o2o.be/en/blog/hoe-werkt-fietsleasing); [Cyclis](https://www.cyclis.be/en/inspiratie/a-complete-guide-to-the-cyclis-bike-lease-maintenance-budget/)

### Inferences
- Cheapest credible differentiator: commission one independent spectral-transmittance / EN ISO 12312-1 report (about €200–900) and publish it. Almost no competitor in the €10–30 night-glasses segment does this, and it converts the regulatory weakness of the category into the brand's selling point.
- A niche position ("commuter glasses for speed-pedelec riders at 45 km/h: wind, tearing, rain, dusk") justifies €45–60 where a generic "night glasses" claim supports €10–25, and the examples show only the €50–60 range carries acquisition costs.
- Dealer and lease channels are slower but structurally better: accessories are sold at the moment a €4,000–7,000 speed pedelec is leased, paid from a budget rather than out of pocket, with zero ad cost.
- A custom visor is the only one of the three with potential product-level defensibility (design registration, fit to popular helmet models), but it adds €1,000–13,000 tooling, CAD/prototyping, and safety questions (attachment to a certified helmet, behaviour at 45 km/h) that a first-time solo founder must resolve before selling.
- Bundles (glasses + reflective item or light, or two lenses: clear and photochromic) raise order value toward the €50–60 range where the model starts to work, without new product development.

### Gaps
- No sourced cost for industrial design/CAD, prototyping (3D printing), or design registration.
- Whether attaching an aftermarket visor affects helmet certification (EN 1078 / NTA 8776) or the helmet maker's warranty was not researched here (regulatory scope of another researcher).
- No evidence was found on the actual size of the speed-pedelec commuter niche or its willingness to pay; no competitor sales data.
- No data on bike-lease companies' supplier onboarding requirements.
