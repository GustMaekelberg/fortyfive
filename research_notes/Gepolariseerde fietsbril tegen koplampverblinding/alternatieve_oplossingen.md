## 1. Physical shading of the light source (helmet peak, cap under helmet, clip-on brim, shading strip on lens/visor)

### Takeaway
Blocking the headlamp itself with an opaque edge (cap peak or helmet visor, used by dipping the head) is the most consistently recommended trick in cyclist and motorcyclist communities and is physically the only approach that removes the glare source without dimming the rest of the scene — but the evidence is 100% anecdotal; no test, study or official body endorsement was found. No product was found that is specifically marketed as an anti-headlight-glare brim/shield for cyclists: this is an unserved niche, but the honest claim is narrow ("lets you shade oncoming lights by dipping your head"), not "eliminates glare".

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE] CycleChat thread "Glasses to reduce glare of headlights" (c. 2009): "I always wear a baseball cap when cycling at night so I can dip my head and use the peak of the cap to block any very bright lights"; another poster suggests a peaked cap or peaked helmet for the same purpose — [CycleChat](https://www.cyclechat.net/threads/glasses-to-reduce-glare-of-headlights.39961/)
- [ANECDOTE] Bike Arlington forum "Night Blindness": posters say a brimmed cap or a helmet visor blocks much of the oncoming glare; one suggests "ducking your head just a touch" with a small cap brim, and adding clear UV-protective lenses "cuts a little more glare"; another advises having vision checked for astigmatism/cataract if glare is a struggle — [Bike Arlington Forum](https://www.bikearlingtonforum.com/forums/topic/night-blindness/page/2)
- [ANECDOTE] Bike Forums "Headlight Washout" (commuting sub-forum, multi-page thread): riders report (a) using a cap under the helmet to shade the eyes from car headlights when a car comes the other way, (b) tilting the head so the helmet visor blocks the glare made vision "acceptable", (c) dropping the view so the brim blocks the lights and looking up again once the car has passed, (d) a helmet with a visor "so you can look down at the pavement without being blinded" — [Bike Forums](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-post20777412.html); [page 2](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-2.html)
- [ANECDOTE] Same thread, worst-case description that matches the Belgian/Dutch two-way-path situation: "the bike lane or path is below the grade of the road, there is no overhead lighting, and you are on the same side of the road as oncoming traffic" — [Bike Forums](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-3-printthread.html)
- [ANECDOTE] Limits: one rider's helmet visor "could only be lowered so far before it pushed the helmet back"; caps can conflict with the helmet — advice is to wear the cap further down on the forehead so the peak is usable without the helmet pushing on it, and some caps are more helmet-friendly than others — [Bike Forums headlight washout](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-post20777375.html); [Bike Forums cycling caps](https://www.bikeforums.net/general-cycling-discussion/1151607-cycling-caps.html)
- [EXPERT/media] Ride On Magazine (Bicycle Network, Australia) quotes an expert: "Avert your eyes. Don't look at the light", and suggests changing your head height if you can — [Ride On Magazine](https://rideonmagazine.com.au/?p=8655)
- [ANECDOTE, motorcycling] A strip of electrical tape across the top of the visor is a known rider trick for riding into low sun — [Bay Area Riders Forum](https://bayarearidersforum.com/forums/goto/post?id=9238523)
- [ANECDOTE/product, motorcycling] webBikeWorld covered a commercial visor sun-strip ("Sunax Sunshield") and a reader's DIY strip of tinted film on the outside of the face shield: "a tilt of the helmet will block a lot of the glare when riding into the Sun" — [webBikeWorld](https://old.webbikeworld.com/?p=49144)
- [MARKETING] The only night-specific cycling cap found, Proviz REFLECT360, has a reflective peak that reflects headlights so drivers see the rider — it is a be-seen product, not an anti-glare product — [Proviz](https://provizsports.com/products/reflect360-cycling-cap-black-reflective); similar: [POC AVIP Reflective Cap at Sigma Sports](https://www.sigmasports.com/item/POC/AVIP-Reflective-Cycling-Cap/HXAV)
- [MARKETING] A Dutch optics retailer notes that gradient-tint lenses with a darker upper part "can help against glare" (daytime sun context) — [Edel-Optics](https://edel-optics.nl/b/welke-bril-kies-je-om-te-fietsen)
- [ANECDOTE] Side benefit in rain: a low-visored cap or helmet visor "keeps most rain off the lenses", which matters because wet lenses multiply headlight glare (see section 2) — [Bike Forums 50+](https://www.bikeforums.net/fifty-plus-50/724110-people-who-wear-glasses-what-do-you-do-when-s-raining-post12452250.html); [Bike Forums rain and glasses](https://www.bikeforums.net/showthread.php/484609-what-to-do-about-rain-and-glasses)
- [Absence] Two targeted searches (EN) for a brim/visor/shield product marketed to cyclists against oncoming headlights returned only forum threads, reflective caps and bike-light listings; no dedicated product — searches run 17 Nov 2026, e.g. results list containing [CycleChat](https://www.cyclechat.net/threads/glasses-to-reduce-glare-of-headlights.39961/) and [Bike Forums](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-post20777412.html)

### Inferences
- Geometry (researcher's reasoning, not sourced): a cyclist's eyes (~1.5–1.7 m) are above a car's low-beam lamps (~0.6–0.9 m), so the lamps sit a few degrees *below* the horizon; a brim blocks from above. Dipping the head until the brim edge covers the lamp still leaves the road surface between the rider and the car visible (the road at any distance is lower in the visual field than the lamp at that distance). That is why the trick works with an opaque brim and why it costs far-field vision (everything above the lamp, including the horizon and anything beyond the car, is hidden while the head is dipped).
- For the specific Belgian/Dutch case — a two-way path where the cyclist rides against the adjacent car lane — the lamps are to the **side** (right-hand side for the "wrong-way" cyclist) as well as low. A top brim then needs a head *turn/tilt* as well as a dip; a **side shield** (temple-side strip on glasses, or a helmet-visor side band) is geometrically the better-matched occluder. No source discusses this; it is a product idea, not a finding.
- A semi-transparent tinted strip (car-windscreen-band style) is a compromise: it attenuates instead of blocks. A dark band (e.g. 10–20% transmission) would cut lamp intensity 5–10x while leaving some see-through; this is how motorcycle sun strips are used, but no night-time evidence exists.
- Sellable? Yes in principle (clip-on helmet brim / stick-on visor strip / cap with a deep, flat-black underside peak). Defensible claim: "physically shades oncoming headlights when you dip your head; does not darken the rest of your view." Not defensible: "anti-glare", "see better at night", any safety-outcome claim. Risk to disclose: restricted upward/far vision while dipped; must not interfere with helmet fit or (for NTA 8776 helmets) certification.
- Existing supply already covers the function cheaply: any cycling cap (roughly EUR 10–25) or MTB/urban helmet with a peak does this. A product must therefore win on fit-under/onto-helmet, peak depth/angle adjustability, side coverage and rain behaviour, not on novelty of principle.

### Gaps
- No controlled test or measurement of how much a brim reduces glare illuminance at the eye or improves detection for cyclists — none found.
- No statement from Fietsersbond (BE/NL), VIAS, SWOV, ANWB, Cycling UK or British Cycling recommending a peak/cap against headlights was found.
- The Dutch-language cycling article "Wat doe jij als een autolicht je verblindt?" ([Bicycling.nl](https://www.bicycling.nl/nieuws/wat-doe-jij-als-een-autolicht-je-verblindt)) and the Fietsersbond forum thread "Verblindende koplampen" ([forum.fietsersbond.nl](https://forum.fietsersbond.nl/t/verblindende-koplampen/6453)) exist and are directly on-topic, but their content could not be retrieved (fetch blocked). They should be read before finalising.
- Reddit (r/cycling, r/bikecommuting, r/ebikes) did not surface in results; not covered.
- Whether an add-on brim affects NTA 8776 / EN 1078 conformity of a helmet was not researched.

## 2. Clear lenses with anti-reflective (AR) coating; clean, scratch-free lenses; rain, hydrophobic and anti-fog coatings

### Takeaway
AR coating has one small peer-reviewed study behind it (about 2x better contrast sensitivity under glare versus uncoated lenses, and a recommendation that spectacle-wearing drivers use AR at night), but it only removes reflections created by the lens itself — it does nothing about the headlamp's direct light, so "AR glasses against headlight glare" is defensible only as "adds less glare than uncoated lenses", mainly for people who must wear prescription glasses. Dirty, scratched or rain-spotted lenses clearly make glare worse (official and anecdotal agreement), and in heavy rain many riders are better off without glasses or with a peak that keeps drops off the lens.

### Cited Findings
- [EVIDENCE] Ross & Bradley-type study, J Am Optom Assoc 1997, "Visual performance and patient preference: a comparison of anti-reflection coated and uncoated spectacle lenses": survey of 18 patients — all noticed fewer reflections and many reported fewer glare-related visual problems; lab test of 28 patients — under normal room light, acuity and contrast sensitivity were identical for coated and uncoated lenses, but under the glare test condition contrast sensitivity was "significantly better (a factor of two)" with AR lenses; authors conclude drivers should wear AR-coated spectacle lenses at night — [PubMed 9190135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9190135/) (authorship and exact glare condition not shown in the snippet; verify)
- [EXPERT] All About Vision: AR coating "only reduces the glare caused by the eyeglass lenses themselves", so it will not help people who do not need prescription lenses; "no apparent visual benefit" to wearing non-prescription lenses with AR coating — [All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/eyeglasses/night-driving-glasses/)
- [EXPERT/trade] 20/20 Magazine (optical trade): AR reduces surface and internal lens reflections and the resulting halos, ghost images and glare when driving at night (no trial cited) — [20/20 Magazine](https://2020mag.com/article/pro-to-pro-tips-3-ways-to-improve-night-vision-with-corrective-lenses)
- [EVIDENCE, manufacturer-sponsored] An Essilor-hosted crossover, double-masked comparison of AR vs non-AR lenses shows contrast-sensitivity differences that were not statistically significant in the excerpt (p = 0.424 and 0.157) — [Essilor Pro PDF](https://essilorpro.com/content/dam/essilor-redesign/product-resources/crizal/lens_performance_in_daily_activities.pdf)
- [MARKETING] Retail pages claim AR coating "eliminates headlight glare from oncoming traffic" — an overclaim relative to the above — e.g. [Zenottic](https://www.zenottic.com/en-il/blogs/eyewear-style-guide/best-glasses-night-driving-reducing-headlight-glare)
- [OFFICIAL/EXPERT] ANWB: a dirty windscreen scatters the light of oncoming traffic "in all directions", making glare worse; cleaning is a primary tip — [ANWB verblinding](https://www.anwb.nl/auto/rijden/verblinding); [ANWB hoe werkt verblinding](https://www.anwb.nl/verkeer/veiligheid/hoe-werkt-verblinding)
- [OFFICIAL] New Zealand motorcycle road code: a scratched visor "will pick up reflective glare from oncoming vehicles" — [NZTA](https://nzta.govt.nz/roadcode/motorcycle-code/about-riding/when-conditions-change/night-riding)
- [ANECDOTE] Rain on lenses: "headlights made a zillion little reflections in all the water in my glasses"; glasses "with spots of rain on them will make you see much less when lit up by headlights"; common fallbacks are taking the glasses off in heavy rain ("when it would rain buckets I would just take them off"), Rain-X type repellents, smearing drops into a film with a finger, and a cap peak to keep drops off — [Bike Forums hydrophobic glasses at night](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1169683-hydrophobic-glasses-night.html); [Bike Forums best night commute glasses](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/685928-best-night-commute-glasses.html?nojs=1); [Bike Forums rain and glasses](https://www.bikeforums.net/showthread.php/484609-what-to-do-about-rain-and-glasses)
- [Media] RoadCyclingUK: in the wettest conditions rain and road spray can obscure vision even through a clear lens — [RoadCyclingUK](https://roadcyclinguk.com/gear/seeing-light-importance-wearing-sunglasses-whatever-weather.html)
- [MARKETING] Hydrophobic coatings are sold on speed-pedelec visors: Casco's Speedmask "Carbonic" visor is described as "self-cleaning and hydrophobic", preventing oil and water adhering — [Rose Bikes](https://www.rosebikes.com/p/casco-speedmask-carbonic-visor-706283)

### Inferences
- The causal chain is: anything on or in the optical path that scatters light (dirt, grease, micro-scratches, droplets, fog, uncoated back-surface reflections) converts a point-like headlamp into a veil. Removing scatter is "free" glare reduction with no loss of transmission — unlike any tint.
- For a cyclist who does not need a prescription, the choice at night is between no eyewear (zero added scatter, but wind/insects/rain in the eye, tearing at speed-pedelec speeds) and a clean clear lens. AR is a second-order improvement on the latter; cheap clear cycling lenses are generally uncoated polycarbonate.
- Sellable? A clear, AR-coated (both sides), hydrophobic + anti-fog night cycling lens is a legitimate product, but the honest claim is "adds minimal reflections and sheds water/dirt", not "blocks headlight glare". A lens-care kit (hydrophobic treatment + microfibre) is honest and cheap but commoditised.
- In heavy rain a peak + no glasses, or a peak that shelters the glasses, is likely better than any coated lens alone (anecdotal consensus).

### Gaps
- Only one small 1997 study supports AR for night glare; no AR study on cyclists, on plano sports lenses, or with modern LED headlamps was found.
- No test of hydrophobic coatings on cycling lenses/visors in rain at night was found.
- Which mainstream clear cycling lenses actually carry AR coating (and on which side) was not established.

Photochromics react to UV, which LED headlamps barely emit, so they will not darken in response to headlights.
- Market is saturated at every price level; no room for a differentiated "night glare" claim on a plain clear or photochromic lens. Sellable: yes, but as a commodity. Honest claim: "protects eyes without reducing night vision".

## 4. Yellow / amber lenses (brief; clinical depth is covered by another researcher)

### Takeaway
Yellow lenses are marketed (and liked by cyclists) for overcast daylight, fog and dusk contrast; for night-time headlight glare the best available trial found no benefit over clear lenses, and any tint removes light. Selling yellow lenses as an anti-headlight-glare product is not defensible.

### Cited Findings
- [EVIDENCE] Hwang, Tuccar-Burak & Peli, JAMA Ophthalmology 2019: 22 adults in a night-driving simulator with a headlight-glare simulator, three commercial yellow "night driving" lenses vs clear; no significant difference in pedestrian-detection response time with any yellow lens and no benefit compared with clear; authors: any tint "cut[s] or filter[s] out a chunk of light, which is basically the same as wearing sunglasses when driving at night"; older participants were hit harder by glare (about 1.5 s added response time vs 0.3 s in young adults); recommendation: advise against night-driving glasses and check glare-troubled patients for cataract or AMD — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/ophthalmology/20190920/yellow-lens-glasses-do-not-improve-night-driving); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20190802/Yellow-lens-glasses-do-not-improve-night-driving-visibility-study-finds.aspx); [AAO EyeNet](https://aao.org/eyenet/article/driving-at-night); [Review of Optometry](https://www.reviewofoptometry.com/news/article/yellow-lenses-no-help-in-night-driving)
- [MARKETING] Dutch retailers describe yellow lenses as "brightening" and "ideal for riding in fog"; Decathlon recommends clear or yellow category-0 lenses for evening/night — [Mantel koopgids](https://www.mantel.com/blog/fietsbril-koopgids); [Decathlon NL](https://www.decathlon.nl/c/htc/hoe-kies-ik-de-juiste-fietsbril_e62fb32d-bbec-466d-b793-a3db91c811c4)
- [ANECDOTE] A CycleChat user reports that "All-Condition red" interchangeable lenses "help cut down the glare from headlights significantly" at night — [CycleChat](https://www.cyclechat.net/threads/glasses-to-reduce-glare-of-headlights.39961/)

### Inferences
- Subjective comfort (the lamp looks less harsh/blue) and objective performance (detecting an unlit obstacle) diverge; this is the same trap as with polarized or tinted night lenses. A product claim based on comfort only ("softer-looking light") might be technically true but invites the charge of misleading by implication.

### Gaps
- No cyclist-specific trial; simulator study n = 22.

## 5. Speed pedelec specifics: required helmets in Belgium and the Netherlands, visor-equipped helmets, visor options and prices

### Takeaway
Netherlands: an NTA 8776 helmet is mandatory on a speed pedelec. Belgium: a helmet is mandatory, but the legal requirement is a bicycle helmet (EN 1078) that also protects temples and back of the head, or a moped helmet — NTA 8776 is *not* legally required (Test-Aankoop published a correction on this). Visors on speed-pedelec helmets are brand/model-specific (clips or magnets), offered in clear, grey-tinted, mirrored and photochromic versions at roughly EUR 40–120 for a spare; no visor found is marketed as an anti-glare/night visor beyond "clear for low light", and no universal aftermarket visor was found.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Netherlands: ordinary cyclists need no helmet, but on a speed pedelec a helmet with the NTA 8776 mark is mandatory — [ANWB](https://www.anwb.nl/fiets/accessoires-uitrusting/fietshelm/fietshelm-verplicht)
- [Retail info] Netherlands: helmet obligation for light mopeds (snorfiets, blue plate) since 1 January 2023; NTA 8776 helmets are accepted for these too — [Toppy](https://www.toppy.nl/advies/588/helmplicht-speed-pedelec-snorscooter-snorfiets.html)
- [OFFICIAL] Belgium: helmet mandatory for rider and passenger of a speed pedelec — [FOD Mobiliteit](https://mobilit.belgium.be/nl/duurzame-mobiliteit/fietsen/verkeersveiligheid-en-verkeersreglement)
- [OFFICIAL/EXPERT] Belgium: VIAS briefing (2025 update) — helmet must meet EN 1078 with extra protection of temples and back of the head — [VIAS Briefing Speedpedelecs](https://www.vias.be/publications/Briefing%20-%20Speedpedelecs%20-%20update%202025/Briefing_Speedpedelecs.pdf)
- [EXPERT] Test-Aankoop (correction to its own article): at least an approved bicycle helmet meeting EN 1078, with the extra condition that temples and back of head are protected; NTA 8776 is a Dutch standard and "not a legal obligation in Belgium" — [Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/klagen/publieke-klachten/fout-in-een-artikel/fada0992d0f1124784)

The latter is the only "universal" route seen.
- A visor has two glare-relevant properties: (a) it is a large, often scratched, rain-collecting surface in front of the eyes (bad — see section 2), and (b) it offers a surface on which a top or side shading band can be placed and used by tilting the head (good — see section 1). - Tinted and photochromic visors are daytime products; a photochromic visor will not react to LED headlamps (UV-driven) and is slightly less clear at night than a clear visor. "Clear visor for night" is the only defensible night claim currently made by sellers.
- Belgian riders are not restricted to NTA 8776 helmets, so open-face moped/jet helmets with ECE visors (and motorcycle visor accessories) are legally usable on a speed pedelec in Belgium.

## 6. Motorcyclist solutions that could transfer (Pinlock, sun strips, drop-down sun visors, photochromic inserts) and what is legal

### Takeaway
Motorcycling offers one directly transferable idea — the sun strip/tape band at the top of the visor used by tilting the head — and one clear warning: regulators and riding codes uniformly say do not use tinted visors, lenses or glasses at night. Pinlock is an anti-fog insert (helps scatter from fog, not glare); photochromic Pinlock/visors are UV-driven daytime aids.

- The regulatory logic (night-approved = at least 80% transmission) gives a ready-made benchmark for honesty: any cyclist product worn in front of the eyes at night should stay at or above about 80% transmission over the main field of view; anything darker must be confined to a small band the rider looks *under/beside*, not through.

## 7. Behavioural and setup measures (where to look, slowing, head tilt, own front light, route choice)

### Takeaway
Road-safety bodies give the same short list — do not look into the lights, keep your gaze on your own side/edge, slow down, keep optics clean — and note that recovery after being dazzled can take up to about 9 seconds. Lighting research supports the idea that a higher adaptation level (more ambient/own light) reduces *discomfort* from oncoming lamps, but illuminance from the oncoming lamp at the eye remains the dominant factor, so a strong, well-aimed own light helps mainly by lighting the path edge you need to follow, not by neutralising the glare.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL/EXPERT] ANWB: keep your gaze on your own half of the road; clean the windscreen; eyes can take up to 9 seconds to see properly again after glare; in an ANWB poll 62% look away, 49% squint and only 5% look straight into the light source — [ANWB verblinding](https://www.anwb.nl/auto/rijden/verblinding); [ANWB hoe werkt verblinding](https://www.anwb.nl/verkeer/veiligheid/hoe-werkt-verblinding)
- [OFFICIAL/EXPERT] ANWB: glare from oncoming traffic is the number-one complaint of ANWB members; survey: road users dazzled by high beam (76%), low beam (65%) and bicycle lights (39%); causes include mis-aimed headlamps, wrong bulbs, incorrect mounting — [ANWB persbericht Jan 2024](https://www.anwb.nl/over-anwb/anwb-actueel/persinformatie/persberichten/2024/240125-verblind-voertuigverlichting); [Hart van Nederland](https://www.hartvannederland.nl/milieu-gezondheid/artikelen/verblinding-grote-ergernis-voor-merendeel-nederlandse-weggebruikers)

- [EVIDENCE] The classic Schmidt-Clausen & Bindels (1974) model predicts discomfort (de Boer rating) from three inputs: adaptation luminance, illuminance at the observer's eye and glare angle — [FHWA report](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/humanfac/04138/introduction.cfm)
- [EVIDENCE] Van Derlofske, Bullough et al., SAE 2004-01-1280 (field study): illuminance at the eye is the dominant factor for both discomfort and disability glare from an oncoming headlamp; lamp size had no significant effect — [SAE Mobilus](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/headlamp-parameters-glare-2004-01-1280)
- [EVIDENCE] NHTSA/Lighting Research Center (2008): glare impairs forward visibility and causes discomfort "even when street lighting is present" — [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/investigation-safety-based-advanced-forward-lighting-concepts-reduce-glare)
- [ANECDOTE] Commuters report that a brighter, better-aimed own front light (one rider: switching to high beam) plus not looking at the car lights made vision acceptable, and that a brighter own light stops the eyes becoming so dark-adapted — [Bike Forums headlight washout](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-2.html)
- [OFFICIAL/EVIDENCE] SWOV: glare raises crash risk, particularly for older road users; SWOV has published on twilight vision and glare and a brochure on handling glare at night — [SWOV schemerzien en verblinding](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/schemerzien-en-verblinding); [SWOV brochure](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/brochure-blinded-light-how-handle-glare-safer-driving-night)

### Inferences
- Three levers follow from the glare models: reduce illuminance at the eye (shade the lamp — section 1), increase the glare angle (look away from the lamp towards the far edge of your own path), and raise adaptation luminance (own strong light, lit routes). A cut-off (StVZO) light of 60–150 lux that puts a bright carpet on the path does the third and also keeps the path edge visible *through* the veil. No study quantifying this for bicycle lights was found, so "measurably helps" is plausible from lab findings on ambient light but unproven for cyclists.
- The standard advice "look at the right-hand edge" is written for drivers meeting traffic on their left. On a two-way path where the car lane is on the cyclist's right, the equivalent is to look at the edge of the path *away from the road* (left edge or the centre line), i.e. increase the angle from the lamps. (Researcher's reasoning; no source states this for cyclists.)
- Route choice/timing (lit routes, the side of the road where you ride with traffic, paths with wider verges or hedges) is free and addresses the cause, but is often not available to a commuter.
- Sellable? A good StVZO front light is a real, honest product ("light the path without dazzling others; keeps your path edge visible when cars come the other way") but the market is mature (Busch & Müller, Lupine, Supernova, Lezyne, Trelock, etc.). Behavioural advice is content marketing, not a product.

### Inferences
- The problem is real and locally reported, but institutionally under-described: guidance treats two-way paths as a crash-risk issue, and glare as a public-lighting design issue, not as a reason for screens. An individual cyclist cannot count on infrastructure change; it is a lobbying point (report via the Fietsersbond "meldpunt"/app, ask for hedges, wider verge, edge marking, or lighting).
- Mechanism (researcher's background knowledge, not verified this session because the search budget ran out): European low beams are asymmetric, with the cut-off rising on the nearside (right) to light the verge and signs. A two-way path on the right of the carriageway puts the "wrong-way" cyclist exactly in that raised nearside part of the beam, at close lateral distance, often on a path lower than the road. This would explain why this configuration is so much worse than meeting cars across a centre line. Needs a source (UNECE R112/R149 beam pattern) before being stated as fact.
- White edge lines / reflective path-edge marking are a cheap infrastructure counter-measure because they give the dazzled cyclist something to steer by within the veil (consistent with the Fietsersbond Zaanstreek argument).

### Gaps
- The CROW Ontwerpwijzer Fietsverkeer and the 2024 Vademecum could not be opened; exact passages on verge width, hedges or glare for two-way paths are unverified.
- No Belgian (Flemish) news item about cyclists dazzled by car LED headlamps on two-way paths was found; no VIAS study on the topic was found; no crash statistics linking headlight glare to cyclist crashes were found.
- No evidence on hedges/screens along cycle paths (existence, effectiveness, cost).

## 9. Medical angle: when glare sensitivity warrants an eye exam; do prescription updates / AR help

### Takeaway
New, worsening or daily-life-limiting glare and halos at night are a recognised reason for an eye examination — cataract is the most cited cause, with uncorrected refractive error/astigmatism, dry eye, keratoconus and post-surgery effects also listed; rainbow halos with pain or sudden blur are an emergency. For spectacle wearers an up-to-date prescription with AR coating is the standard optical recommendation; this is a referral message, not a product.

### Cited Findings
- [EXPERT/charity] Age UK London: halos around lights "can be an important sign of cataracts — particularly if they are new, persistent, or affecting daily life" — [Age UK London](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/london/publications/blogs/articles/halos-as-the-nights-get-darker/)
- [EXPERT] All About Vision: rainbow halos can signal acute angle-closure glaucoma — contact an eye doctor immediately if combined with sudden blurred vision, headache or severe eye pain; keratoconus is a rarer cause of glare and halos — [All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/symptoms/halo-lights/)
- [Clinic pages] Glare from headlights at night is described as the most common trigger for cataract surgery; blurry lights at night can indicate worsening cataract — [CEENTA](https://ceenta.com/news-blog/five-signs-your-cataracts-are-getting-worse); [Vision Institute](https://www.vision-institute.com/glare-halos-cause-effect-solution/)
- [Clinic pages] Dry eye can worsen halos and glare and is treatable with lubricating drops; even minor uncorrected prescriptions can amplify night halos; after LASIK or cataract surgery halos typically last weeks and night driving should wait for clearance — [Ocala Eye](https://ocalaeye.com/what-causes-glare-and-halos/); [Refocus Eye Doctors](https://hamden.refocuseyedoctors.com/article/driving-at-night-before-and-after-cataract-surgery/)
- [EVIDENCE-linked advice] The authors of the 2019 yellow-lens trial advise that patients troubled by night glare be examined for cataract or AMD rather than sold night-driving glasses — [AAO EyeNet](https://aao.org/eyenet/article/driving-at-night)
- [EVIDENCE] Older observers lose far more performance under headlight glare (about 1.5 s added response time vs 0.3 s in young adults in the simulator study) — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20190802/Yellow-lens-glasses-do-not-improve-night-driving-visibility-study-finds.aspx)
- [EVIDENCE/OFFICIAL] SWOV: glare increases crash risk particularly for older road users — [SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/schemerzien-en-verblinding)
- [EVIDENCE] AR-coated prescription lenses gave about 2x better contrast sensitivity under glare than uncoated lenses (1997 study) — [PubMed 9190135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9190135/)
- [ANECDOTE] Cyclists on forums give the same advice: if oncoming lights are a struggle, get checked for astigmatism or cataracts — [Bike Arlington Forum](https://www.bikearlingtonforum.com/forums/topic/night-blindness/page/2)

### Inferences
- For the individual in Belgium: if glare has become worse than it used to be, is worse than for riding companions, or comes with halos/starbursts, an eye exam (optician/optometrist first, ophthalmologist if lens opacity or corneal issues are suspected) is likely the highest-yield single step, because intra-ocular scatter cannot be fixed by any eyewear — and any tinted product would mask a treatable condition.
- For a seller: any product page should carry this referral message; it also limits liability.

### Gaps
- Most medical sources retrieved are clinic marketing pages or consumer portals; no guideline from a professional body (e.g. Belgian/Dutch ophthalmology or optometry associations, College of Optometrists) was retrieved.
- No data on prevalence of abnormal glare sensitivity among commuting cyclists.

## 10. Emerging tech: electrochromic / LCD "active" glare blocking

### Takeaway
Glasses or visors that locally darken only the bright spot (camera + pixelated LCD) exist only as research prototypes and concept demos (Optics Express LCD glasses at 10 fps; Valeo/Inoptec 2016; Bosch Virtual Visor for cars, CES 2020) — nothing is purchasable for cyclists. What *is* on sale in 2026 are uniform electrochromic sunglasses (POVEC C1, Chamelo, ONESEC), whose lightest state (e.g. 37% transmission) is far too dark for night and which do not target the lamp.

### Cited Findings
- [EVIDENCE] SAE paper 2015-01-1707, "Methods of Glare Reduction from Oncoming Traffic during Driving": states no commercial product exists; proposes a pixelated film/glass with locally variable transmittance (electrochromic or transparent LCD) — [SAE Mobilus](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2015-01-1707)
- [EVIDENCE] Optics Express work (Optica spotlight): LCD-based adaptive eyeglasses aimed at headlamp glare; refresh limited to about 10 fps — [Optica](https://opg.optica.org/spotlight/summary.cfm?id=315738)
- [Concept] Valeo night-driving glasses (Inoptec with PA Consulting, shown 2016): liquid-crystal shutter lenses synchronised with pulsed LED headlamps (requires matching car lighting — different approach from spot dimming) — [Elektor Magazine](https://www.elektormagazine.com/news/Night-Driving-Glasses-by-Valeo)
- [Concept] Bosch Virtual Visor (CES 2020): transparent LCD panel plus driver camera that darkens only the part casting a shadow on the eyes; presented for cars against the sun; reports ranged from "concept" to "talks with automakers"; no confirmed production vehicle found — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/05/heres-how-bosch-engineers-transformed-the-regular-ol-sun-visor); [Bosch Mobility](https://bosch-mobility.com/en/company/current-news/how-bosch-innovates-the-sun-visor); [Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/node/100278)
- [Patent-stage] Apple explored windscreen modulators (photochromic, liquid-crystal or electrochromic layers) to dull bright lights at specific points; a US patent covers an "electrochromic adaptive driving beam system" — [AppleInsider forum](https://forums.appleinsider.com/discussion/207939); [USPTO 11059416](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11059416)
- [Product, 2026] POVEC C1 electrochromic sport sunglasses (shown CES 2026; on sale 2026): solid-state electrochromic lens switching between category 2 and 3 in about 1–3 seconds, visible light transmission 37% to 9%, USB-C, up to 28 days per charge, USD 249 (earlier guidance USD 250–350) — [BikeRumor](https://bikerumor.com/povec-looks-to-disrupt-the-performance-eyewear-market-with-its-c1-electrochromic-sunglasses/); [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/povec-c1-launch-0626); [Cycle Tech Review](https://cycletechreview.com/2026/accessories/povec-c1-electrochromic-sunglasses/); [road.cc](https://road.cc/?p=1732815)
- [Product] Chamelo (Music Shield): 0.1-second tint change, manually controlled, not polarized; ONESEC (Decathlon HK partner brand): "one second" colour change, IP57 — [ONESEC at Decathlon HK](https://www.decathlon.com.hk/en/decathlon-partners/onesec); [Cycle Tech Review](https://cycletechreview.com/2026/accessories/povec-c1-electrochromic-sunglasses/)

### Inferences
- Uniform electrochromic lenses are instant-photochromics for daytime; with a clearest state of category 2 they would be unsafe at night and do nothing lamp-specific. A hypothetical "auto-dim when headlights appear" mode would dim the whole scene — the opposite of what is needed.
- Spot-dimming needs a camera, eye-position knowledge and a fast pixelated shutter close to the eye (out of focus, so the dimmed spot must be large); nobody has commercialised it in a decade. Not a realistic basis for a small-company product in the near term.
- The passive equivalent of spot dimming is a fixed shading band/patch plus head movement (section 1): cheap, no electronics, honest.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm 2025–2026 status of any startup working on pixelated anti-glare eyewear, nor price/availability of CTRL Eyewear or Out Of "Electra/IRID" LCD lenses (searches did not surface them); absence of a spot-dimming product is based on a small number of searches.
- No independent night-time test of any electrochromic eyewear found.

## 11. Cross-cutting: which solution is a "sellable product", and is its claim defensible?

### Takeaway
Only physical shading offers an unserved product niche with a defensible (narrow) claim; clear/AR/hydrophobic lenses and StVZO lights are honest but commoditised; tinted, yellow and photochromic lenses and visors cannot honestly be sold against headlight glare at night; active glare-blocking is not available.

### Cited Findings
- Shading by peak/visor is recommended only anecdotally; no dedicated cyclist product was found — [CycleChat](https://www.cyclechat.net/threads/glasses-to-reduce-glare-of-headlights.39961/); [Bike Forums](https://www.bikeforums.net/commuting/1165425-headlight-washout-post20777412.html); [Bike Arlington Forum](https://www.bikearlingtonforum.com/forums/topic/night-blindness/page/2)
- Tints at night are advised against by riding codes; night-approved motorcycle visors transmit 80% or more — [Highway Code rule 94](https://theorytest.org.uk/hwc/highway-code/rule-94.html); [RAD.eu blog](https://blog.rad.eu/en/can-you-ride-with-a-dark-or-tinted-visor/)
- Yellow lenses showed no benefit under headlight glare in the best available trial — [AAO EyeNet](https://aao.org/eyenet/article/driving-at-night)
- AR helps only against lens-generated reflections — [All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/eyeglasses/night-driving-glasses/); [PubMed 9190135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9190135/)
- Speed-pedelec visors are proprietary per helmet family and cost about EUR 40–120 as spares — [Fietshelmspecialist](https://fietshelmspecialist.com/products/abus-vizier-pedelec-2-0-ace-hyban-2-0-ace); [Rose Bikes](https://www.rosebikes.com/p/casco-speedmask-vautron-visor-813548); [R2B store](https://www.r2bstore.nl/en/products/r2b-transparant-en-magnetisch-helm-vizier-geschikt-voor-de-speed-pedelec-helm-van-r2b)
- No spot-dimming eyewear is on the market; electrochromic sunglasses bottom out at about 37% transmission — [SAE Mobilus](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2015-01-1707); [BikeRumor](https://bikerumor.com/povec-looks-to-disrupt-the-performance-eyewear-market-with-its-c1-electrochromic-sunglasses/)

- Advice for the individual Belgian cyclist, in order of likely yield (synthesis): (1) rule out an eye problem if glare is new/worse or accompanied by halos; (2) clean, unscratched clear lens or visor — or none in heavy rain; (3) cap/peak and the head-dip, looking to the path edge away from the road; (4) strong, correctly aimed cut-off front light; (5) route/side choice where possible; (6) report the location (hedge, edge marking, verge) to the road authority/Fietsersbond.
- Product direction with the best honesty-to-opportunity ratio: a purpose-designed, helmet-compatible shading brim and/or a repositionable shading strip for speed-pedelec visors and large cycling shields, sold with explicit instructions (dip, do not look through) and explicit limits; possibly bundled with a clear AR/hydrophobic lens. All performance claims would rest on first-principles optics and user testing, since no published evidence exists.

### Gaps
- No user testing or measurement exists for the proposed product direction; market size (number of night-commuting cyclists and speed-pedelec riders in Belgium/Netherlands bothered by car headlights specifically) was not quantified beyond general ANWB/Radar survey figures.
- Regulatory status of an add-on brim/strip on EN 1078 / NTA 8776 helmets and of marketing claims under EU consumer law (unfair commercial practices) was not researched here.
