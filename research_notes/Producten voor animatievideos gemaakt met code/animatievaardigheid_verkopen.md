Method note. Researched on 20 Nov 2026 with 40 web searches. Everything else comes from search-result extracts of the cited pages. Labels used throughout:
- **[hard]** official page, licence text or marketplace's own data
- **[vendor]** a seller's own claim or marketing

- **[synthesis]** my own inference

Currency: I convert at an **assumed 1 USD ≈ €0.86**. That is an approximate late-2026 rate and was **not verified in this session**, so re-check it before quoting prices.

## 1. Productised animation service: 2026 prices, demand, under-served segments, named examples, first-client tactics

### Takeaway
Short animated explainers and promos are a commodity at the bottom of the market:
- **Fiverr:** about $100–$250 per gig. Fiverr's own guide gives a 2D-explainer average fixed price of $210.87 and a typical range of $168–$1,260.
- **App-store previews on Fiverr:** $50–$400.
- **Productised launch-video shops:** a mid tier at roughly $245–$3,000 per video with 2–6-day delivery (Flowjam, Videodeck, ContentBeta, Contra freelancers).
- **Premium specialists:** $9,000 per video and up.
- **"Unlimited video" subscriptions:** about $260–$1,700/month.

Freelance demand for video and design work held up in 2025–2026 surveys, but low-budget segments report rate pressure from AI tools. The most reachable newcomer segment with documented buying behaviour is **indie SaaS/app founders around a launch**. Documented productised video shops in that niche got early clients by **giving away free videos**.

### Inferences
- **[synthesis]** Price ladder for a newcomer in USD (EUR at the assumed rate):
  - Fiverr-competitive: $100–$250 (€86–€215)
  - Productised launch clip sold direct: $250–$1,000 (€215–€860)
  - Experienced studio: $1,500–$3,000+
  
  Selling direct (Contra 0% commission, or own Stripe invoice) avoids Fiverr's 20% cut and the buyer fee, which matters at $250 tickets.
- **[synthesis]** Indie SaaS/app founders are the best-documented reachable segment. They launch publicly (Product Hunt, X), so they can be found and timed. Their product is a UI, which code-animation reproduces well. Videodeck and FoxPlug both used free videos as their acquisition channel. Weak points: tight budgets ("too expensive for a solo budget") and a growing set of $0 DIY/AI tools.
- **[synthesis]** App-store previews are a well-specified, repeatable 30-second product. That suits a code pipeline (device frames, UI mock-ups, captions), but the bottom of the market is at $50–$100.

## 2. What a code-based pipeline changes: commercial use of programmatic video, personalised video at scale, strengths and limits

### Takeaway
Programmatic video is used commercially, mostly for **automation**: personalised or batch videos, video SaaS and pipelines, rather than one-off client animations. Remotion is the commercial reference point, with paid licences and an "automators" tier. Public freelance prices are scarce: Upwork shows fixed-price Remotion jobs around $1,200 and "build a video automation studio" service listings. "Personalised video at scale" exists as a niche, but in 2026 it is crowded by funded vendors (HeyGen, SundaySky, Blings, KCM). A solo developer's realistic opening is narrower: **batch variants and data-driven series for one client**, not a platform.

### Inferences
- **[synthesis] Advantages over After Effects:**
  - Charts and numbers come straight from data, so one template yields N variants (sizes 9:16/1:1/16:9, languages, prices, client names).
  - Revisions are text edits plus re-render, which cuts the cost of "unlimited revisions" promises like Flowjam's.
  - Rendering is deterministic, so output is consistent.
  - The same scene can be delivered as MP4, GIF and stills, the "launch kit" packaging FoxPlug describes.
  - Batch personalisation (e.g. 50 event-attendee or customer videos) is affordable for one person.
- **[synthesis] Limits:**
  - Character animation, organic and secondary motion, hand-drawn feel and lip-sync are weak or very laborious in HTML/SVG/GSAP.
  - Sound design (SFX timing, music edit) must be done separately.
  - Clients who want "a cartoon character explainer" are a poor fit. Clients who want UI, kinetic type, charts or icons are a good fit.
- **[synthesis] Personalised video at scale:** sellable in principle, as the YARX, GitHub Unwrapped and VScreen precedents show, but the buyers are companies with data access and integration needs, the sales cycle is long, and funded AI-avatar vendors dominate. For a €500 first experiment, offer it as an add-on ("10 variants of your launch clip, one per audience/feature") or as a one-off batch (e.g. a year-in-review for a newsletter's or community's members), not as a platform.

## 3. Templates and toolkits: marketplaces, payout terms, emerging code-video kits, AI-agent skills, tutorials

### Takeaway
The established motion-template marketplaces (Envato, Motion Array) are built around After Effects/Premiere formats. Their author economics worsened in 2026: Envato moved all Market authors to a 50% author fee from 1 July 2026, and Motion Array pays 15–45% tiered royalties and had **closed artist submissions**. CapCut and Canva template programmes pay per use but are region-limited, beta or opaque, and use those tools' own editors, not code. For **code-based video**, the free supply is huge and official: HyperFrames' about 450 free registry blocks, Remotion's free official agent skills, and community skills. Paid "skill" marketplaces exist (SkillHQ, Agensi, MCP Market) but have no verified sales data. Paid tutorials exist (a 2026 Udemy course on Remotion plus HyperFrames plus Codex; GSAP courses from $12.99 to $599). **No named seller of paid HyperFrames kits was found.** That points to early, unproven demand, not a proven market.

## 4. Licences that determine what may be sold

### Takeaway
- **HyperFrames (Apache-2.0):** permits commercial use, modification and redistribution of derived templates, with notice and attribution conditions. It grants no trademark rights.
- **GSAP:** free for commercial use, including the former "Club" plugins, since 30 Apr 2025 under Webflow's "Standard No Charge" licence. It **prohibits use in visual no-code animation builders that compete with Webflow**, and Webflow can revoke it at its discretion. Selling videos and code templates is fine; building a GUI animation editor on GSAP is the risk zone.
- **Remotion:** free for individuals and companies with up to 3 employees. Larger companies need a Company Licence ($25/seat/month for "Creators"; $0.01/render with a $100/month minimum for "Automators"; Enterprise minimum $500). Copying or modifying Remotion code to sell a derivative is forbidden.
- **Icons:** Lucide (ISC/MIT) and Heroicons (MIT) are free for client work and resold templates if the licence notice is kept.
- **unDraw:** allows commercial use without attribution but forbids distributing its assets "in packs", which matters for resold templates.
- **Storyset:** reportedly requires attribution on free use.
- **Google Fonts (OFL):** fonts may be bundled and sold with software but not sold alone.
- **Music:** Epidemic Sound's Personal plan excludes brand advertising (client work needs Commercial/Pro). YouTube Audio Library's standard licence is YouTube-only. Pixabay reportedly allows client work, with Content ID claims possible.

## 6. B2B basics from Belgium: invoicing foreign businesses, payment, contracts/usage rights, marketplace income

### Takeaway
- **Services to EU businesses:** generally taxed where the customer is (B2B general rule) and reverse-charged. The invoice shows the customer's VAT number and a reverse-charge mention.
- **Services to US businesses:** outside the scope of EU VAT.
- **Exempt small enterprise:** whether one supplying cross-border B2B services must file an intra-community listing was **not confirmed** (one vendor says exempt firms normally file no VAT returns). Ask an accountant.
- **Copyright:** under Belgian law, transfer of copyright in a commissioned animation does **not** happen automatically. It must be proven in writing and is interpreted restrictively in the author's favour, with a statutory exception for commissioned works in the non-cultural/advertising sector (art. XI.167 §3 WER) whose conditions should be checked. So every client deal needs a short written licence or assignment clause.
- **Marketplaces:** Fiverr and Upwork income is ordinary professional income. Repeated gigs mean a professional activity under the same registration picture as the earlier notes, and those platforms fall under DAC7 reporting.

### Cited Findings
- **Copyright in Belgium:**
  - contracts on copyright must always be proven in writing toward the author; Cassation (18 June 2020) applied this strictly
  - rights do not pass automatically to the commissioning party
  - the contract should state works, remuneration, scope and duration per mode of exploitation; clauses are interpreted restrictively in the author's favour (art. XI.167 §1 WER)
  - general future transfer of moral rights is impossible
  - commissioned works in the non-cultural sector or advertising fall under a statutory exception (art. XI.167 §3 WER)
  - Belgium has no Dutch-style fictive authorship for commissioning parties
  - Cass. 19 Feb 2026 addressed transfer for works in the non-cultural sector (details not read)
  
  **[legal commentary]** — [legalnews.be, overdracht van auteursrechten (Reyns)](https://legalnews.be/it-ip/de-overdracht-van-auteursrechten-reyns-advocaten/); [legalnews.be, schriftelijk bewijs (Schoups)](https://legalnews.be/bouw-vastgoed/schriftelijk-bewijs-vereist-bij-overdracht-auteursrechten-architect-schoups/); [legalnews.be, Cass. 19 feb 2026](https://legalnews.be/it-ip/overdracht-van-auteursrechten-bij-werken-binnen-de-niet-culturele-sector-cass-19-februari-2026-eric-b/); [legalnews.be, Tomorrowland/HvJ](https://legalnews.be/it-ip/creaties-logos-etc-voor-tomorrowland-met-enkel-een-mondelinge-overeenkomst-en-discussies-over-de-auteursrechten-het-hof-van-justitie-zal-zich-moeten-uitspreken-legalnews/); [Unizo auteursrechten](https://www.unizo.be/ondernemersgids/juridisch/intellectuele-eigendom/auteursrechten-0); legal text: [Justel WER](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/wet/2013/02/28/2013A11134/justel) (not fetched)

  - B2B services to a business in another EU state: no VAT charged, "btw verlegd" (reverse charge) on the invoice with the customer's VAT number (Dutch government guide; the same EU rule applies, arts. 44 and 196 VAT Directive, legal text not fetched)

- Belgian status, the €25,000 small-enterprise exemption, social-contribution thresholds, DAC7 (which covers "personal services" platforms such as Fiverr and Upwork as well as goods), W-8BEN, and Stripe/Wise/PayPal fees are all in the earlier notes. Not repeated. **[earlier notes]**

### Inferences
- **[synthesis]** Minimal paperwork for each service sale:
  1. A one-page order confirmation or terms stating deliverables, revision rounds, delivery date and price.
  2. A **licence clause**: worldwide, non-exclusive or exclusive, perpetual right to use the delivered video in all online and advertising media. Keep ownership of reusable code, templates and scene libraries with the creator. List third-party assets (fonts, icons, music) and their licences.

  4. An invoice with enterprise number, the small-enterprise exemption mention or "btw verlegd" plus the customer's VAT number (EU B2B), and a note of the non-EU place of supply (US).

## 7. Comparison and outcomes: time to first euro, first-three-months income, capital, skills built (survivor bias explicit)

### Takeaway
**[synthesis, based on sections 1–6 and the earlier notes]**
- **Service (direct outreach with free samples):** the fastest and most probable route to a first euro (weeks), and the highest income per sale ($100–$1,000). It needs no capital beyond a domain or optional Contra profile and builds sales, scoping and client-feedback skills.
- **Templates/kits:** cheap but slow and uncertain. The free official supply is large; Gumroad base rates are a median of about $72/month and 44% at $0 (earlier notes).
- **Affiliate:** €0 capital, but the slowest and most variance-driven, depending on reach. Credible accounts suggest near zero in months 1–3.

All the documented successes (Videodeck $8K MRR in 3 months, Flowjam's "30+ YC startups", the $3K/month affiliate claim) are survivor stories with self-reported figures. Videodeck's founders had a team owning "the entire production process", which is not comparable to a solo newcomer.

### Inferences
**1. "Launch Clip in 72 hours" for indie SaaS / AI-tool founders. Chance: moderate (best in set).**
- **Offer:** a 20–30 s animated launch/demo clip (UI mock-up, kinetic type, captions), delivered as 16:9 + 9:16 + 1:1 plus a GIF and 3 stills ("launch kit"), with 2 revision rounds.
- **Price:** $290 (≈ €250) intro, rising to $490 (≈ €420); add-on of 5 extra variants (feature, audience or language) for $150.
- **Target:** founders with an upcoming Product Hunt or X launch, or a new landing page without video.
- **First customers:**
  - each week, pick 10 upcoming or recent PH and IH launches with no video
  - render a free 10-second teaser of *their* product from public screenshots and send it with the price
  - post every teaser (with permission) as build-in-public content on X and LinkedIn
  - add a free Contra listing
- **Main risk:** founders' low budgets and $0 DIY/AI alternatives; scope creep on real UI fidelity.
- **Test-video line (15–25 s):** Hook: "Your launch video, made with code, in 72 hours." Demo: a blank browser frame fills with a fake SaaS dashboard; cursor clicks, charts animate in, the frame splits into 16:9/9:16/1:1. Result: a "Launch kit: video + GIF + stills, $290" card with an upvote counter ticking up.

**2. App Store Preview Pack for indie iOS/Android developers. Chance: moderate.**
- **Offer:** a 30 s preview to Apple spec (device-framed UI flow, captions, no filmed footage) plus a Google Play cut.
- **Price:** $199 (≈ €171); Play add-on $79.
- **Target:** solo app developers with new or updated apps that have screenshots but no preview video.
- **First customers:** scan new releases in a category (habit, finance, AI-utility apps), send a 6-second animated mock-up made from their store screenshots, and post before/after store-listing comparisons as shorts.
- **Main risk:** Fiverr undercutting at $50–$100; Apple spec rules (previews should show the app's own UI), so mock-ups must use real screens.
- **Test-video line:** Hook: "Your app has no preview video. Here's what it's missing." Demo: a static App Store listing; a phone frame slides in and plays an animated UI flow with captions. Result: the listing now shows a playing preview, with a "$199, 3 days" tag.

**3. Animated Chart Clips for finance, newsletter and data creators. Chance: moderate (for a first sale) / low (for recurring income).**
- **Offer:** you send a CSV or a chart; I return a 15–20 s vertical animated chart Reel in your brand colours, with a hook title and captions.
- **Price:** $79 (≈ €68) per clip, or $249/month for 4 clips.
- **Target:** personal-finance and markets creators, beehiiv/Substack data newsletters, B2B SaaS publishing benchmark reports.
- **First customers:** take a chart a creator already published as a static image, animate it, and send it ("your chart, but it moves"); post 3–5 data-clip examples weekly on X and LinkedIn.
- **Main risk:** small creator budgets; tools like ChartAnimator and AECharts; finance-content rules if you write the claims yourself (earlier notes: ESMA/FSMA), so animate the client's data and avoid adding advice.
- **Test-video line:** Hook: a static bar chart with the caption "Static charts get scrolled past." Demo: the same chart rebuilds itself, with bars growing, a counter ticking to the key number and a highlight pulse. Result: "Send a CSV → get a Reel in 48 h. $79."

**4. "Launch Video Kit for HyperFrames" (code starter kit plus AI-agent skill). Chance: low (for sales) / good (for learning and lead generation).**
- **Offer:** a paid repo (Gumroad or Polar) with:
  - 6–8 polished scenes (device frame, UI-flow cursor, kinetic headline, stat counter, logo outro)
  - a brand config file (colours, fonts, logo)
  - render presets for PH/X/App Store sizes
  - a SKILL.md / prompt file so Claude Code can adapt it
  - Apache/OFL/Lucide notices
- **Price:** $29 (≈ €25); "done-for-you" upsell to offer 1.
- **Target:** developer-founders already using Claude Code or Codex who want to make their own launch video.
- **First customers:** a "made with this kit" demo video in every post; share a free mini-scene on GitHub, Indie Hackers and r/SideProject that links to the paid kit.
- **Main risk:** the free official registry (about 450 blocks) and free skills anchor price at $0; no proven demand for paid HyperFrames kits; HyperFrames API changes break the kit.
- **Test-video line:** Hook: "One prompt. One launch video." Demo: a terminal types `"make my launch video"`, then a split screen shows scene thumbnails assembling into a timeline and the final clip playing. Result: "Launch Video Kit for HyperFrames, $29" with a file tree glowing.

**5. Animated "how it works" explainers for website builders and AI-voice tools, with affiliate links (Webflow / Framer / ElevenLabs / Hostinger). Chance: low (for income within 3 months).**
- **Offer:** 20 s explainers on your own account ("build a portfolio site in 3 steps"), with affiliate link and disclosure.
- **Commission:** 50% of first-year payments (Webflow/Framer), 22% for 12 months (ElevenLabs), about 40–60% one-time (Hostinger).
- **Target:** beginners choosing a site builder or voice tool.
- **First customers:** none needed; the bottleneck is reach. Repost each explainer as a portfolio piece and pitch the same style to the tool companies' smaller competitors (feeds offer 1).
- **Main risk:**
  - reach (median about 500 views per post, per earlier notes)
  - programme approval and term changes (Descript cut to a $25 flat fee)
  - brand-bidding and misleading-claims clauses
  - ad-spam perception and originality de-ranking
- **Test-video line:** Hook: "This website was built in 90 seconds." Demo: an empty canvas; blocks snap in (hero, gallery, contact), a cursor drags a colour swatch and the site goes "Live". Result: a URL bar with a green check and the caption "Tool + affiliate disclosure in caption."

**Not in the top 5:**
- **Personalised-video-at-scale platform** (e.g. automated real-estate market updates): long sales cycle, data integration, and HeyGen and KCM incumbents. Use it only as a batch-variant add-on to offer 1.
- **Envato, Motion Array, CapCut and Canva templates:** wrong file formats, closed submissions or worse 2026 terms.
