One gram of activated sludge holds tens of billions of living bacteria.
A wastewater plant is not really a filter; it is a bacteria farm.
In 1914, Ardern and Lockett in Manchester aerated sewage and reused the sludge.
Blowers pump in air, and bacteria clump into flocs that eat the pollution.
In the clarifier, heavy flocs settle and treated water flows off the top.
With oxygen, nitrifying bacteria convert toxic ammonia into nitrate.
Without oxygen, other bacteria turn that nitrate into harmless nitrogen gas.
Sludge age is about 5 to 15 days, long enough for slow nitrifiers.
US law demands 85 percent removal of organic pollution; good plants reach 95.
Blowing that air is the biggest energy cost, often around half the total.
Honestly, waste never truly vanishes; it becomes gas, water and surplus sludge.
The real engineering is building a home where the right microbes can thrive.
