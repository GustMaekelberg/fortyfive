## 1. Established personalised-print categories: brands, prices (physical vs digital), demand evidence, saturation

### Takeaway
The personalised-print family is large and proven, but crowded at the obvious end. Star maps and city maps have big DTC brands with thousands of reviews: The Night Sky has ~9.6–10k Trustpilot reviews and Mapiful ~2.6k. Etsy also has hundreds of shops, some with 6k–100k+ sales, selling digital versions for roughly $4–15 and physical prints for ~$20–35. Free browser generators and open-source repos now produce the same outputs at no cost. Narrower formats (race/route posters with splits, 1:1 birth posters) have fewer and more specialised sellers. Those sellers cluster at ~£20 / $35–45 for an unframed poster, ~€59–71 for larger premium sizes, and $79+ framed.

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Saturation ranking (most → least saturated) from the evidence found: star maps ≈ city maps (big DTC brands, hundreds of Etsy shops, free generators, open-source repos) > birth-stats prints (many cheap Etsy templates; 1:1 is a smaller premium niche) > major-marathon route posters (several specialist sellers per big race, ~£20 entry price) > smaller races, splits-heavy designs, Hyrox, chess and other hobby data (few or no direct sellers found).
- [synthesis] Price anchors a newcomer must fit under or justify: digital $4–15 (star/city maps), unframed physical ~£20 / $35–45, premium large ~€59–71, framed from ~$79.
- [synthesis] The DTC brands' main moat is review volume and ad spend, not technology. The rendering itself is now commoditised by free tools. A newcomer's edge has to come from data richness (splits, elevation, pacing), a niche audience, or a visibly distinctive style. Animation can demonstrate all three.

- [synthesis] Most promising niches for this seller, with the evidence strength:

## 3. Data and licensing: what can be used safely

### Takeaway
**OpenStreetMap data** can be printed and sold commercially if you credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors / openstreetmap.org/copyright" on the print. Self-rendering OSM data (OSMnx, Protomaps PMTiles with a CC0 style, own SVG) avoids tile-provider print licences. Mapbox and Stadia say to contact sales for revenue posters or print exports, and MapTiler's free tiers are non-commercial. **Hipparcos** star data is CC BY-NC (commercial use needs ESA Legal authorisation), even though the market leader advertises it. The Yale Bright Star Catalogue is described as public domain only in secondary sources. **Strava's API** limits display to the user, bans AI use, and since June 2026 requires a paid subscription for Standard-tier developers. Customer-uploaded GPX files avoid it entirely. Lyrics, team or league marks and race brand names are the highest takedown risks.

### Cited Findings
**Maps**
- The ODbL permits use "for any purpose", including commercial. A printed map is a "Produced Work", and attribution is required when it is used publicly. Suggested print wording is "Map data from OpenStreetMap" or "© OpenStreetMap" with "openstreetmap.org/copyright", placed near the work or where users would expect it, and legible. If the product is used to extract or recreate substantial OSM data, it becomes a Derivative Database with share-alike duties [hard: OSMF guidance] — [OSMF Licence and Legal FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ); [OSMF Attribution guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Attribution); [OSM copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright/attribution-guide/); [OSM help: poster prints credit](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/20608/commercial-use-osm-in-poster-prints-how-to-credit)
- **Mapbox**: to print images from the Studio print export "for any use, contact us". Print maps need the Mapbox logo plus "© Mapbox, © OpenStreetMap". Static Images API exports go up to 1,280×1,280 px. Studio print exports are limited to 100 per account, with no SVG/EPS/PDF [hard: Mapbox docs] — [Mapbox print help](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/print/); [Mapbox static maps](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/static-maps/); [Mapbox attribution](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/attribution/)
- **Stadia Maps**: the FAQ lists "a poster that you designed for revenue (contact us to discuss licensing)" as commercial use. Cacheable Static Maps (2,000 credits/request) cover "most print use cases" with a paid subscription, up to **5,000 printed copies per image**. Perpetual print licences are enterprise-only. No per-poster dollar price was found [hard: Stadia pages] — [Stadia FAQ](https://stadiamaps.com/faqs/); [Stadia cacheable static maps](https://stadiamaps.com/news/announcing-cacheable-static-map-images/); [Stadia static maps docs](https://docs.stadiamaps.com/static-maps); [Stadia pricing](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/)
- **MapTiler**: Free MapTiler Data is "limited to non-commercial and evaluation use", and the free Cloud plan is non-commercial. Commercial products need a paid Data licence. Removing the © MapTiler credit needs written permission. A 2021 post on "attribution-free maps for videos, posters and games" exists, but its conditions were not retrieved [hard: MapTiler terms] — [MapTiler Data licence](https://www.maptiler.com/data/license); [MapTiler Cloud terms](https://www.maptiler.com/terms/cloud/); [MapTiler attribution-free maps](https://maptiler.com/news/2021/09/attribution-free-map-for-videos-posters-and-games)
- **Protomaps**: code is BSD-3, map design (styles) CC0, tilesets ODbL (attribute OSM). It is designed for self-hosting with no API key (planet file ~120 GB, z0–15). The hosted API is free for non-commercial use, and commercial hosted use runs via GitHub Sponsors (~$14/month tier per a snippet, unverified). Forks and commercial products must use a different name [hard: Protomaps docs; tier price 3rd-party snippet] — [Protomaps FAQ](https://protomaps.com/faq); [protomaps/styles GitHub](https://github.com/protomaps/styles); [Protomaps downloads](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads); [GitHub Sponsors protomaps](https://github.com/sponsors/protomaps)
- maptoposter (MIT) renders directly from OSM via OSMnx. A Flutter port prints "© OpenStreetMap contributors" on every poster by default [3rd-party] — [GitHub maptoposter](https://github.com/originalankur/maptoposter); [pub.dev maposter](https://pub.dev/documentation/maposter/latest/)

**Stars**
- **Hipparcos/Tycho catalogues: CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO.** Credit "ESA" is required. "Prior to any commercial use … including any use … that directly or indirectly generates a financial gain, a request for authorisation/licence shall be made … by contacting the ESA Legal office". ESA endorsement may not be implied [hard: ESA terms] — [ESA ESDC terms](https://cosmos.esa.int/web/esdc/terms-and-conditions); [ESA Hipparcos catalogues](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/hipparcos/catalogues)
- **Yale Bright Star Catalogue**: a NASA-era doc file says "in the public domain", but a forum poster could not determine its IP status, and Debian packages a Yale-derived dataset under non-free. d3-celestial's data files are said (secondhand) to be BSD-3. The HYG database merges Hipparcos, Yale and Gliese data, so its upstream licence matters [3rd-party; unresolved] — [data.nasa.gov Bright Star Catalog](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/bright-star-catalog); [Debian yale copyright](https://metadata.ftp-master.debian.org/changelogs/non-free/y/yale/stable_copyright); [dieghernan/celestial_data](https://github.com/dieghernan/celestial_data); [HYG database](https://www.astronexus.com/hyg/964)

**Strava vs GPX upload**
- Nov 2024 API agreement: third-party apps may display a user's Strava data **only to that user**. Use of API data in AI models is prohibited. Coverage also reads it as restricting analytics-type processing. Strava said <0.1% of apps were affected [3rd-party news] — [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/11/stravas-changes-to-kill-off-apps.html); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-users-cant-publicly-share-data-third-party-apps-311329); [the5krunner clarification](https://the5krunner.com/2024/11/20/strava-issues-clarification/)
- 2026 changes: the API Agreement version is effective **1 June 2026**. Standard-tier developers now need a regular **Strava subscription (reported $11.99/month)**: new developers from 1 June 2026, existing ones from 30 June 2026, with 3 months free for active ones. Extended Access (>10,000 users, approval needed) is exempt. Apps routing data via intermediary platforms are no longer supported, and AI users must seek written guidance. A Strava forum reply to a commercial-use question only pointed to the policy and developers@strava.com [hard: Strava pages; 3rd-party for price] — [Strava API agreement](https://www.strava.com/api-agreement); [Strava community: developer program update](https://communityhub.strava.com/insider-journal-9/an-update-to-our-developer-program-13428); [Gigazine Jun 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260602-strava-ai-scraping); [Terra blog](https://tryterra.co/blog/strava-api-changes-2026); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-strava-api-scraping-crackdown/); [Strava community license issue](https://communityhub.strava.com/developers-api-7/license-issue-1798)

**IP risks**
- Lyrics are separately copyrighted literary works. Commercial use of even short excerpts can draw claims, often from publishers. Etsy acts on DMCA notices without judging merits, and repeat notices can suspend a shop. A counter-notice gives the claimant ~10 days to sue [3rd-party law-firm/vendor guides] — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/etsy-copyright-infringement-in-the-uk-what-sellers-should-know/); [Etsy IP policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/ip-dmca/); [Printify on Etsy copyright](https://printify.com/blog/etsy-copyright-infringement/)
- Team names and logos are trademarks. The NFL has sent blanket notices claiming the names and logos of all 32 teams (an EFF case on Shopify). Etsy "reserves the right to disable any listing, shop or account" and removes reported listings, usually without warning [3rd-party/hard] — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/node/102379); [Etsy IP policy](https://etsy.com/legal/ip/); [Printful on copyrighted designs](https://www.printful.com/blog/can-i-sell-copyrighted-designs-on-etsy-t-shirts)
- GitHub's terms on selling prints of Skyline or contribution-graph art: not found [gap].

### Inferences
- [synthesis] **Safe defaults** for this seller:
  - Maps: render OSM data yourself (OSMnx, Overpass extract, or Protomaps PMTiles with a CC0 style) to SVG, and print "© OpenStreetMap contributors" on the poster face. Avoid Mapbox/MapTiler/Stadia raster exports for paid prints unless you have a written licence.
  - Stars: do **not** rely on Hipparcos without ESA authorisation. Prefer the Yale BSC (public domain per NASA-era docs, still unresolved) or email ESA Legal. Any planetarium-style calculation (precession, sidereal time) is your own code and carries no licence.
  - Routes: take a **GPX/FIT file upload or typed splits from the customer**. Do not build on the Strava API: it would need an $11.99/month subscription, has display-to-user-only rules, and commercial use is unclear.
  - Race results: use only the customer's own result. Scraping results sites for prospecting is a separate risk (not researched).
- [synthesis] **Avoid** in product titles and on prints, unless licensed: lyrics and album art; team crests and league names (NFL, Premier League, F1); race brand names and logos in titles (e.g. "London Marathon", "HYROX"). This last one is an inference: race names are likely registered marks, which was not verified. Use descriptive wording such as "26.2 finisher poster – any race" with the customer entering the race name as personalisation. Etsy takedown risk is the practical enforcement mechanism.
- [synthesis] Chess moves (PGN), birth stats, the customer's own GPX and own git history are customer-supplied facts or data with low IP risk. The design and code are the seller's own copyright.

- Race-name trademark status (Abbott WMM, London Marathon Events, HYROX) not verified.

## 7. Risks and realistic outcomes

### Takeaway
The main risks are commoditisation (free generators, open-source repos and AI tools for star and city maps), Etsy price erosion (custom map sets at $6.50), IP takedowns (lyrics, teams, race brands), and the support load of personalisation errors. Legally, EU consumers have **no 14-day withdrawal right for clearly personalised goods** (CRD Art. 16(c)), even before production starts (CJEU Möbel Kraft, C-529/19, 21 Oct 2020). UK/US sellers commonly state "personalised = non-refundable unless faulty". Documented small-seller outcomes in maps range from ~$3.5k/month at a 25% margin (Mapsery, since closed) to $15k/month (Mapply). Both are self-reported survivors.

### Cited Findings
- CRD 2011/83/EU Art. 16(c): the withdrawal right does not apply to "goods made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised" [hard] — [Directive 2011/83/EU Art. 16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/83/article/16)
- CJEU C-529/19 Möbel Kraft (21 Oct 2020): the exception applies regardless of whether the trader has started production [hard: judgment; 3rd-party summaries] — [BAILII judgment](https://mansfield.bailii.org/eu/cases/EUECJ/2020/C52919.html); [Garrigues](https://garrigues.com/en_GB/garrigues-digital/online-sales-cjeu-clarifies-right-withdrawal-personalized-goods); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/consumer-law/1077804/consumers-right-to-renounce-if-its-customized-sold-means-sold-even-if-not-yet-in-production)
- Market practice: PrintMetrics states personalised made-to-order goods are "non-refundable, unless faulty". Some Etsy custom-map shops still accept returns via messages [anecdote] — [NOTHS PrintMetrics](https://www.notonthehighstreet.com/printmetrics); [Etsy custom map shop](https://www.etsy.com/ca/shop/MapCraftShop)
- Quality complaints at the market leader: bent prints and damaged packaging, and foil that looks "light and plastic" [3rd-party] — [Trustpilot The Night Sky foil](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/thenightsky.com/product/star-map-foil-print-6a555112b198c3794d59be6d)
- Commoditisation: free no-watermark star map makers, open-source city map generators, and MapFrame selling 4K files for $1 [3rd-party] — see Q1 sources ([madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=138706); [maptoposter](https://github.com/originalankur/maptoposter); [MapFrame](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/mapframe))
- Outcomes: Mapsery ~$3.5k/month at 25% margin, 2017–2023, closed. Mapply $15k/month [anecdote, founder-reported] — [Starter Story Mapsery](https://starterstory.com/custom-maps-posters); [Starter Story Mapply](https://www.starterstory.com/start-art-prints-ecommerce)
- Etsy enforcement: DMCA-driven listing removal without warning, and repeat strikes can suspend a shop [3rd-party] — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/etsy-copyright-for-uk-sellers-avoid-infringement-and-takedowns/)

### Inferences
- [synthesis] Copycat risk: any successful listing design can be cloned within weeks, and the rendering code is easy to reproduce. The defensible assets are the niche audience, the video library, reviews and the data depth (e.g. full split analytics).
- [synthesis] Error load: free-text personalisation (names, dates, times, splits) is the biggest source of reprints. Structured fields, a live preview and a mandatory proof cut it. Budget 5% for redo/refund, as in the calculations.
- [synthesis] Refund policy for this seller: state the EU Art. 16(c) exception for personalised goods, but reprint free for seller errors and POD defects (POD providers usually cover misprints, as in the earlier notes). Treat US buyers the same way. US law has no general withdrawal right (general knowledge, not sourced here), and Etsy case handling still pushes sellers toward resolving disputes.
- [synthesis] Realistic outcome: a niche personalised line is unlikely to reach €500/month in the first 2–3 months. Reaching ~30 orders/month at the mixed price points of Q5 is a plausible 6–12-month target if videos land. This is an inference without cohort data.

### Gaps
- No data on refund/chargeback rates for personalised prints specifically.
- No Reddit seller threads surfaced (r/EtsySellers, r/printondemand) in search results.
- AI-tool impact on prices was not quantified beyond the existence of free generators.

Also considered and ranked lower [synthesis]:
- **Star maps**: **low**. The category has the strongest demand, but also brands with ~10k reviews, free no-watermark generators, and the Hipparcos non-commercial licence trap.
- **City/"our place" maps in your own style**: **low–moderate**. Demand is proven, but maptoposter-style tools are free, Etsy prices go as low as $6.50 per set, and the only differentiator would be style.
- **Song/soundwave prints**: **low**. Lyric/album-art IP risk. A voice-message soundwave (the customer's own recording) is the safe variant.
